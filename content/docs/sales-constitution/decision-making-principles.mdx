# Owning Mistakes & Accountability

When a situation isn't covered by any specific rule, default to the choice you'd be comfortable explaining afterward. When you get something wrong, own it plainly — that's what personal responsibility actually looks like.

## Lesson Objective

- Apply the honesty-and-candidate-interest test when no specific guidance exists
- Own a mistake directly and plainly, without minimizing or over-apologizing
- Escalate genuine uncertainty rather than resolving it alone

## Coaching Notes

<CoachingNotes title="Coaching Notes">
Correcting information without owning the mistake plainly isn't full accountability — it's just an update. Emily noticed and named the difference directly.
</CoachingNotes>

<CoachingNotes title="Why This Worked">
"That was my mistake" is a complete sentence that does more for trust than any explanation of why it happened. Accountability doesn't need justification attached to it.
</CoachingNotes>
