# Decision-Making Principles

> This is the internal operating constitution for Surely Placed Sales Advisors. This article is what an advisor should reach for when a specific situation isn't explicitly covered by any other article in this academy — the underlying principles that generated all the specific rules, usable directly when a new situation arises.

## Purpose

No documentation set can anticipate every situation an advisor will actually face. This article states the principles an advisor can apply directly, in the moment, when a specific situation doesn't map cleanly onto existing guidance — so that judgment calls remain consistent with this academy's values even where no specific rule exists yet.

## Core Principle

When facing a situation without clear existing guidance, default to the choice that is most honest, most protective of the candidate's genuine interest, and most consistent with treating this decision as one the advisor would be comfortable explaining afterward to a manager, to the candidate themselves, or to their own future self reviewing the call.

## Why It Matters

Advisors will regularly encounter specific situations — an unusual candidate circumstance, a question that falls between two existing articles, a moment that doesn't fit any documented pattern — that this academy hasn't and can't fully anticipate. Without a principle to fall back on, those moments risk being resolved by whatever feels easiest in the moment rather than by what's actually consistent with everything else in this academy.

## Practical Application

When a situation doesn't clearly map to existing guidance, first check whether it's genuinely novel or actually a variation of something covered elsewhere — many apparently new situations turn out to be closely related to an existing pattern. If it's genuinely uncovered, apply the core principle directly: what's the most honest choice, what best protects the candidate's genuine interest, and would this decision hold up to being explained afterward. If real uncertainty remains, escalate rather than resolve it alone, consistent with [Internal Escalation Guidelines](/docs/sales-operations/internal-escalation-guidelines).

## Advisor Behaviours

- Check existing modules for a closely related pattern before treating a situation as entirely novel
- Apply the honesty-and-candidate-interest test directly when no specific guidance exists
- Escalate genuine uncertainty rather than resolving it alone and hoping it was the right call
- Document the reasoning behind a judgment call made in an uncovered situation, so it can inform future guidance

## Common Mistakes

- Treating the absence of a specific rule as permission to do whatever is most convenient in the moment
- Failing to recognize that a "new" situation is actually a variation of an already-documented pattern
- Resolving genuine uncertainty alone rather than escalating it, out of a desire to appear capable of independent judgment

## Ethical Considerations

The "would this hold up to being explained afterward" test is deliberately a high bar — it should be applied honestly, not rationalized after the fact to justify a decision already made for other reasons. This principle exists precisely for situations without existing rules, which makes honest self-application even more important than in situations with clear guidance to check against.

When a specific situation isn't covered by existing guidance, default to the most honest choice that best protects the candidate's genuine interest and would hold up to being explained afterward — and escalate rather than resolve genuine uncertainty alone. This principle exists so judgment calls in new situations remain consistent with everything else in this academy.
