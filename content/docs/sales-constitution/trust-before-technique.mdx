# Trust Before Technique

> This is the internal operating constitution for Surely Placed Sales Advisors. This principle underlies [Trust and Credibility](/docs/objections/trust-and-credibility) and [Building Rapport](/docs/discovery/building-rapport) — trust isn't one technique among the many taught in this academy, it's the precondition for all of them working at all.

## Purpose

Every specific skill in this academy — presenting value, handling an objection, discussing investment, closing — assumes a baseline of trust already exists between advisor and candidate. This article states that assumption explicitly: technique without trust doesn't just work less well, it tends to backfire, reading as manipulation rather than genuine help.

## Core Principle

Trust has to be built and protected before any specific technique is applied, and it has to be protected continuously, not just established once at the start of a relationship. A perfectly executed technique delivered without trust in place will often be read with suspicion rather than received as intended.

## Why It Matters

The same words — a confident price presentation, a well-diagnosed objection response, a clear closing question — land completely differently depending on whether trust is present. With trust, they read as helpful clarity. Without it, they can read as pressure or manipulation, regardless of how well-intentioned or well-executed they actually were. This is why this academy consistently emphasizes honesty over persuasive polish: honesty is what builds and protects the trust every technique depends on.

## Practical Application

Before applying any specific technique from this academy, check whether the trust it depends on is actually present in this specific conversation, with this specific candidate — trust built in an earlier call doesn't automatically transfer if something has since eroded it. When a technique doesn't seem to be landing as expected, consider whether the issue is the technique itself or a trust gap underneath it that needs to be addressed first.

## Advisor Behaviours

- Treat every interaction, not just the first one, as either building or eroding trust
- Notice when a technique isn't landing and check for a trust gap before assuming the technique itself needs adjustment
- Prioritize honesty over polish whenever the two seem to be in tension, consistent with the honesty standards throughout this academy

## Common Mistakes

- Applying a technique from this academy mechanically without checking whether trust is actually present to support it
- Assuming trust built in one conversation carries forward automatically without needing to be maintained
- Mistaking a candidate's politeness or engagement for trust, when it may reflect social convention rather than genuine confidence in the advisor

## Ethical Considerations

Trust that's built through genuine honesty, and used to help a candidate see their situation clearly, is fundamentally different from trust that's built and then leveraged to make persuasion more effective regardless of the candidate's actual best interest. This academy's techniques are meant to work because trust makes honest communication clearer — never as a way to make a candidate more persuadable regardless of what's actually best for them.
