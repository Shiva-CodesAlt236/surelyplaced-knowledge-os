# Sales Constitution Checklist

> This is the internal operating constitution for Surely Placed Sales Advisors. Unlike the stage-specific checklists elsewhere in this academy, this one isn't meant to be run before or after a single call — it's a periodic gut-check on whether the foundational principles behind every other module are genuinely held, not just remembered.

## Purpose

The nine articles before this one state the beliefs, mindset, and boundaries that every other module in this academy assumes. This checklist doesn't summarize their content so much as ask whether each one is genuinely operating, in practice, across an advisor's actual conversations — the kind of check that's more honest done occasionally and seriously than constantly and superficially.

## Core Principle

A principle that's understood intellectually but doesn't show up in how an advisor actually handles a difficult moment isn't yet operating as a principle — it's still an idea. This checklist exists to surface the gap between the two honestly.

## Why It Matters

The nine constitutional articles in this module are meant to be timeless and foundational — but a foundation that's only ever read once during onboarding and never revisited tends to erode quietly under the pressure of real conversations, deadlines, and difficult candidates. Returning to this checklist periodically is how that erosion gets caught before it becomes a pattern.

## Practical Application

Belief and mindset
- [ ] When a recent conversation felt like "selling" and "serving" were in tension, did I actually resolve it the way [What We Believe](/docs/sales-constitution/what-we-believe) describes?
- [ ] Has my mindset shifted toward "getting to yes" under any recent pressure, contrary to [Advisor Mindset](/docs/sales-constitution/advisor-mindset)?

Candidate-first and trust
- [ ] Have I recently adapted a framework to a candidate's actual situation, or applied it mechanically regardless of fit, per [Candidate-First Selling](/docs/sales-constitution/candidate-first-selling)?
- [ ] Is there a conversation where a technique didn't land, and did I check for a trust gap underneath it, per [Trust Before Technique](/docs/sales-constitution/trust-before-technique)?

Ethics and boundaries
- [ ] Have I stated anything recently — a figure, a guarantee, a policy detail — that I wasn't fully certain was accurate, contrary to [Ethical Selling](/docs/sales-constitution/ethical-selling)?
- [ ] Can I recall the six categories in [Things We Never Say](/docs/sales-constitution/things-we-never-say) without needing to look them up?

Judgment and differentiation
- [ ] Have I faced a situation without clear guidance recently, and did I apply [Decision-Making Principles](/docs/sales-constitution/decision-making-principles) honestly rather than defaulting to convenience?
- [ ] When asked what makes Surely Placed different, have I stayed within what [Surely Placed Differentiation](/docs/sales-constitution/surely-placed-differentiation) actually supports?

## Advisor Behaviours

- Run this checklist as genuine self-examination, on a slower cadence than the stage-specific checklists elsewhere in this academy
- Treat an honest "no" on any item as valuable information, not a failure to hide
- Revisit the specific constitutional article behind any item that surfaces a gap

## Common Mistakes

- Treating this checklist as a formality completed once during onboarding rather than revisited periodically
- Answering quickly and generously rather than honestly recalling specific recent conversations
- Reading the nine constitutional articles as background philosophy rather than as principles that should visibly shape actual behavior

## Ethical Considerations

Honest self-assessment against this checklist is itself an application of [Ethical Selling](/docs/sales-constitution/ethical-selling) — there's little value in a constitution that's affirmed in the abstract but not checked honestly against real behavior. A recurring gap on the same item, noticed honestly, is more valuable than a checklist completed quickly and generously.
