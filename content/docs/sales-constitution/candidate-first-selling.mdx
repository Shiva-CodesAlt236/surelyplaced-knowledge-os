# Candidate-First Selling

> This is the internal operating constitution for Surely Placed Sales Advisors. This principle underlies [Understanding Candidate Goals](/docs/discovery/understanding-candidate-goals) and every stage of this academy's process — the process exists to serve the candidate's actual situation, not the other way around.

## Purpose

It's possible to run every step of the process correctly — discovery, discussion, objection-handling, closing — and still fail this principle, if the process is applied mechanically rather than in genuine service of the specific person in front of the advisor. This article states the principle that keeps the process itself honest: it exists to help a real, specific candidate, not to be followed for its own sake.

## Core Principle

Every stage of the sales process should be shaped by the candidate's actual situation, goals, and constraints — not applied as a fixed script regardless of who's on the other end of the call. When the process and the candidate's actual situation seem to conflict, the candidate's actual situation wins.

## Why It Matters

A process treated as more important than the person it's meant to serve produces conversations that feel, to the candidate, like they're being moved through a funnel rather than genuinely understood. Candidates notice this, even when they can't articulate exactly what feels off — and it undermines the trust that [Trust Before Technique](/docs/sales-constitution/trust-before-technique) identifies as the actual foundation of every successful conversation.

## Practical Application

Use the frameworks throughout this academy — Discovery's qualification questions, Discussion's structure, Closing's frameworks — as tools in service of understanding and helping a specific candidate, not as a checklist to complete regardless of fit. When a candidate's situation doesn't match the expected pattern for a given stage, adapt the conversation to their actual situation rather than forcing it back onto the expected script.

## Advisor Behaviours

- Treat every framework in this academy as a tool for understanding a specific candidate, not a script to complete
- Notice when a conversation feels like it's being run "at" a candidate rather than "with" them, and adjust
- Prioritize a candidate's actual stated goals and constraints over an advisor's assumptions about what a "typical" candidate in their position wants

## Common Mistakes

- Applying a stage's framework mechanically even when it clearly doesn't fit a specific candidate's actual situation
- Treating deviation from the expected script as a problem to correct rather than a sign the process needs to adapt
- Assuming a candidate's goals based on their industry, visa category, or general profile rather than what they've actually said

## Ethical Considerations

Candidate-first selling means the process serves the person, not the reverse — an advisor should never use a candidate's unfamiliarity with the process as an opportunity to move them through it faster than genuinely serves their interests. If following a specific framework would work against a candidate's actual best interest in a given moment, the framework should give way.

A process that's followed mechanically, regardless of fit, has stopped serving the purpose it was built for.
