# Things We Never Say

> This is the internal operating constitution for Surely Placed Sales Advisors. This article consolidates categories already established individually throughout Pricing, Visa Playbooks, Industry Playbooks, Objections, and Sales Operations — it does not introduce new rules, and it does not state what an advisor should say instead of these categories, since the accurate version of that answer is Operational Policy Pending or belongs to qualified outside expertise.

## Purpose

Across this academy, individual articles establish specific non-invention rules in their own context — no invented ROI figure in [ROI Conversations](/docs/pricing/roi-conversations), no invented discount in [Discount Guidelines](/docs/pricing/discount-guidelines), no invented visa timeline in the Visa Playbooks. This article gathers those into a single consolidated list, so the pattern is visible as one constitutional boundary rather than several separate rules that happen to look similar.

## Core Principle

An advisor never states a specific number, guarantee, or authoritative claim in a category where this academy hasn't documented one — regardless of how confident the advisor feels, how reasonable the number would sound, or how much smoother the conversation would go with a direct answer. Every category below is answered honestly with "I don't know that specifically, let me confirm" or a redirect to the appropriate authority, never with an invented or approximated answer.

## Why It Matters

Each of these categories represents a place where an invented answer, even a well-intentioned or plausible-sounding one, could cause real harm: a candidate making a financial decision based on a number that isn't accurate, a candidate believing they have a legal protection that isn't confirmed, a candidate trusting a placement outcome that was never actually promised. The consolidated list makes clear these aren't isolated exceptions — they're one consistent boundary applied everywhere it's relevant.

## Practical Application

Never state a specific price, discount amount, or financing term — confirm through the latest approved Surely Placed pricing policy, consistent with [Discount Guidelines](/docs/pricing/discount-guidelines).

Never state or imply a guarantee of any outcome — a job offer, a specific salary, a placement timeline, or an immigration approval — consistent with [No Guarantee Concern](/docs/objections/no-guarantee-concern) and [ROI Conversations](/docs/pricing/roi-conversations).

Never state a specific statistic — a placement rate, a success percentage, a salary figure, or a market statistic — unless it comes from a confirmed, current, and approved source, consistent with the non-invention standard in the Industry Playbooks and Visa Playbooks modules.

Never give specific legal or immigration advice, including interpreting a visa condition, predicting an approval outcome, or advising on a specific legal course of action — always redirect to qualified immigration counsel, consistent with [Visa Discovery Framework](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-discovery-framework).

Never state an internal company policy, CRM workflow detail, or approval hierarchy that hasn't been confirmed through the current internal process — consistent with the Operational Policy Pending standard throughout the Sales Operations module.

Never disparage a competitor or make an unverifiable comparative claim about another company — consistent with [Already Working With a Consultancy](/docs/objections/already-working-with-a-consultancy).

## Advisor Behaviours

- Recognize these six categories immediately when a conversation approaches them, rather than realizing only afterward that an invented answer was given
- Respond to a question in any of these categories with an honest "let me confirm" or a redirect to the appropriate authority, never a guess
- Treat confidence in an answer as irrelevant to whether it's appropriate to state — confidence doesn't substitute for a confirmed, current source

## Common Mistakes

- Answering a question in one of these categories with an approximation that feels close enough to be harmless
- Assuming a past answer, even one given confidently before, is still accurate without reconfirming it
- Believing that avoiding these categories will make a conversation less persuasive, rather than recognizing that honesty here is what makes the rest of the conversation credible

## Ethical Considerations

Each of these six categories exists because an invented answer in that specific area carries real potential for harm to the candidate — financial, legal, or otherwise. This list is not about caution for its own sake; it's a direct application of the ethical standard in [Ethical Selling](/docs/sales-constitution/ethical-selling), applied to the specific places where invention is most tempting and most consequential.

## Key Takeaways

Six categories of statement are never invented, approximated, or guessed at anywhere in this academy: specific pricing or discount terms, outcome guarantees, unconfirmed statistics, legal or immigration advice, unconfirmed internal policy, and disparaging or unverifiable competitor claims. Each is answered honestly with a confirmation or a redirect, never with a plausible-sounding invention.
