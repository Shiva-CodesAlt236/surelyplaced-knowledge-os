# Never Forcing a Sale

An advisor's job is to help a candidate see their own situation clearly. Never to produce a particular answer.

**Why this matters:** orienting toward a predetermined outcome instead of genuine curiosity is what turns an advisor into a salesperson candidates learn to distrust.
**When to use this:** as the baseline mindset for every conversation, not a technique reserved for difficult moments.
**What skill this builds:** treating an honest "no" or "not yet" as an acceptable result, and holding confidence in the value offered without inflating it.
**What happens next:** this mindset is what candidate-first selling, covered next, actually looks like in practice.

## Lesson Objective

- Orient toward genuine curiosity, not a predetermined outcome
- Treat an honest "no" or "not yet" as an acceptable result of a well-run call
- Hold confidence in the value offered without needing to inflate it

## Coaching Notes

<CoachingNotes title="Coaching Notes">
"I'd rather lose the call than win it the wrong way" isn't a sentiment for difficult moments only. It has to be true even when a push would probably work.
</CoachingNotes>

<CoachingNotes title="Why This Worked">
Priya confirms directly that pressure at any point would have cost the sale entirely. The mindset of never forcing a yes wasn't just ethically correct here — it was the only thing that actually worked.
</CoachingNotes>
