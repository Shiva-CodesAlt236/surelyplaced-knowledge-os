# Ethical Selling

> This is the internal operating constitution for Surely Placed Sales Advisors. This article consolidates the ethical standard already applied throughout every module's "Ethical Guidelines" section into a single statement of principle.

## Purpose

Discovery, Discussion, Closing, Objections, Pricing, Sales Operations, and Sales Coaching each include ethical guidance specific to their stage. This article states the common standard underneath all of them, so it can be understood as one coherent principle rather than a collection of separate, stage-specific rules.

## Core Principle

An advisor never invents information to make a conversation easier, never uses a candidate's trust to override their genuine best interest, and never treats a candidate's vulnerability — financial, emotional, or situational — as an opportunity rather than a responsibility. When honesty and persuasive momentum conflict, honesty wins, every time, without exception.

## Why It Matters

Every specific "never invent pricing," "never guess at policy," "never imply a guarantee" instruction throughout this academy exists because inventing information, however well-intentioned, ultimately harms the candidate it was meant to help — they end up making a decision based on something that isn't true. Ethical selling isn't a constraint on effective selling in this academy's philosophy; it's what makes selling here trustworthy at all.

## Practical Application

When facing a moment where an honest answer feels less persuasive than an invented or approximated one, treat that as the exact moment this principle is meant for — not an exception to make. Escalate genuine uncertainty rather than resolving it with a guess, consistent with [Internal Escalation Guidelines](/docs/sales-operations/internal-escalation-guidelines), and slow down rather than push forward when a candidate's situation raises a genuine red flag, consistent with [Pricing Red Flags](/docs/pricing/pricing-red-flags).

## Advisor Behaviours

- Choose an honest "I don't know, let me confirm" over any invented or approximated answer, in every conversation
- Recognize genuine vulnerability — financial strain, emotional pressure, situational urgency — as a signal to slow down and care, not an opening to close faster
- Apply the same ethical standard consistently regardless of how close a candidate is to a decision or how much a specific outcome might matter in the moment

## Common Mistakes

- Treating ethical guidance as specific to certain high-risk topics (pricing, visas) rather than as a single, constant standard across every conversation
- Rationalizing a small invention or approximation as harmless because it seemed unlikely to matter
- Letting proximity to a decision — a candidate who seems ready to say yes — lower the bar for honesty in that specific moment

## Ethical Considerations

This entire article is an ethical consideration — it is the statement of the standard, not an application of a separate one. Every other module's ethical guidance should be read as a specific instance of this same underlying commitment: honesty and candidate wellbeing over persuasive convenience, without exception.
