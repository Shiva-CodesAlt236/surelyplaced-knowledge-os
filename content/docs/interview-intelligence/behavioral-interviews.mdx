# Behavioral Interviews

## Purpose

A behavioral interview asks a candidate to describe past experience — how they handled a specific situation, made a decision, or worked with others — rather than testing knowledge directly. This article covers how an advisor can help a candidate structure that kind of answer clearly, without writing or inventing content on the candidate's behalf.

## Scope

This article covers general communication structure for behavioral-style answers. It does not supply example answers describing a real candidate, does not promise that a particular structure will produce a favorable outcome, and does not claim to know what a specific employer is looking for beyond general communication clarity.

## Core Content

Candidates often struggle less with having relevant experience and more with organizing it clearly under interview pressure. An advisor can help by encouraging a candidate to think in terms of situation, action, and result — describing what was happening, what they specifically did, and what came of it — without dictating the content of any particular story, which belongs entirely to the candidate's own real experience.

This is coaching on communication structure, not content generation. An advisor should never draft, embellish, or suggest specific claims for a candidate to present as their own experience — the value an advisor adds here is helping a true story land clearly, not manufacturing one.
