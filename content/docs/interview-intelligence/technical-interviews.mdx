# Technical Interviews

## Purpose

A technical interview asks a candidate to demonstrate job-relevant knowledge directly, in whatever form a given employer uses — through discussion, a practical exercise, or a combination. This article covers what an advisor can usefully say about that format in general, without stepping into an evaluative role the advisor isn't positioned to fill.

## Scope

This article covers the shape and experience of a technical round generically — what a candidate might expect in terms of format and pacing. It does not judge a candidate's actual technical readiness, and it does not describe a specific assessment length, question set, or evaluation process, since that varies by employer and role.

## Core Content

Candidates preparing for a technical round often benefit most from orientation rather than content coaching — knowing broadly what kind of interaction to expect (a conversation, a live exercise, a mix) reduces uncertainty in a way that's independent of the technical substance itself. An advisor can offer that orientation without commenting on whether a candidate is technically ready, which is a judgment this repository never asks an advisor to make.

Where a candidate raises a specific technical question about what they'll be asked or how to prepare technically, that question belongs with an internal technical subject matter expert, consistent with the non-invention standard already established in [Technology Mapping](/docs/candidate-intelligence/TECHNOLOGY_MAPPING). An advisor's role here is logistical and emotional support, not technical instruction.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article applies [Interview Preparation Framework](/docs/interview-intelligence/interview-preparation-framework)'s general approach to the technical-round format specifically, and defers to the same SME-routing discipline [Industry Discovery Framework](/docs/industry-playbooks/industry-discovery-framework) already established for technical questions generally.

## Advisor Guidance

Offer orientation about format and pacing, not an opinion on technical readiness. Route any genuinely technical question to an internal SME rather than answering it directly, and never suggest a specific assessment duration or structure that isn't confirmed for that particular employer.

## Key Takeaways

Technical interviews vary widely by employer, and an advisor's useful role is orienting a candidate to the general shape of the format rather than judging their technical readiness or predicting the specific content of the round — any genuinely technical question goes to an internal SME.
