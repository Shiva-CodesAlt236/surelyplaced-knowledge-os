# Case Study Interviews

## Purpose

A case-study interview presents a candidate with a business problem or scenario and asks them to reason through it out loud, common in business-analysis, consulting-adjacent, and some product-oriented roles. This article covers what an advisor can offer around that format without evaluating the candidate's business reasoning.

## Scope

This article covers the general experience and pacing of a case-study round. It does not describe a specific case format as standard, since practice varies by employer and role, and it does not assess a candidate's problem-solving ability.

## Core Content

This format often rewards a candidate who thinks out loud in a structured, organized way rather than one who arrives at a single "correct" answer quickly — interviewers are typically evaluating the reasoning process as much as the conclusion. An advisor can reasonably encourage a candidate to practice structuring an answer clearly (stating assumptions, breaking a problem into parts, checking in with the interviewer along the way) without needing business-domain expertise to do so.

Where a candidate asks about the specific business content or domain knowledge a case might involve, that question belongs with an internal SME familiar with the relevant field, not the advisor's own judgment.

## Advisor Guidance

Encourage structured, out-loud reasoning — stated assumptions, a clear breakdown, checking in with the interviewer — without commenting on whether a candidate's business reasoning is correct. Route domain-specific questions to an internal SME.
