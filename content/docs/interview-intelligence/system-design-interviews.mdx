# System Design Interviews

## Purpose

A system design interview asks a candidate to reason through how a larger technical system might be structured, typically in open discussion rather than a fixed right-or-wrong exercise. This article covers what an advisor can usefully say about that format without engaging with the design content itself.

## Scope

This article covers the general shape and pacing candidates might expect from this format. It does not evaluate a candidate's design ability, does not describe a specific employer's evaluation criteria, and does not claim a fixed duration or structure, since practice varies considerably by employer and seniority level.

## Core Content

This format tends to feel less defined than other technical rounds, since it's usually a conversation rather than a single correct answer to produce — candidates sometimes find that ambiguity itself the most disorienting part, independent of their actual technical background. An advisor can help by naming that ambiguity as a normal feature of the format rather than something the candidate is missing information about.

Any question about what a specific system design round will actually cover, or what level of depth is expected, should go to an internal technical SME. An advisor's contribution here is normalizing the open-ended nature of the format, not commenting on the design reasoning itself.

## Advisor Guidance

Normalize the open-ended, conversational nature of this format rather than trying to explain its technical substance, and route any question about expected depth or evaluation criteria to an internal SME.
