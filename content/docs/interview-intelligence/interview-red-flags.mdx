# Interview Red Flags

## Purpose

Most of what a candidate describes about an interview process is ordinary and doesn't need escalation. Occasionally, though, a candidate describes something that raises a genuine concern — about fairness, legality, or basic professionalism — that an advisor shouldn't try to resolve alone. This article names those signals.

## Scope

This article covers what to notice and route, not how to resolve a concern independently. It does not define a specific escalation process beyond pointing to existing internal escalation guidance, and it does not speculate about whether a specific employer's practice is or isn't appropriate.

## Core Content

Watch for a candidate describing an interview request that sounds unusually invasive, seems disconnected from the actual role, or makes the candidate visibly uncomfortable in a way that goes beyond normal interview nerves. Watch also for anything a candidate describes that could suggest discriminatory questioning, unpaid work disguised as an assessment, or a process that seems designed to extract free labor rather than genuinely evaluate a candidate.

None of these situations should be assessed or resolved by the advisor alone. The right response is to take the candidate's account seriously, avoid speculating about intent, and escalate through the existing internal process rather than offering a personal opinion on whether something crossed a line.
