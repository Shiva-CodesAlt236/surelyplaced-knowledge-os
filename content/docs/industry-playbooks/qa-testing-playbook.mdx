# QA & Testing Playbook

> This is a sales playbook, not technical training — see [Industry Discovery Framework](/docs/industry-playbooks/industry-discovery-framework) for the standard this article follows. Nothing here states specific tools, methodologies, or requirements as fact; questions requiring real technical judgment go to an internal technical SME.

## Typical Candidate Profile

Candidates in this playbook range from manual testers to automation-focused QA engineers, and often have backgrounds that vary more widely than in some other technical fields — some come from a pure QA path, others transitioned from a different technical or even non-technical role. This variation matters more here than in most other playbooks in this module.

## Career Mindset

Many candidates in this field carry a specific frustration worth understanding directly: QA is sometimes perceived, unfairly, as a lesser or entry-level technical path compared to development, and candidates are often working against that perception rather than against a lack of real skill. There's frequently a strong desire to be taken seriously as a technical professional in their own right.

## Buying Behaviour

Candidates in this space often respond well to being taken seriously as technical professionals from the outset — a conversation that subtly implies QA is a stepping stone to "real" engineering roles will likely lose credibility quickly, even if unintentional. Many are also carefully evaluating whether a program understands the distinction between manual and automation-focused work, which varies significantly by candidate.

## Biggest Career Pain Points

- Facing a perception, whether from employers or recruiters, that QA is a secondary or less technical path
- Difficulty communicating the specific technical depth of automation-focused QA work to people who don't distinguish it from manual testing
- Uncertainty about whether to invest in developing further automation skills, and how that affects their market position
- Frustration with generic outreach that doesn't reflect their specific specialization within QA

## Discovery Questions

1. "Would you describe your background as more manual testing, automation-focused, or a mix of both?"
2. "How did you come into QA — was it your intended path, or did you transition from something else?"
3. "Have you experienced your work being treated as less technical than it actually is? What's that been like?"
4. "What are you hoping to build toward — deeper QA specialization, or a transition into a different technical role?"
5. "Is there anything about your technical background you'd want a specialist's opinion on, rather than mine?"

## Qualification Signals

- The candidate can clearly describe their specific mix of manual and automation work
- The candidate speaks about their field with a clear sense of its technical depth, regardless of how others may have treated it
- The candidate's described career goals are coherent with their actual background

## Risk Signals

- The candidate's description of their technical work, particularly around automation, feels vague or inconsistent
- The candidate is targeting a role that assumes a level of automation expertise not reflected in their described background
- The candidate asks a specific technical question the advisor isn't equipped to answer accurately

See [Industry Red Flags](/docs/industry-playbooks/industry-red-flags) for what to do when a technical claim needs real verification.

## Presentation Strategy

Treat QA as a real technical specialization from the first sentence — avoid any framing, even subtle, that implies it's a stepping stone to something else unless the candidate has stated that's their own goal. Be specific about the difference between manual and automation-focused paths rather than treating QA as one undifferentiated category.

## Common Objections

Candidates who've felt underestimated before may raise a sharper version of [Trust and Credibility](/docs/objections/trust-and-credibility). Candidates weighing whether to invest in further automation skills versus continuing to search independently may also raise [Already Applying Myself](/docs/objections/already-applying-myself).

## Closing Strategy

Confirm the recommendation reflects the candidate's actual specialization — manual, automation, or a genuine mix — and their real stated goals, rather than a generic QA framing. Route any unresolved technical claim to an SME before closing.

## Advisor Mistakes

- Implying, even subtly, that QA is a lesser or transitional technical path
- Treating manual and automation-focused QA as interchangeable when they're often quite different in practice
- Using generic language that doesn't reflect the candidate's specific background within the field
- Guessing at how a candidate's specific technical skills compare to market demand

## Key Takeaways

QA and testing candidates often carry real, valid frustration about being underestimated as technical professionals — treat their work as the real technical specialization it is, and be precise about the difference between manual and automation-focused paths. Route technical evaluation to an internal SME rather than guessing.
