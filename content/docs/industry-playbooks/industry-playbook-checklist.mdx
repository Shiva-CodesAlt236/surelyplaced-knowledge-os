# Industry Playbook Checklist

> This checklist confirms a conversation stayed within the sales-first standard this module is built on — it does not confirm anything about a candidate's actual technical skill level, which is never an advisor's determination to make.

## Typical Candidate Profile

This article applies to any candidate whose field was discussed using this module, across any of the nine field-specific playbooks it contains.

## Career Mindset

By the end of a conversation that touched on their specific field, most candidates are gauging whether the advisor genuinely understood their situation or leaned on generic industry language — and this checklist is the advisor's own way of checking which one actually occurred.

## Buying Behaviour

Not applicable in the usual sense — this article is a quality check, not a candidate-facing conversation. It exists to protect the credibility built elsewhere in the call by confirming nothing inaccurate or unverified was stated along the way.

## Biggest Career Pain Points

- Advisors under time pressure may be tempted to sound more technically fluent than they actually are
- It's easy to default to generic language about a field rather than the candidate's specific version of it
- Reviewing a call after the fact for accuracy takes discipline that's easy to skip when a call otherwise went well

## Discovery Questions

The checklist itself, run as self-directed questions after any call that touched a candidate's specific field:

1. Did I ask what the candidate's specific role or specialization actually involves, rather than assuming from a job title?
2. Did I avoid stating any salary figure, placement statistic, or technical fact as though it were confirmed?
3. If a technical or industry-specific claim came up that I couldn't verify, did I say so and route it to an SME?
4. Did the recommendation I gave reflect the candidate's actual described background, not a generic version of their field?
5. Looking back at the call, is there a specific moment where I sounded more technically confident than I actually was?

## Qualification Signals

A conversation passes this checklist when every question above can be answered with a clear yes, and any unverifiable claim was routed to an SME rather than accepted or guessed at.

## Risk Signals

- Any "no" or uncertain answer to the checklist above
- A call where a candidate's field was treated generically rather than specifically
- A call where an unverifiable claim was noticed but not escalated, because the conversation was going well otherwise

## Presentation Strategy

Run this checklist as a habit immediately after any call that touched a candidate's specific field, while the details are still fresh. If something needs correcting with the candidate, follow up proactively rather than waiting for them to notice.

## Common Objections

Not applicable — this is an internal quality check, not a candidate-facing conversation.

## Closing Strategy

If this checklist surfaces a gap — a generic recommendation, an unresolved technical claim, an uncertain statement — treat it as something to correct before any closing conversation proceeds.

## Advisor Mistakes

- Skipping this checklist because a call felt like it went smoothly
- Assuming a recommendation was specific enough without checking it against what the candidate actually described
- Noticing an unverified claim in hindsight and not following up to correct course
- Using this checklist to judge a candidate's technical skill, rather than to judge the advisor's own conduct

## Key Takeaways

This checklist isn't about judging a candidate's technical skill — it's about confirming an advisor stayed sales-first: ask about the candidate's specific field rather than assuming, never state an unverified technical fact or figure, and route anything requiring real expertise to an internal SME. Run it as a habit after every call that touches a candidate's specific field, not just when something felt off.
