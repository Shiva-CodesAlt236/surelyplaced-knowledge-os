# Supply Chain Playbook

> This is a sales playbook, not technical or logistics training — see [Industry Discovery Framework](/docs/industry-playbooks/industry-discovery-framework) for the standard this article follows. Nothing here states specific tools, regulatory requirements, or market claims as fact; questions requiring real domain expertise go to an internal SME.

## Typical Candidate Profile

Candidates in this playbook come from roles across procurement, logistics, operations, planning, and increasingly, supply chain analytics and systems. The field has become more technology-driven over time, so candidates can range from operationally-focused professionals to those working closely with data and planning systems.

## Career Mindset

Many candidates in this field think in terms of process, efficiency, and measurable outcomes, and they often bring that same practical, results-oriented lens to evaluating a career decision. There's frequently a strong emphasis on reliability and follow-through — traits central to the work itself — which shows up in how carefully these candidates evaluate commitments made to them.

## Buying Behaviour

Candidates in this space tend to respond well to clear, concrete information and are often less swayed by broad enthusiasm than by a specific, credible plan. Given the field's increasing overlap with data and systems work, some candidates are also specifically evaluating how well a program understands that shift, rather than treating supply chain as a purely operational field.

## Biggest Career Pain Points

- Difficulty positioning increasingly technical or analytics-driven supply chain experience to employers who still see the field in purely operational terms
- Uncertainty about how much further to lean into the data and systems side of the field versus staying more operationally focused
- A sense that supply chain work is sometimes undervalued or less visible than more customer-facing or product-facing roles
- Frustration with generic career support that doesn't reflect the field's practical, outcomes-driven culture

## Discovery Questions

1. "What part of supply chain would you say you specialize in — procurement, logistics, planning, analytics, or a mix?"
2. "How has your role evolved over time, especially as it relates to data or systems work?"
3. "What does a strong outcome look like in your current or most recent role?"
4. "Is there a specific direction you want to lean further into — more operational, or more analytical and systems-focused?"
5. "Is there anything about your background you'd want a specialist's opinion on, rather than mine?"

## Qualification Signals

- The candidate can describe measurable outcomes and specific responsibilities from their work
- The candidate has a clear sense of where they currently sit on the operational-to-analytical spectrum within the field
- The candidate evaluates the conversation itself with the same practical, concrete lens they apply to their work

## Risk Signals

- The candidate's description of their responsibilities or outcomes is vague or difficult to pin down
- The candidate is targeting a more analytics- or systems-focused role without a background that supports it, without explanation
- The candidate asks a specific technical or regulatory question the advisor isn't equipped to answer accurately

See [Industry Red Flags](/docs/industry-playbooks/industry-red-flags) for what to do when a claim needs real verification.

## Presentation Strategy

Be concrete and outcomes-focused rather than broadly enthusiastic — this population tends to respond to specific, credible plans more than general encouragement. Be honest about where the recommendation reflects the candidate's current operational strength versus an analytical direction they're still building toward.

## Common Objections

Given this population's practical, ROI-minded evaluation style, [Price Objection](/docs/objections/price-objection) often comes up framed in terms of concrete return rather than general hesitation. [Discussing Investment](/docs/discussion/discussing-investment) is a useful reference for handling that framing directly.

## Closing Strategy

Confirm the recommendation reflects where the candidate genuinely sits on the operational-to-analytical spectrum, not an assumed direction. Route any unresolved technical or regulatory claim to an SME before closing.

## Advisor Mistakes

- Treating supply chain as a purely operational field when a candidate's actual work is heavily systems- or analytics-driven, or the reverse
- Using broad enthusiasm instead of concrete, specific information with a population that values practicality
- Guessing at how a candidate's specific background compares to current market demand
- Missing the field's increasing overlap with data and systems work

## Key Takeaways

Supply chain candidates are practical, outcomes-focused evaluators who increasingly span a spectrum from operational to analytics-driven work — find out where a candidate actually sits on that spectrum rather than assuming. Be concrete rather than broadly enthusiastic, and route technical or regulatory questions to an internal SME.
