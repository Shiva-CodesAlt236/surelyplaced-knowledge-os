# Cloud & DevOps Playbook

> This is a sales playbook, not technical training — see [Industry Discovery Framework](/docs/industry-playbooks/industry-discovery-framework) for the standard this article follows. Nothing here states specific platforms, certifications, or requirements as fact; questions requiring real technical judgment go to an internal technical SME.

## Typical Candidate Profile

Candidates in this playbook typically work on infrastructure, deployment, and operational reliability rather than application-level development, though many have a software engineering background and moved into this specialization over time. The field covers a range from infrastructure-focused engineers to candidates whose work blends closely with security or platform engineering.

## Career Mindset

Many candidates in this field think in terms of systems and reliability, which often carries into a preference for concrete, verifiable information over general reassurance. There's frequently a strong sense of ownership in how these candidates describe their work — a habit of talking about what they're responsible for keeping running, not just what they build.

## Buying Behaviour

Candidates in this space tend to value efficiency and directness — long, generic pitches often lose their attention faster than in some other fields. They respond well to a conversation that respects their time and gets specific quickly about how a recommendation actually applies to their situation.

## Biggest Career Pain Points

- Being lumped in with a broader "software engineer" category that doesn't reflect their actual specialization
- Uncertainty about how their specific mix of skills compares to what the market is currently prioritizing
- Frustration with recruiters who use infrastructure and platform terminology loosely or inaccurately
- Feeling like their operational, reliability-focused work is less visible or valued than feature-building work

## Discovery Questions

1. "How would you describe your specific focus — more infrastructure, more platform, more on the operational side, or a mix?"
2. "What does ownership look like in your current role — what are you actually responsible for keeping running?"
3. "How did you move into this specialization, if it wasn't where you started your career?"
4. "What's frustrated you most about how recruiters or programs have approached you so far?"
5. "Is there anything technical about your background you'd want a specialist's opinion on, rather than mine?"

## Qualification Signals

- The candidate can describe their specific ownership and responsibilities with real clarity
- The candidate uses infrastructure and platform terminology in a way that's consistent and precise
- The candidate is direct and efficient in how they communicate, consistent with the field's general working style

## Risk Signals

- The candidate uses technical terminology loosely or inconsistently in a way that doesn't track with genuine hands-on experience
- The candidate is targeting a role that seems disconnected from their described ownership and responsibilities
- The candidate asks a specific technical question the advisor isn't equipped to answer accurately

See [Industry Red Flags](/docs/industry-playbooks/industry-red-flags) for what to do when a technical claim needs real verification.

## Presentation Strategy

Be direct and efficient — this population tends to respond well to a recommendation that gets to the point quickly and specifically, rather than one wrapped in a longer, more general pitch. Ground the conversation in the candidate's actual described ownership and responsibilities.

## Common Objections

Given this population's efficiency-minded style, a direct version of [Already Applying Myself](/docs/objections/already-applying-myself) is common — candidates who are used to solving their own problems may be skeptical of outside help by default. [Price Objection](/docs/objections/price-objection) also comes up, often evaluated with the same precision this population brings to technical decisions.

## Closing Strategy

Confirm the recommendation is grounded in the candidate's specific described responsibilities, not a generic infrastructure pitch. If a technical claim came up that wasn't verified, get an SME's answer before calling the conversation settled.

## Advisor Mistakes

- Using infrastructure or platform terminology loosely, which this population tends to notice quickly
- Treating this field as interchangeable with general software engineering
- Delivering a longer, more general pitch to a population that tends to prefer directness and specificity
- Guessing at how a candidate's specific technical background compares to market demand

## Key Takeaways

Cloud and DevOps candidates tend to be direct, precise, and skeptical of loosely used technical language — match that with efficiency and specificity rather than a generic pitch. Ground the conversation in what the candidate is actually responsible for, and route real technical evaluation to an internal SME rather than guessing.
