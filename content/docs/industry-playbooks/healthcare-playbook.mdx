# Healthcare Playbook

> This is a sales playbook, not clinical, regulatory, or technical training — see [Industry Discovery Framework](/docs/industry-playbooks/industry-discovery-framework) for the standard this article follows. Nothing here states licensure requirements, regulatory rules, clinical facts, or specific technical requirements as fact; questions requiring real domain expertise go to an internal SME.

## Typical Candidate Profile

Candidates from healthcare backgrounds vary widely, spanning clinical roles, health administration, health information systems, and technology roles specifically within healthcare organizations. This playbook doesn't assume a single kind of healthcare background — discovery should establish exactly what a candidate's specific role and specialization actually involve.

## Career Mindset

Many candidates from healthcare backgrounds carry a strong sense of mission connected to their work, which can shape how they evaluate a career move — not purely on compensation or title, but on whether a role feels meaningful and aligned with why they entered the field. There's often also real awareness of how regulated and structured this industry is, which can make candidates more cautious evaluators than in less regulated fields.

## Buying Behaviour

Candidates from this background often want clear, structured information and tend to be uncomfortable with vague or overly casual claims, consistent with the general culture of their field. Given the regulatory weight many healthcare roles carry, candidates may also ask specific questions about compliance or licensure that require a careful, honest response rather than a confident guess.

## Biggest Career Pain Points

- Difficulty translating specialized healthcare experience into terms that make sense to employers or hiring processes outside their specific niche
- Uncertainty about how licensure, certification, or regulatory requirements affect their options in a new role or region
- A sense of mission or purpose that can be hard to find in less mission-driven target roles
- Concern about being taken seriously if moving between clinical, administrative, or technical tracks within the field

## Discovery Questions

1. "What's your specific background within healthcare — clinical, administrative, technical, or some mix?"
2. "What draws you to the type of role you're targeting now, beyond just the practical side?"
3. "Are there licensure, certification, or regulatory considerations relevant to your target roles that we should be aware of?"
4. "How has your healthcare background shaped what you're looking for in a next role?"
5. "Is there anything regulatory or technical about your situation you'd want a specialist's opinion on, rather than mine?"

## Qualification Signals

- The candidate can clearly describe their specific track within healthcare and how it connects to their target roles
- The candidate raises licensure or regulatory questions directly rather than assuming they don't apply
- The candidate's stated motivation reflects a coherent connection between their healthcare background and their new goals

## Risk Signals

- The candidate's description of their healthcare background and target role suggests a licensure or regulatory question the advisor isn't equipped to answer
- The candidate seems to be assuming a regulatory detail is settled without having confirmed it
- The candidate's account of their credentials or experience is vague or inconsistent under follow-up questions

See [Industry Red Flags](/docs/industry-playbooks/industry-red-flags) for what to do when a claim needs real verification, and route any licensure or regulatory question to the appropriate internal or external expert rather than answering it directly.

## Presentation Strategy

Be structured, specific, and comfortable saying "I'm not the right person to answer that" on any licensure, regulatory, or clinical question — this population tends to respect that far more than a confident guess. Ground the recommendation in the candidate's actual described track and motivation, including the mission-driven element when it's genuinely present.

## Common Objections

Given the regulatory weight and mission-driven nature of much healthcare work, [Trust and Credibility](/docs/objections/trust-and-credibility) is a common and legitimate concern. Candidates transitioning between clinical, administrative, or technical tracks may also raise questions similar to [Already Applying Myself](/docs/objections/already-applying-myself) if they've been navigating that transition independently already.

## Closing Strategy

Confirm any licensure or regulatory question raised during the call has been properly routed and resolved, rather than assumed settled, before closing. Ground the close in the candidate's actual described track and motivation.

## Advisor Mistakes

- Guessing at a licensure, regulatory, or clinical detail instead of routing it to the appropriate expert
- Treating "healthcare" as a single category rather than asking about the candidate's specific track
- Missing the mission-driven element of a candidate's motivation by focusing only on logistics
- Using overly casual language with a population that often expects structure and precision

## Key Takeaways

Healthcare candidates span clinical, administrative, and technical tracks, and treating the field as one category misses real differences in what each candidate needs. Never state a licensure, regulatory, or clinical fact directly — route those questions to the appropriate expert, and ground the conversation in the candidate's actual track and often mission-driven motivation.
