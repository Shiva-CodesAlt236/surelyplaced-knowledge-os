# No Guarantee Concern

## Purpose

At some point, most candidates ask a version of "so you're not actually guaranteeing me a job?" This is one of the most important moments in any conversation with Surely Placed, and it should be met with a direct, honest yes — that's correct, there is no guarantee — followed by a real explanation of why that's true and what the absence of a guarantee actually says about the program's credibility, rather than a weakness to explain away.

## Learning Objectives

- Confirm plainly, without hedging, that Surely Placed does not guarantee placement
- Explain honestly why a guarantee isn't offered, grounded in what a guarantee would actually require to be genuine
- Reframe the conversation toward what is actually provided, without overstating it to compensate for the absence of a guarantee
- Recognize a guaranteed-outcome claim from another provider as something to be candid about, without disparaging them
- Avoid any language that implies a guarantee exists in substance, even if the word itself is avoided

## When To Use

- Any time a candidate asks directly whether a job or outcome is guaranteed
- When a candidate compares Surely Placed to another program that claims to guarantee placement
- When a candidate's hesitation seems to be rooted in wanting certainty that this article can't honestly provide

## Understanding the Candidate's Concern

This concern is completely reasonable. A candidate considering a real investment of time, money, and — for many candidates in this population — a career transition tied to work authorization timing, wants some assurance the investment will pay off. Asking about a guarantee is a sign the candidate is thinking carefully, not a sign they're difficult to work with.

It's worth being honest about something else too: a genuine, individual outcome guarantee in a job placement context is not something any credible provider can actually deliver, because the outcome depends on factors no program fully controls — an employer's hiring decision, market conditions, and the candidate's own engagement with the process, among others. A program that claims to guarantee an individual outcome anyway is making a promise it can't actually keep. That's worth explaining plainly, without turning it into an attack on any specific competitor.

## Recommended Conversation Framework

Confirm directly and without hedging: there is no guarantee of placement. Don't soften this into something that sounds like there might be a workaround or an unstated exception.

Explain honestly why: an individual outcome depends on factors outside any program's control, and a genuine guarantee would require controlling things no program actually controls.

Reframe toward what's actually provided — preparation, structure, access, support — described specifically and honestly, without overstating it to compensate for the absence of a guarantee.

If the candidate mentions another program that does claim a guarantee, respond honestly and without disparaging them by name — it's fair to explain, generally, what a real guarantee would require and let the candidate draw their own conclusion.

## Questions to Ask

1. "Can I ask what a guarantee would actually mean to you here — is it about certainty of an outcome, or something else, like feeling like the investment is protected?"
2. "Have you seen another program claim to guarantee placement? I'm happy to talk honestly about what that would actually require to be real."
3. "Setting the word 'guarantee' aside — what would make you feel confident this is worth the investment, even without one?"
4. "Is there a specific worst-case scenario you're trying to protect against, so we can talk about that directly?"

## Best Practices

- Say plainly that there is no guarantee — don't let the answer become vague or evasive.
- Explain the reasoning honestly rather than treating the absence of a guarantee as something to minimize or rush past.
- Reframe toward real, specific value without overcompensating with inflated claims to make up for the lack of a guarantee.
- If a competitor's guarantee comes up, respond with honest, general reasoning rather than a direct attack on them.

## Common Mistakes

- Hedging the answer in a way that implies a guarantee might exist in some form, even when it doesn't
- Getting defensive about the absence of a guarantee instead of explaining it as a matter of honesty
- Overselling what is provided to compensate for not being able to offer a guarantee
- Disparaging a specific competitor who does claim a guarantee, rather than explaining honestly what such a claim would require
- Avoiding the question or changing the subject instead of answering it directly

## Real Conversation Examples

**Answering the direct question honestly:**

> **Candidate:** "So just to be clear — you're not guaranteeing me a job at the end of this?"
> **Advisor:** "That's right, and I want to be completely straightforward with you about that. No program can honestly guarantee an individual outcome — that depends on an employer's decision, market conditions, and your own engagement with the process, none of which any program fully controls. What I can tell you honestly is what we do provide, and let you judge whether that's worth it on its own terms."
> **Candidate:** "Okay, I appreciate you just saying that directly."

**Responding to a competitor's guarantee claim without disparaging them:**

> **Candidate:** "Another program I looked at says they guarantee placement, though."
> **Advisor:** "I won't comment on them specifically since I don't know the details of what they're offering. What I can say generally is that a genuine, individual outcome guarantee would require controlling things — like a specific employer's hiring decision — that aren't actually within any program's control. I'd encourage you to ask them directly what's behind that guarantee, and what happens if the outcome doesn't happen. I'd rather you go in with clear eyes than take my word for it."

## Key Takeaways

Confirm plainly that there is no placement guarantee, explain honestly why a genuine one isn't possible, and reframe the conversation toward the real, specific value that is provided — without overselling to compensate. This is one of the most trust-building moments available in the entire funnel when handled with direct honesty, and one of the most damaging when hedged or avoided.
