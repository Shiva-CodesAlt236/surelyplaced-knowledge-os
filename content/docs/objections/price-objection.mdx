# Price Objection

"It's expensive" can mean two very different things: the number itself, or the timing of paying it. They need different responses.

**Why this matters:** responding to the wrong version of "it's expensive" wastes the rest of the conversation and can make a genuinely solvable timing issue look like a hard no.
**When to use this:** any time cost objection language comes up, at any stage of the funnel, not just during a scheduled pricing conversation.
**What skill this builds:** distinguishing a number-objection from a timing-objection, and never stating an unconfirmed figure or inventing a discount to resolve either one.
**What happens next:** once the real shape of the concern is identified, the conversation addresses that specific concern directly rather than the price in the abstract.

## Lesson Objective

- Tell a number-objection apart from a timing or affordability-objection
- Never state an unconfirmed price figure, on this call or any other
- Never invent a discount or special arrangement to resolve the objection

## Coaching Notes

<CoachingNotes title="Coaching Notes">
Resist the urge to fill an awkward pause with a number just to keep the conversation moving. "Let me confirm that accurately" is a complete, professional answer on its own.
</CoachingNotes>

<CoachingNotes title="Why This Worked">
Asking "is it the number itself, or timing" took less than ten seconds and completely changed what the honest, useful next response needed to be. Responding to the wrong one would have wasted the rest of the call.
</CoachingNotes>
