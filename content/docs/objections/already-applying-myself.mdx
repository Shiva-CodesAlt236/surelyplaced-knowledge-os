# Already Applying Myself

## Purpose

A common objection, especially early in discovery, is some version of "I'm already applying to jobs on my own — why would I need this?" This article covers how to respond in a way that respects the candidate's existing effort, understands what's actually behind the objection, and honestly explains what Surely Placed adds without disparaging a self-directed job search that many candidates are reasonably proud of.

## Learning Objectives

- Respond to this objection without implying the candidate's own efforts haven't been good enough
- Ask questions that surface what's actually working and not working in a candidate's current search
- Explain what Surely Placed provides in terms specific to the candidate's own experience, not a generic pitch
- Recognize when this objection is really about trust, cost, or control rather than genuine doubt about value
- Avoid overstating what structured support can do compared to independent effort

## When To Use

- When a candidate states or implies they're already actively job searching on their own
- Early in discovery, when a candidate is testing whether this conversation is worth continuing
- When a candidate compares their own progress favorably against what a program might offer

## Understanding the Candidate's Concern

This objection often comes from a reasonable place: many candidates have put in real, sustained effort applying to jobs, and a pitch that implies they need "help" can land as a subtle judgment on that effort, even when it's not intended that way. Some candidates are also testing whether an advisor will actually listen to what's already been tried, or just deliver a generic pitch regardless of their specific situation.

There's also often something more specific underneath this objection: control, or trust. A candidate managing their own search may feel a sense of ownership over the process that they're not eager to hand off, and a candidate who's had a bad experience with a program or recruiter before may be testing whether this is going to be another disappointing outsourcing of effort that doesn't actually help.

## Recommended Conversation Framework

Start by asking about the current search, genuinely — what's working, what isn't, and how long it's been going. This does two things: it respects the effort already made, and it surfaces real, specific information to build a response around.

Avoid a generic pitch about what the program offers. Connect anything you describe directly to a gap the candidate has actually named in their own search.

Be honest about where independent effort and structured support genuinely differ, without disparaging what the candidate has already done. If their current approach is actually working reasonably well, say so — this objection sometimes has a straightforwardly honest answer: "You might not need this yet."

## Questions to Ask

1. "How long have you been applying, and what's that been like so far — any traction, or mostly silence?"
2. "What part of the search feels like it's working, and what part feels like the sticking point?"
3. "If nothing changed about your approach, where do you think you'd be in a few more months?"
4. "What would actually make this worth adding on top of what you're already doing?"

## Best Practices

- Ask before explaining — understand the candidate's current search in real detail before describing what Surely Placed provides.
- Connect anything you say about the program directly to a specific gap the candidate has named, not a general list of features.
- Be willing to say honestly that a candidate's current approach might already be working, if that's genuinely what the conversation reveals.
- Watch for whether this objection is really about trust or control rather than doubt about value, and respond to that if it surfaces.

## Common Mistakes

- Responding with a list of program features without first understanding what the candidate has already tried
- Implying, even subtly, that independent job searching isn't a real or valid effort
- Overstating what structured support changes compared to what a motivated, self-directed candidate can achieve alone
- Failing to ask how long the search has been going, which is often the most useful piece of information in this conversation
- Pushing forward with a pitch when the honest answer might be that the candidate's current approach is working fine for now

## Real Conversation Examples

**Understanding the current search before responding:**

> **Candidate:** "I'm already applying to a bunch of places myself, not sure I need this."
> **Advisor:** "That's a fair thing to bring up — can I ask how long you've been at it, and what it's been like so far?"
> **Candidate:** "About four months. I get a few responses but mostly it's silence, or I don't hear back after a first interview."
> **Advisor:** "That's useful to know, and it actually tells me something specific — the getting-in-the-door part seems to be working somewhat, but something's happening after that first conversation. That's a pretty different problem than 'not enough applications,' and it's worth talking about specifically rather than me giving you a generic pitch."

**Being honest that the objection might be valid:**

> **Candidate:** "Honestly, I've gotten two offers already this month just applying on my own."
> **Advisor:** "That's genuinely great, and I want to be straightforward with you — it sounds like your current approach is working. I'm not going to try to convince you otherwise. If anything changes, or if you want a second read on the offers themselves, feel free to reach back out."

## Key Takeaways

This objection deserves a real answer built on the candidate's actual experience, not a generic pitch. Ask what's working and what isn't in their current search before describing what Surely Placed offers, and be honest — including, when it's true, that their current approach might already be working well enough that they don't need anything else right now.
