# Not Interested

Candidate gives a soft initial brush-off (e.g. "I'm not interested", "No thanks", "Nah I'm good", "I don't think I need this").

> **Policy & Strategy Note:** When a candidate gives a soft first-time brush-off, advisors are permitted **ONE respectful diagnostic attempt** to identify whether there is a real underlying concern (such as timing, self-applying, or cost). If the candidate reiterates disinterest or asks not to be contacted, advisors must immediately transition to `explicit-refusal` and end the conversation professionally.

**Why this matters:** First-time "not interested" statements are often reflex reactions before understanding what is offered. A polite acknowledgment followed by one low-pressure question uncovers real fit without harassing the prospect.
**When to use this:** Upon receiving a first soft brush-off during an initial conversation.
**What skill this builds:** Acknowledging candidate hesitation gracefully, asking exactly one diagnostic question, and respecting candidate boundaries.
**What happens next:** Either the candidate reveals their true objection (pricing, DIY, timing) or confirms disinterest, leading to a clean exit.

## Lesson Objective

- Acknowledge soft brush-offs without pressure or defensiveness
- Execute exactly ONE respectful diagnostic question to surface the underlying topic
- Transition immediately to professional exit if the candidate confirms disinterest
