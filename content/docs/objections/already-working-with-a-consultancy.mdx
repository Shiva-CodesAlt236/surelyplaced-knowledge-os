# Already Working With a Consultancy

> **Policy note:** This article does not state whether a candidate can be enrolled with Surely Placed while working with another consultancy, recruiter, or program at the same time, including any exclusivity terms, conflict-of-interest rules, or overlap policy. That guidance does not yet exist in this repository. Advisors should not improvise an answer to a candidate's direct question about simultaneous engagement — flag it internally and confirm with the appropriate resource before answering. This article covers only how to have the conversation itself professionally, not the underlying policy answer.

## Purpose

Candidates sometimes mention, early or late in the funnel, that they're already working with another consultancy, recruiter, or program. This article covers how to handle that conversation with genuine curiosity and without disparaging the other party — while being explicit about a real gap: this repository does not yet contain Surely Placed's policy on whether or how a candidate can be engaged with more than one program at once.

## Learning Objectives

- Respond to this disclosure with curiosity rather than defensiveness or dismissiveness
- Ask questions that surface what the other engagement is and isn't providing, without asking the candidate to badmouth it
- Recognize the specific point in this conversation where a real policy question exists that this article cannot answer
- Avoid ever criticizing a competitor, named or unnamed, as a way of winning the comparison
- Escalate a direct exclusivity or overlap question rather than guessing at an answer

## When To Use

- When a candidate mentions they're already working with another program, recruiter, or consultancy
- When a candidate compares Surely Placed directly to another service they're currently using
- When a candidate asks directly whether they can work with more than one program or recruiter at the same time

## Understanding the Candidate's Concern

A candidate who mentions another engagement is often doing one of two things: comparison shopping, genuinely trying to understand what's different between the options, or testing whether Surely Placed is going to criticize the competition as a substitute for making its own case. Neither of these should be met with defensiveness. A candidate bringing this up openly is, if anything, giving the advisor useful information and a chance to be honest.

There's also a real, practical question that can come up here that this article cannot resolve: whether being engaged with another consultancy at the same time is something Surely Placed allows, restricts, or has a formal position on. That's a genuine policy gap, not a training gap, and it should be treated as one.

## Recommended Conversation Framework

Respond with curiosity, not comparison. Ask what the other engagement is actually providing, and what, if anything, feels like it's missing — this gives you real information rather than requiring you to guess at what makes Surely Placed different in the abstract.

Never criticize the other program or recruiter, even if the candidate volunteers a negative experience with them. Acknowledge what they share without piling on.

If the candidate asks directly whether they can work with both at once, or whether there's a conflict, say plainly that you want to give them an accurate answer rather than guess, and that you'll confirm it rather than answer on the spot.

## Questions to Ask

1. "What's that experience been like so far — what's working, and what feels like it's missing?"
2. "What made you look into other options alongside that, if you don't mind me asking?"
3. "Is there something specific you're hoping this conversation clarifies that the other one hasn't?"
4. "Are you looking to move away from that engagement, or were you thinking about running both at the same time?"

## Best Practices

- Treat this disclosure as useful information, not a threat to respond to defensively.
- Ask open, genuinely curious questions about the other engagement rather than questions designed to make it look bad.
- Never say anything critical about a named or unnamed competitor — let Surely Placed's own fit speak for itself.
- If a direct policy question comes up (can a candidate work with two programs at once), say plainly that you'll confirm rather than answer from assumption.

## Common Mistakes

- Criticizing the other consultancy or recruiter, even subtly, as a way of making Surely Placed look better by comparison
- Treating the disclosure as evidence the candidate isn't serious, rather than as normal comparison-shopping behavior
- Guessing at an answer to a direct question about simultaneous engagement or exclusivity instead of confirming it properly
- Skipping the chance to understand what the other engagement is and isn't providing, and going straight into a generic pitch instead

## Real Conversation Examples

**Responding with curiosity instead of comparison:**

> **Candidate:** "I'm actually already working with another consultancy, for what it's worth."
> **Advisor:** "Good to know, thanks for telling me. What's that experience been like so far — anything that's working well, or anything that feels like it's missing?"
> **Candidate:** "It's fine, honestly, but I haven't heard much from them in a few weeks."
> **Advisor:** "That's useful context. I'm not going to comment on them specifically, but I'm glad to talk through what's specific to your situation here, and you can judge for yourself where the real differences are."

**Handling a direct policy question honestly:**

> **Candidate:** "Can I actually work with both of you at the same time, or does that cause a problem?"
> **Advisor:** "That's a fair and important question, and I'd rather give you an accurate answer than guess at one right now. Let me confirm that properly and get back to you rather than tell you something that might not be right."
