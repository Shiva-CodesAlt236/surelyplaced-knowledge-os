# Objection Handling Framework

An objection isn't an obstacle. It's a candidate telling you, directly, what information they're still missing before they can decide.

## Start Here

**When do I use this?** Anywhere in the funnel — an objection can surface during Discovery, in the middle of Discussion, or right at Closing. It isn't tied to one stage the way the earlier modules are.

**What will this module help me achieve?** A repeatable way to find what's actually behind an objection instead of reacting to the first sentence of it, and the discipline to accept that a well-handled objection can still end in "no."

**When should I move to the next module?** Once objections stop feeling like something to survive and start feeling like information you can work with — Pricing is where that same discipline gets applied specifically to money, which is where objections show up most often.

## Lesson Objective

- Apply the same four-step approach to any objection, regardless of content
- Ask what's underneath an objection instead of responding to the surface version
- Accept that a well-handled objection can still end in "no"

## Coaching Notes

<CoachingNotes title="Coaching Notes">
"That's fair" costs nothing and keeps the door open. Resist any urge to sound more certain than you actually are before you know what you're responding to.
</CoachingNotes>

<CoachingNotes title="Why This Worked">
"A lot" turned out to mean bandwidth, not doubt about the decision itself. Responding to the wrong guess — reassurance about legitimacy, say — would have wasted the exchange entirely.
</CoachingNotes>

  objective="Get past the vague statement to something concrete"
  successCriteria="Advisor asks an open question instead of guessing at the concern"
  hints={["Don't guess what she means", "One open question does more than three assumptions"]}
  recommendedAnswer="Can you say more about that? I want to understand what's actually behind it before I respond to anything."
  managerTip="Resist the urge to respond to the objection as stated — most objections are a symptom, and this question is what surfaces the actual cause."
/>
