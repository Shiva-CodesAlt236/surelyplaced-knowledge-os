# Communication Standards

> This is a sales operations playbook. It covers general tone and responsiveness standards for advisor communication — it does not specify particular channels, tools, or response-time policies, which are Operational Policy Pending.

## Purpose

Every conversation covered elsewhere in this repository — discovery, discussion, objections, closing — assumes a consistent baseline of professionalism and honesty in how an advisor communicates. This article makes that baseline explicit, covering tone and responsiveness across all forms of candidate communication, not just live calls.

## Learning Objectives

- Maintain a consistent, professional, and warm tone across calls, messages, and written communication
- Respond to candidate communication in a timeframe that respects their decision timeline
- Apply the same honesty standard in writing that this repository establishes for live conversation
- Recognize when a written exchange should be moved to a live conversation instead

## When to Use

- In any written communication with a candidate — email, message, or other written channel
- When deciding how quickly a candidate's message needs a response
- When a written exchange starts covering ground better suited to a live conversation

## Operational Context

This repository doesn't specify which particular channels or tools Surely Placed uses for candidate communication, or a formal response-time policy — those are Operational Policy Pending. What can be said consistently with the rest of this repository is that communication, in any channel, should reflect the same tone and honesty standards established throughout Discovery, Discussion, Objections, Closing, and Pricing.

## Recommended Process

Match the tone of written communication to the tone already established in live conversation with that candidate — consistency across channels builds trust, while a mismatch (warm on calls, terse in writing) can undermine it.

Respond to a candidate's message in a timeframe that reflects genuine respect for their situation — a candidate actively deciding something time-sensitive deserves faster attention than a general inquiry, similar to the prioritization logic in [Daily Activity Standards](/docs/sales-operations/daily-activity-standards).

Move a written exchange to a live conversation when it starts covering something nuanced — an objection, a financial concern, anything from [Pricing Red Flags](/docs/pricing/pricing-red-flags) — rather than trying to resolve something sensitive entirely in writing.

Apply the same non-invention discipline in writing as in conversation: never state a specific price, policy, or guarantee in a message that wouldn't be said confidently on a call, and never write something in a message that contradicts what's said live.

## Advisor Responsibilities

- Keep tone consistent and professional across every communication channel
- Respond within a timeframe that reflects the candidate's actual situation and urgency
- Apply the same honesty standards in writing as in live conversation
- Recognize when a written exchange needs to move to a live conversation instead

## Quality Standards

Written communication should read, to the candidate, as continuous with the live conversations they've had — not like a different, less personal version of the advisor. A message a candidate could forward to someone else without embarrassment or confusion is generally a well-written one.

## Common Mistakes

- Letting tone in writing become noticeably colder or more transactional than tone on calls
- Responding too slowly to a candidate in an active, time-sensitive decision
- Trying to resolve a nuanced objection or financial concern entirely through written messages
- Writing something in a message that isn't consistent with what's been said live

## Escalation Guidance

Specific channel policies, required tools, or formal response-time targets are Operational Policy Pending and should be confirmed with a manager. If a written exchange reveals something requiring escalation — a red flag, a discount request, an out-of-authority question — follow [Internal Escalation Guidelines](/docs/sales-operations/internal-escalation-guidelines) rather than resolving it in writing.
