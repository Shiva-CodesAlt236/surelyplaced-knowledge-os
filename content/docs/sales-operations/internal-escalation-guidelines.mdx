# Internal Escalation Guidelines

> **Operational Policy Pending.** This repository does not define a specific approval hierarchy, chain of command, or named role to escalate to. Advisors should escalate through the latest approved internal process — typically a direct manager, unless current internal guidance specifies otherwise. This article covers when escalation is appropriate and what makes an escalation useful, not who specifically sits above an advisor in the organization.

## Purpose

Several other articles in this repository — [Discount Guidelines](/docs/pricing/discount-guidelines), [Pricing Red Flags](/docs/pricing/pricing-red-flags) — already point toward escalation for specific situations without naming a specific approval chain. This article consolidates the general discipline of escalation itself: recognizing when it's needed and doing it well, regardless of exactly who it goes to.

## Learning Objectives

- Recognize the categories of situations that call for escalation rather than an advisor's independent judgment
- Escalate clearly and with enough context for someone else to act on it
- Understand that this repository intentionally does not define a specific approval hierarchy
- Distinguish escalation from simply documenting a concern in a note and hoping it's noticed

## When to Use

- Any time a candidate's situation involves a genuine financial red flag, as covered in [Pricing Red Flags](/docs/pricing/pricing-red-flags)
- Any time a discount, exception, or nonstandard arrangement is requested, as covered in [Discount Guidelines](/docs/pricing/discount-guidelines)
- Any time a question requires legal, immigration, or technical expertise outside an advisor's role, as covered throughout the Visa Playbooks and Industry Playbooks modules
- Any time an advisor is genuinely uncertain whether something is within their authority to decide alone

## Operational Context

This repository doesn't name specific roles, titles, or an approval chain — that structure is Operational Policy Pending and defined by Surely Placed's actual current organization, not this documentation. What this repository does consistently establish, across the Pricing, Visa Playbooks, and Industry Playbooks modules, is that certain categories of questions are not for an advisor to resolve independently, regardless of who specifically they get routed to.

## Recommended Process

Recognize the situation as one requiring escalation as early as possible, rather than attempting to resolve it independently and escalating only if that attempt fails.

Escalate with enough specific context that whoever receives it can act without needing to reconstruct the situation from scratch — what the candidate said, what's being asked, and why it falls outside independent judgment.

Follow up on an escalation that hasn't been addressed rather than assuming it will be handled automatically, while also not repeatedly pressuring for an urgent answer when the situation itself isn't genuinely urgent.

Communicate honestly with the candidate about the fact that something needs to be confirmed, consistent with the honesty standard established in [Discount Guidelines](/docs/pricing/discount-guidelines) — never bridge the gap with a guess while waiting for the escalation to resolve.

## Advisor Responsibilities

- Recognize escalation-worthy situations promptly rather than attempting to resolve them independently
- Provide clear, complete context when escalating
- Follow up appropriately on unresolved escalations
- Never fill an escalation gap with a guess or invented answer while waiting for a response

## Quality Standards

A good escalation should be understandable and actionable by someone with no other context than what's provided in it. A vague escalation ("this candidate has a weird situation, can someone look at it?") is far less useful than a specific one that states exactly what's being asked and why it needs review.

## Common Mistakes

- Attempting to resolve an escalation-worthy situation independently before escalating, especially for discount or authority-related questions
- Escalating without enough context for the recipient to act
- Filling the gap with a guess or assumption while an escalation is pending
- Assuming a specific approval hierarchy exists, when this repository intentionally leaves that detail to current internal process

## Escalation Guidance

The specific person or role an advisor escalates to is Operational Policy Pending in this repository — follow the latest approved internal process, typically a direct manager unless told otherwise. What matters more than the specific destination is recognizing the situation early, escalating clearly, and never resolving it with a guess in the meantime.
