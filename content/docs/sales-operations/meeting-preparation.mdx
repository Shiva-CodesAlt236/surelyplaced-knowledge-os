# Meeting Preparation

> This is a sales operations playbook. It covers the general discipline of preparing for a call, not a script for what to say — see the Discovery, Discussion, Objections, and Closing modules for the conversation itself.

## Purpose

Every call type in this repository — discovery, discussion, closing — assumes the advisor walks in already knowing the candidate's situation. This article covers the preparation discipline that makes that possible: reviewing the record, anticipating what's likely to come up, and walking into the call ready rather than reconstructing context live.

## Learning Objectives

- Review a candidate's record efficiently before a call, rather than skipping preparation or over-preparing
- Anticipate the likely content of a call based on its stage and the candidate's history
- Recognize when a candidate's industry or visa category calls for reviewing the relevant playbook beforehand
- Walk into a call with the right context already loaded, rather than reconstructing it live

## When to Use

- Before any candidate call, regardless of stage
- When a candidate has been inactive for a while and the advisor needs to reorient before reconnecting
- When handing off or picking up a candidate from another advisor and needing to get up to speed quickly

## Operational Context

This article assumes an accurate, current candidate record already exists, consistent with [CRM Workflow](/docs/sales-operations/crm-workflow) and [Candidate Notes Standards](/docs/sales-operations/candidate-notes-standards). Preparation is only as good as the record it draws from — this article covers how to use that record well, not how to build it.

## Recommended Process

Review the candidate's most recent notes and current stage before the call, so the conversation can pick up naturally from where things actually left off rather than starting over.

If the candidate's industry or visa category is known, briefly review the relevant [Industry Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/industry-discovery-framework) or [Visa Playbook](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-discovery-framework) beforehand, so the conversation can reflect that context without needing to look anything up mid-call.

Anticipate the likely purpose of the call based on its stage — a discussion call likely needs the investment conversation prepared for (see [Discussing Investment](/docs/discussion/discussing-investment)), a closing call likely needs objection-handling ready (see [Objection Handling Framework](/docs/objections/objection-handling-framework)) — without assuming the candidate will follow the anticipated script exactly.

## Advisor Responsibilities

- Review the relevant record and context before every call, not just the ones that feel important
- Bring the right playbook context (industry, visa category) into calls where it's relevant
- Adjust prepared expectations honestly when the actual conversation goes differently than anticipated
- Avoid over-preparing to the point of following a rehearsed script rather than actually listening

## Quality Standards

A well-prepared call should feel, from the candidate's side, like the advisor remembers and understands their specific situation — not like they're meeting a stranger who happens to have read a summary. Preparation should be invisible in its effect: the candidate notices continuity, not effort.

## Common Mistakes

- Walking into a call without reviewing the record, forcing the candidate to re-explain their own situation
- Over-preparing to the point of relying on a rehearsed script instead of genuinely listening
- Failing to review relevant industry or visa context before a call where it clearly matters
- Assuming a call will go exactly as anticipated and being thrown off when it doesn't

## Escalation Guidance

If a candidate's record is missing information needed to prepare properly, that's a documentation gap worth raising — see [Candidate Notes Standards](/docs/sales-operations/candidate-notes-standards). If preparation reveals a situation genuinely outside an advisor's expertise (a specific legal, immigration, or technical question), route it through the appropriate channel rather than preparing to answer it directly — see [Internal Escalation Guidelines](/docs/sales-operations/internal-escalation-guidelines).

## Key Takeaways

Good preparation means reviewing the actual candidate record and relevant playbook context before a call, so the conversation reflects genuine continuity rather than starting from scratch. It should be thorough enough to inform the call without becoming a rehearsed script that replaces real listening.
