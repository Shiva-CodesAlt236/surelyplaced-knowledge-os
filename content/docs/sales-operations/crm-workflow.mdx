# CRM Workflow

> **Operational Policy Pending.** This repository does not document a specific CRM system, field names, stage labels, automation rules, or step-by-step data-entry workflow. This article covers the general principles of good CRM discipline — why accurate records matter and what they should generally accomplish — without inventing any specific tool, field, or process. Advisors should follow the latest approved internal CRM process for actual system usage.

## Purpose

Whatever system Surely Placed uses to track candidates, the underlying discipline is the same: a record should be accurate, current, and useful to anyone who needs to pick up where the last conversation left off. This article covers that discipline at a principle level, since the specific tool and workflow steps are not something this repository is positioned to document.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand why consistent CRM discipline matters, independent of which specific system is used
- Identify the general categories of information a candidate record should reflect
- Recognize the difference between a documentation gap this repository can fill and one that requires the actual current CRM process
- Know where to go when a specific CRM question arises that this article doesn't answer

## When to Use

- As a general orientation to CRM discipline for a newer advisor, before being trained on the actual current system
- As a reminder of the underlying purpose of record-keeping, independent of the specific tool in use
- When a workflow question arises that isn't answered here — as a prompt to escalate rather than guess

## Operational Context

This repository does not name or describe Surely Placed's actual CRM system, its fields, its stage labels, or any automation built into it. That information changes independently of this documentation and belongs in the current, internally maintained CRM training material — not here. What follows is the general discipline that should hold true regardless of which specific system or version is in use.

## Recommended Process

Keep records current at the natural checkpoints of a candidate relationship — after a call, after a significant update, and before handing a candidate off to another stage or person. A record that's accurate the day it's written but never updated again quickly becomes misleading rather than merely incomplete.

Record what actually happened and what a candidate actually said, rather than a summary shaped by what the advisor hopes happens next. See [Candidate Notes Standards](/docs/sales-operations/candidate-notes-standards) for the specific discipline behind writing notes themselves.

Where the actual system requires a specific field, stage label, or step not described here, follow the current internal training for that system rather than trying to infer it from this article.

## Advisor Responsibilities

- Keep candidate records accurate and current as the relationship progresses
- Learn and follow the actual, current CRM system and process through internal training rather than this repository
- Flag to a manager if this article or any other documentation appears to describe the CRM inaccurately, so it can be corrected or removed
- Treat record-keeping as part of the job itself, not an administrative afterthought

## Quality Standards

A good record allows someone unfamiliar with a specific candidate to understand their situation accurately from the record alone. That standard doesn't depend on which specific system is used — it depends on the advisor's consistency and honesty in keeping the record current.

## Common Mistakes

- Letting records fall out of date between conversations
- Writing notes that reflect hoped-for outcomes rather than what actually happened
- Assuming a workflow detail not covered in this repository is safe to guess at, rather than confirming it internally
- Treating CRM discipline as separate from the actual sales conversation, rather than a natural extension of it

## Escalation Guidance

Any specific question about the current CRM system — which fields to use, how stages are labeled, what automation exists, how data flows between tools — is **Operational Policy Pending** in this repository and should be directed to a manager or the appropriate internal process owner. Do not infer an answer from this article or from how a previous, possibly outdated, system worked.
