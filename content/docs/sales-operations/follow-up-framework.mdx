# Follow-Up Framework

> **Operational Policy Pending.** This repository does not specify exact follow-up timing rules, cadences, or the number of attempts expected before a candidate is considered unreachable. Those specifics belong to the latest approved internal process. This article covers the general principles behind purposeful, honest follow-up.

## Purpose

[Need Time to Think](/docs/objections/need-time-to-think) covers what to say when a candidate needs space before deciding. This article covers the operational side of that same situation: how to follow up afterward in a way that respects the candidate's stated timeline while still keeping the conversation moving forward honestly.

## Learning Objectives

- Distinguish purposeful follow-up from generic, repetitive check-ins
- Time follow-up around what the candidate actually said, rather than an arbitrary internal rhythm
- Recognize when continued follow-up is no longer appropriate
- Know that specific cadence rules, if any exist, are confirmed internally rather than assumed here

## When to Use

- After any conversation where a candidate needs time before deciding
- When a candidate has gone quiet after previously being engaged
- When planning how to space out contact with a candidate who is early in their decision process

## Operational Context

This repository does not define a specific number of days between follow-ups, a maximum number of attempts, or a point at which a candidate is formally considered unreachable — those are Operational Policy Pending. What's established elsewhere in this repository is the principle that follow-up should be honest and purposeful rather than pressuring (see [Need Time to Think](/docs/objections/need-time-to-think)), and this article extends that principle to the operational rhythm of follow-up itself.

## Recommended Process

Base the timing of a follow-up on what the candidate actually said about their own timeline, rather than a fixed internal schedule applied uniformly to everyone.

Give each follow-up a genuine reason to exist — new information, a direct answer to something they asked, or simply checking in as promised — rather than a repetitive "just checking in" with nothing new to offer.

Notice when continued follow-up stops being useful to the candidate and starts feeling like pressure, and adjust accordingly; the specific point at which to stop is a judgment call informed by the candidate's own signals, not a fixed rule documented here.

## Advisor Responsibilities

- Follow up when promised, and not sooner than what the candidate's own stated timeline suggests is appropriate
- Make each follow-up purposeful rather than a hollow check-in
- Read the candidate's engagement level honestly rather than following a script regardless of their responsiveness
- Confirm any specific cadence or attempt-limit policy internally rather than assuming one

## Quality Standards

A good follow-up should feel, from the candidate's side, like genuine continued interest in their decision — not administrative pressure to move faster than they're ready for. If a follow-up wouldn't feel welcome from the candidate's perspective, it needs a different approach or timing, not just fewer attempts.

## Common Mistakes

- Following up mechanically on a fixed schedule regardless of what the candidate actually asked for
- Sending repetitive "just checking in" messages with nothing new to offer
- Continuing to follow up well past the point where a candidate has clearly disengaged
- Assuming a strict cadence or attempt limit exists without confirming it internally

## Escalation Guidance

Specific follow-up cadence rules, maximum attempt counts, or formal "unreachable" thresholds are **Operational Policy Pending** in this repository and should be confirmed with a manager. If a candidate's situation seems to call for a different approach than what's described here — for example, a sensitive personal circumstance — raise it rather than applying a generic follow-up pattern.
