# Lead Stage Management

> **Operational Policy Pending.** This repository does not define the specific stage names, statuses, or transition rules used in Surely Placed's actual tracking system. This article covers the general discipline of knowing and accurately reflecting where a candidate stands, using the conceptual stages already established across Discovery, Discussion, Closing, and Objections — not a specific system's labels.

## Purpose

At any point, an advisor should be able to say clearly and accurately where a given candidate stands in the process — not just narrate the last conversation, but locate them on the map described in [Sales Workflow Overview](/docs/sales-operations/sales-workflow-overview). This article covers that discipline in general terms, since the exact labels and rules of the system used to record it are not documented here.

## Learning Objectives

- Locate a candidate's position within the conceptual stages already established across this repository's modules
- Understand why accurate stage awareness matters, independent of the specific tracking system used
- Recognize the difference between a candidate's actual readiness and their recorded status, and why the two should stay aligned
- Know to confirm specific stage labels and transition rules internally rather than inventing them

## When to Use

- When reviewing a candidate's status before a call, to prepare appropriately for where they actually are
- When handing a candidate off to another advisor or team, to communicate their status accurately
- When a candidate's recorded stage and their actual behavior seem to have drifted apart

## Operational Context

This repository doesn't specify the exact stage names, statuses, or automated transition rules used in Surely Placed's actual system — those are Operational Policy Pending. What this repository does establish, across Discovery, Discussion, Objections, and Closing, is the conceptual shape of the journey: a candidate is qualified, then presented with the program and investment, works through any objections, and eventually decides. Stage management means keeping a candidate's recorded status honestly aligned with where they actually are in that conceptual journey.

## Recommended Process

Update a candidate's stage promptly after any conversation that changes it — don't let the record lag behind reality by days or calls.

Base the stage on the candidate's actual behavior and readiness, not on hope or on how the advisor wishes the conversation had gone. A candidate who raised a serious unresolved objection is not ready to be marked as closing-stage, regardless of how the call felt in the moment.

Where the actual system requires specific stage names, codes, or transition triggers not described here, follow current internal training rather than approximating from this article's conceptual language.

## Advisor Responsibilities

- Keep a candidate's recorded stage honest and current
- Learn the actual system's specific stage labels and transition rules through internal training
- Communicate a candidate's real status clearly during any handoff, rather than leaving it to be inferred from notes alone
- Raise it internally if a stage seems to have been advanced or left stagnant inappropriately

## Quality Standards

A candidate's recorded stage should match what an advisor would say out loud if asked directly, without needing to check anything else first. If the two don't match, the record needs to be corrected, not the verbal description softened to match the record.

## Common Mistakes

- Advancing a candidate's stage optimistically rather than based on actual readiness
- Letting stage records go stale after a call that changed the candidate's actual status
- Assuming this article's conceptual stages map one-to-one onto the actual system's specific labels without confirming
- Failing to communicate a candidate's true stage clearly during a handoff

## Escalation Guidance

Questions about the actual system's specific stage names, statuses, or automated transition rules are **Operational Policy Pending** and should go to a manager or the appropriate internal process owner. If a candidate's stage seems to have been set inconsistently by someone else, raise it directly rather than silently correcting or ignoring it.
