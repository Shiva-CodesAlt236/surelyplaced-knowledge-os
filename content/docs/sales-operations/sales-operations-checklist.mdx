# Sales Operations Checklist

> This is a sales operations playbook, not an employee handbook. Several items below are marked **Operational Policy Pending** because this repository doesn't define the specific CRM system, stage labels, activity targets, or approval hierarchy behind them — confirm those specifics through the latest approved internal process.

## Purpose

The nine articles in this module cover distinct pieces of operational discipline — workflow awareness, CRM habits, stage tracking, daily prioritization, follow-up, preparation, notes, escalation, and communication. This checklist pulls them into a single working reference an advisor can run through periodically, without reopening each article individually.

## Learning Objectives

- Confirm operational discipline is being applied consistently across a working week
- Use this checklist as a self-audit tool rather than a one-time onboarding read
- Identify which specific article to revisit when a gap surfaces
- Recognize which items are general discipline versus Operational Policy Pending specifics that require internal confirmation

## When to Use

- Periodically, as a self-audit of operational habits rather than conversation skill
- When onboarding a new advisor, as a companion summary to the full module
- When something in the sales process has clearly broken down and the source isn't obvious

## Operational Context

This checklist assumes the reader has already gone through the full module; it exists as a condensed reference, not a substitute for it. Items marked Operational Policy Pending point to specifics this repository intentionally does not define.

## Recommended Process

Workflow awareness
- [ ] I can locate any given candidate within the overall sequence described in [Sales Workflow Overview](/docs/sales-operations/sales-workflow-overview)
- [ ] I understand how this module's operational discipline supports, rather than replaces, the conversation-focused modules

Records and stage tracking
- [ ] My candidate records are current, not lagging behind the most recent conversation (see [CRM Workflow](/docs/sales-operations/crm-workflow) — specific system details are Operational Policy Pending)
- [ ] Each candidate's recorded stage matches what I'd say out loud if asked directly (see [Lead Stage Management](/docs/sales-operations/lead-stage-management))

Daily rhythm and follow-up
- [ ] My daily attention is prioritized by candidate urgency and stage, not convenience (see [Daily Activity Standards](/docs/sales-operations/daily-activity-standards) — specific activity targets are Operational Policy Pending)
- [ ] My follow-ups are purposeful and timed around what each candidate actually said, not a generic rhythm (see [Follow-Up Framework](/docs/sales-operations/follow-up-framework))

Preparation and notes
- [ ] I review the relevant record and playbook context before every call (see [Meeting Preparation](/docs/sales-operations/meeting-preparation))
- [ ] My notes are specific and honest, including what didn't go well, not just favorable summaries (see [Candidate Notes Standards](/docs/sales-operations/candidate-notes-standards))

Escalation and communication
- [ ] I escalate discount requests, financial red flags, and out-of-authority questions promptly rather than resolving them independently (see [Internal Escalation Guidelines](/docs/sales-operations/internal-escalation-guidelines) — the specific approval hierarchy is Operational Policy Pending)
- [ ] My written communication matches the tone and honesty standard of my live conversations (see [Communication Standards](/docs/sales-operations/communication-standards))

## Advisor Responsibilities

- Run through this checklist periodically as genuine self-review, not a box-ticking exercise
- Address any gap by returning to the specific article it relates to
- Confirm Operational Policy Pending items through the current internal process rather than guessing at them
- Raise a pattern of difficulty with any item to a manager rather than working around it silently

## Quality Standards

A consistent "yes" across this checklist over time is a reasonable indicator of healthy operational habits — a recurring "no" on the same item across multiple reviews is worth addressing directly rather than accepting as normal.

## Common Mistakes

- Treating this checklist as a one-time onboarding exercise rather than a periodic self-audit
- Marking an item as fine without genuinely reflecting on recent behavior
- Trying to resolve an Operational Policy Pending gap independently instead of confirming it internally
- Using this checklist as a substitute for the depth found in each individual article

## Escalation Guidance

Any item marked Operational Policy Pending should be confirmed through the current internal process, not resolved by guessing based on this checklist alone. A pattern of difficulty on any specific item is worth raising with a manager directly.
