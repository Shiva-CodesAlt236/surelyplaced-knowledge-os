# Daily Activity Standards

> **Operational Policy Pending.** This repository does not state a specific number of calls, follow-ups, or other activity targets an advisor is expected to hit per day or week. Those figures, if they exist, belong to the latest approved internal process, not this article. What follows are the general principles behind organizing an effective advisor day.

## Purpose

Beyond any specific quota, there's a general discipline to how an effective advisor structures a day: prioritizing the candidates who need attention most, following up reliably, and not letting any one part of the pipeline go quiet without reason. This article covers that discipline at a principle level.

## Learning Objectives

- Prioritize a day's activity based on candidate stage and urgency rather than convenience
- Understand why consistency across days matters more than any single high-activity day
- Recognize the difference between being busy and being effective
- Know that specific activity targets, if any exist, are confirmed internally rather than assumed from this article

## When to Use

- When planning a day's work across multiple candidates at different stages
- When deciding which candidate to prioritize when time is limited
- When onboarding a new advisor to the general shape of daily work, before specific targets are set internally

## Operational Context

This repository does not specify a required number of daily calls, follow-ups, or other logged activities — any such target is Operational Policy Pending and set through the current internal process, not documented here. What can be said generally, consistent with the rest of this repository, is that a candidate's stage (see [Lead Stage Management](/docs/sales-operations/lead-stage-management)) and the time sensitivity of their situation should drive how a day's attention is allocated.

## Recommended Process

Start a day by reviewing which candidates are waiting on a follow-up, which are mid-conversation and time-sensitive, and which are further out and lower urgency — then prioritize accordingly rather than working through a list in whatever order it happens to appear.

Protect time for candidates already in an active, time-sensitive conversation (for example, someone deciding between closing now or later) over candidates who are earlier in the process and less time-sensitive, without neglecting the latter entirely.

Close out a day by confirming that anything requiring same-day follow-up has actually happened, rather than assuming it will get done the next day.

## Advisor Responsibilities

- Prioritize daily activity based on candidate need and urgency, not convenience
- Maintain consistency across days rather than alternating between high and low activity
- Confirm any specific activity target or quota through the current internal process rather than this repository
- Flag capacity concerns honestly if the volume of candidates genuinely exceeds what can be handled well

## Quality Standards

A well-run day should leave no candidate who needed attention that day without it, and shouldn't require the advisor to recall from memory who's waiting on what — that's what the CRM discipline in [CRM Workflow](/docs/sales-operations/crm-workflow) is for. Activity should reflect genuine candidate need, not just volume for its own sake.

## Common Mistakes

- Prioritizing easy or comfortable conversations over urgent or difficult ones
- Letting a candidate go quiet simply because addressing them felt less pressing in the moment
- Treating activity volume as a proxy for effectiveness rather than a means to it
- Assuming a specific daily quota exists or doesn't, rather than confirming through the current internal process

## Escalation Guidance

Any question about specific daily or weekly activity targets is **Operational Policy Pending** in this repository and should be directed to a manager. If actual candidate volume genuinely exceeds what can be handled with appropriate quality, that's a capacity concern worth raising directly rather than quietly cutting corners.
