# Hiring Red Flags

## Purpose

The decision and offer stage carries its own specific risks distinct from the earlier recruiter and interview stages covered in [Recruiter Red Flags](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/recruiter-red-flags). This article separates what genuinely warrants caution at this later stage from what's ordinary delay or process friction.

## Scope

This article covers what to notice specifically at the decision and offer stage. It does not make a legal determination about any specific situation, and it does not treat ordinary internal delay as evidence of a problem.

## Core Content

**Worth a careful, direct conversation:**
- An offer whose written terms materially differ from what was discussed verbally, worth clarifying directly with the employer before responding.
- Repeated, vague postponement of a promised decision timeline well beyond what was originally communicated, worth a polite check-in rather than continued silent waiting.

**Worth routing rather than resolving directly:**
- A verbal offer that's withdrawn or a written offer that's rescinded after acceptance — this carries real legal and financial implications a candidate should discuss with an appropriate qualified professional rather than the advisor addressing informally.
- Pressure to accept an offer within an unreasonably short window, especially paired with a claim that the offer will otherwise disappear entirely.
- Anything in an offer's terms touching work authorization sponsorship or status that conflicts with what the candidate already understands — route to [Visa Discovery Framework](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-discovery-framework) rather than resolving it here.

**Not actually a red flag:**
- A decision or offer that takes longer than a candidate expected, especially after multiple interview rounds — as [Stakeholder Decision-Making](/docs/hiring-intelligence/stakeholder-decision-making) and [Offer Decision Process](/docs/hiring-intelligence/offer-decision-process) already cover, this is often ordinary internal coordination, not a bad sign.
- An employer taking time to answer a candidate's own reasonable clarifying question about the offer — this reflects normal internal process, not reluctance or hidden concern.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article follows the same escalate-rather-than-resolve pattern used throughout this repository's other red-flags articles, including [Recruiter Red Flags](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/recruiter-red-flags), applied here specifically to the decision and offer stage.

## Advisor Guidance

Take a rescinded offer, a material term mismatch, or unreasonable acceptance pressure seriously and route it to an appropriate qualified professional rather than advising on it directly. Reassure candidates that a longer-than-expected decision or offer timeline is ordinary and not something to read into.

A rescinded offer, a material mismatch between verbal and written terms, or unreasonable acceptance pressure should be routed to a qualified professional rather than resolved informally — while a longer-than-expected decision or offer timeline is ordinary and not a sign of a problem.
