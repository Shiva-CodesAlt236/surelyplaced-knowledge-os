# Hiring Lifecycle

## Purpose

[Recruiter Workflow](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/recruiter-workflow) already covers what a recruiter is doing day to day. This article zooms out to the organizational view — why a role opens in the first place and what has to happen internally before and after the recruiter-facing steps a candidate actually sees.

## Scope

This article describes a commonly observed general pattern at the organizational level, not a fixed or universal sequence. It does not claim every employer structures headcount approval or internal sign-off the same way, and it does not state a typical duration for any stage.

## Core Content

Before a role is ever posted, it typically requires some form of internal approval — budget, headcount, or leadership sign-off — that has nothing to do with any individual candidate and happens entirely before the recruiter's work begins. Delays or changes candidates sometimes notice mid-process (a role temporarily paused, requirements shifting) often trace back to this internal layer rather than to anything about the candidates being considered.

Once a role is active, it generally moves through sourcing and screening (covered in [Recruiter Intelligence](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/overview)), interview rounds, an internal decision, an offer, and — once accepted — an onboarding process that begins the employment relationship itself. Each of these stages involves people and approvals a candidate doesn't see directly.

After a role is filled or a search is closed for any other reason, the internal process usually includes closing out other candidates in the pipeline — which is part of why candidates sometimes hear back later than expected, or not at all, even when nothing about their own candidacy changed.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article gives the organizational context surrounding [Recruiter Workflow](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/recruiter-workflow)'s recruiter-level process, and sets up [Candidate Evaluation Framework](/docs/hiring-intelligence/candidate-evaluation-framework) and the decision-stage articles that follow it in this module.

## Advisor Guidance

Use this lifecycle view to help a candidate understand that delays, pauses, and even a role disappearing entirely often trace back to internal organizational factors that have nothing to do with them. Avoid stating a specific timeline for any stage.

## Key Takeaways

A role typically requires internal approval before it's ever posted and continues generating internal work — closing out other candidates, coordinating onboarding — after it's filled, which explains much of what can otherwise feel like unexplained delay or silence during a candidate's own process.
