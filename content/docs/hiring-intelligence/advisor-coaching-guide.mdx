# Advisor Coaching Guide

> This title recurs across five modules now, each covering a different conversation. [Sales Coaching](/docs/sales-coaching/coach-review-framework) reviews an advisor's own call performance. [Interview Intelligence](/docs/interview-intelligence/advisor-coaching-guide) covers pre- and post-interview coaching. [Resume Intelligence](/docs/resume-intelligence/advisor-coaching-guide) and [LinkedIn Intelligence](/docs/linkedin-intelligence/advisor-coaching-guide) cover those respective artifacts. [Recruiter Intelligence](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/advisor-coaching-guide) covers navigating recruiter interactions. This one covers the decision-and-offer stage specifically — often the most emotionally charged conversation of the entire process.

## Purpose

Waiting for a decision, and then navigating an actual offer, tends to carry more emotional weight than any earlier stage. This article covers how an advisor supports a candidate through that specific weight without overpromising an outcome the advisor has no way to know.

## Scope

This article covers the advisor's own conversational approach during the waiting and offer stages. It does not cover contacting the employer on the candidate's behalf, and it does not describe a specific negotiation-coaching service this repository doesn't define.

## Core Content

During the waiting period, the most useful thing an advisor can offer is often just accurate context — using [Hiring Lifecycle](/docs/hiring-intelligence/hiring-lifecycle) and [Interview Feedback Process](/docs/hiring-intelligence/interview-feedback-process) to explain what's likely happening internally, rather than empty reassurance that everything will work out. Accurate context tends to reduce anxiety more durably than optimism the advisor can't actually back up.

When an offer arrives, help the candidate slow down before reacting, using [Offer Management](/docs/hiring-intelligence/offer-management) as the framework for reading it carefully and identifying real questions before responding. Resist the pull to tell a candidate what to do — the decision to accept, negotiate further, or decline is genuinely theirs.

If a candidate reports something from [Hiring Red Flags](/docs/hiring-intelligence/hiring-red-flags), respond seriously and promptly, and be direct that certain questions belong with a qualified professional rather than the advisor — this isn't a deflection, it's the honest and responsible answer.

Whether the outcome is an offer, a rejection, or continued silence, close by checking in on how the candidate is actually doing, not just what happens next procedurally — this stage is often where a candidate needs steady, honest presence more than any specific piece of new information.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This conversation draws on the accurate-context-over-empty-reassurance approach already used in [Advisor Coaching Guide](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/advisor-coaching-guide) in Recruiter Intelligence, extended here to the higher-stakes decision-and-offer stage specifically.

## Advisor Guidance

Offer accurate context instead of empty reassurance, help the candidate slow down and read an offer carefully without telling them what to decide, take any red flag seriously and route what needs routing, and close by checking on the candidate as a person, not just the process.
