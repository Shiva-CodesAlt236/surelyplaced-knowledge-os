# Offer Decision Process

## Purpose

A candidate who's cleared interview feedback still isn't guaranteed an offer, and understanding why helps advisors set realistic expectations without discouraging a candidate who's genuinely in a strong position. This article covers what typically stands between a positive feedback synthesis and an actual offer.

## Scope

This article describes a commonly observed general pattern. It does not claim to know how any specific employer makes this decision, and it does not suggest a positive interview feedback synthesis guarantees an offer will follow.

## Core Content

Positive interviewer feedback is usually necessary but not always sufficient on its own. An employer often still needs to confirm budget approval for the specific compensation level under discussion, finalize which candidate to prioritize if more than one is under serious consideration for the same or a related role, and sometimes secure sign-off from someone who wasn't part of the interview process at all.

Where multiple strong candidates are being considered for one role, this stage is where that comparison typically happens — a process largely invisible to any individual candidate, and not something a candidate's own performance can fully control once other genuinely strong candidates are also in play.

Once an internal decision is reached, there's often a short additional delay before a candidate hears anything, while the offer itself — compensation, start date, and other terms — gets finalized internally before being communicated. This article does not describe how any specific employer structures that internal offer-finalization step.

## Advisor Guidance

Help a candidate hold two things at once: positive feedback is a genuinely good sign, and it still isn't a guarantee, because of factors — budget, competing candidates, additional approvals — outside anyone's full visibility. Avoid promising an offer is coming based on positive signals alone.

Positive interview feedback is a genuinely good sign but not a guarantee — budget confirmation, competing-candidate comparison, and additional internal approvals all sit between a positive synthesis and an actual offer, largely invisible to the candidate.
