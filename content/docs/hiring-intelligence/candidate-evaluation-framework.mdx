# Candidate Evaluation Framework

> **Illustrative Only:** This article describes broad, commonly observed evaluation categories. It does not state that any category is weighted a specific way, does not claim to describe any specific employer's actual rubric, and does not predict an evaluation outcome.

## Purpose

[Recruiter Decision Framework](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/recruiter-decision-framework) already covers what a recruiter is generally weighing at the screening stage. This article covers what an interview panel is typically evaluating once a candidate moves further into a process.

## Scope

This article describes general categories of evaluation at a conceptual level. It does not rank these categories against each other, and it does not claim they're applied identically across employers, roles, or individual interviewers.

## Core Content

**Role-specific capability.** Later interview rounds generally probe more deeply into whether a candidate can actually do the specific work the role requires — this is usually where technical or functional depth, rather than general fit, gets evaluated most directly.

**Working style and collaboration.** Panels commonly consider how a candidate is likely to work with the existing team — communication style, how they handle disagreement or ambiguity, and similar softer signals that are harder to standardize than a technical assessment.

**Trajectory and motivation.** Interviewers often form a view of why a candidate wants this specific role, and whether that motivation seems durable — this connects directly to the narrative work covered in [Resume Storytelling](/docs/resume-intelligence/resume-storytelling), since a candidate who can articulate their own throughline tends to land this more convincingly.

**Practical fit.** Compensation expectations, timeline, location or remote-work fit, and — where relevant — work authorization continue to matter at this stage, the same way they did during initial screening, and can still affect a decision independent of how strong the rest of the evaluation looks.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article extends [Recruiter Decision Framework](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/recruiter-decision-framework)'s general categories past the screening stage into the fuller interview-panel evaluation, and connects to [Resume Storytelling](/docs/resume-intelligence/resume-storytelling) for the narrative work that supports the trajectory-and-motivation category specifically.

## Advisor Guidance

Share these categories to help a candidate feel prepared across the full range of what's typically evaluated, not just technical depth. Never rank or weight the categories, and don't imply that strength in one guarantees an outcome regardless of the others.
