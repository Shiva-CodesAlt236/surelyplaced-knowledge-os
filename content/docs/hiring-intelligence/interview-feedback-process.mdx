# Interview Feedback Process

## Purpose

The gap between a candidate's last interview and hearing anything back is often the most anxious part of a process, and much of that gap is genuinely explained by an internal feedback process the candidate never sees. This article gives advisors a general picture of what's actually happening during that silence.

## Scope

This article describes a commonly observed general pattern for collecting and synthesizing interviewer feedback. It does not claim every employer follows this exact process, and it does not state how long it typically takes.

## Core Content

After each interview, an interviewer commonly records their own impressions — sometimes informally, sometimes through a structured form tied to the categories in [Candidate Evaluation Framework](/docs/hiring-intelligence/candidate-evaluation-framework) — before comparing notes with anyone else. This sequencing, where it's followed, is often intentional: an interviewer forming an independent view first, rather than being anchored by a colleague's impression, tends to produce a more genuinely varied set of perspectives to work from.

Once all interviewers have weighed in, a debrief conversation — sometimes a meeting, sometimes an asynchronous written exchange — typically follows, where the panel works toward a shared read on the candidate. Disagreement among interviewers at this stage is normal and doesn't mean anything is wrong with the process or the candidate; it's simply multiple people forming honest, sometimes differing, impressions of the same conversation.

Scheduling this debrief around several busy people's calendars is often the single biggest source of delay a candidate experiences at this stage — not any active uncertainty about the candidate specifically.
