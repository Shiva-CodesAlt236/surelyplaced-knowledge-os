# Recruiter Workflow

## Purpose

Most of what makes a hiring process feel opaque to a candidate is simply not knowing what happens on the other side between conversations. This article gives advisors a general map to share, so waiting periods and next steps feel less mysterious.

## Scope

This article describes a commonly observed general sequence, not a fixed or universal process. It does not claim every employer follows these steps in this order, and it does not state a typical timeline, since timelines vary too widely to state usefully.

## Core Content

A recruiter typically opens a role by working with a hiring manager to confirm what the role actually needs — sometimes a well-defined list, sometimes something the hiring manager is still refining. This intake step, invisible to candidates, often explains why a job posting can read as vague or why requirements seem to shift slightly over the course of a process.

From there, a recruiter generally reviews incoming applications or sourced candidates against that intake conversation, has an initial screening call with candidates who seem like a plausible fit, and forwards a shortlist to the hiring manager for further rounds. At each handoff, the recruiter is often coordinating scheduling and expectations on both sides — a task that can make response times feel slow even when a process is moving normally.

Toward the end of a process, a recruiter is usually involved in gathering feedback from interviewers, coordinating a decision internally, and — once an employer decides to move forward — handling the offer conversation. None of this repository defines what happens in that offer conversation specifically, since that varies by employer and situation.

## Advisor Guidance

Share this as a general map to reduce anxiety about silence or delay between steps, not as a guarantee of how any specific process will unfold. Be clear with candidates that timelines and exact steps vary considerably by employer.

## Key Takeaways

A hiring process generally moves from intake through screening, shortlisting, interview rounds, and an offer stage, with real coordination work happening at each handoff that often explains delays — but the exact steps and timeline vary too much by employer to state as a fixed rule.
