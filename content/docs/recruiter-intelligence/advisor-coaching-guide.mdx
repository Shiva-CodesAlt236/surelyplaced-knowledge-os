# Advisor Coaching Guide

> Several modules share this exact title, and each covers a separate conversation. [Sales Coaching](/docs/sales-coaching/coach-review-framework) reviews an advisor's own call performance. [Interview Intelligence](/docs/interview-intelligence/advisor-coaching-guide) covers the pre- and post-interview conversation. [Resume Intelligence](/docs/resume-intelligence/advisor-coaching-guide) covers the resume-review conversation. [LinkedIn Intelligence](/docs/linkedin-intelligence/advisor-coaching-guide) covers the LinkedIn-presence conversation. This one is about helping a candidate understand and navigate their interactions with recruiters.

## Purpose

Everything else in this module gives an advisor background knowledge about how recruiting works. This article covers how to actually put that knowledge into a conversation that leaves a candidate feeling more capable of navigating recruiter interactions on their own.

## Scope

This article covers the advisor's own approach to this coaching conversation. It does not cover contacting a recruiter directly on a candidate's behalf, and it does not describe a specific Surely Placed recruiter-liaison service this repository doesn't define.

## Core Content

Start from what the candidate has actually experienced so far, if anything — a candidate already mid-process with a confusing signal from a recruiter needs a different conversation than one who hasn't started reaching out yet. Ground the conversation in their real situation rather than a generic briefing.

Use [Recruiter Workflow](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/recruiter-workflow) and [Hiring Manager Collaboration](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/hiring-manager-collaboration) to reframe confusing or frustrating moments — a slow response, a mismatched signal between a recruiter and hiring manager — as ordinary features of the process rather than something specific to the candidate. This reframing is often the most immediately useful thing an advisor can offer.

When a candidate reports something from [Recruiter Red Flags](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/recruiter-red-flags), respond calmly and take it seriously in proportion to what it actually is — most reported concerns turn out to be ordinary process friction once discussed, and the few genuine ones deserve clear, prompt escalation rather than a reassurance that dismisses them.

Close by checking whether the candidate feels more oriented going into their next recruiter interaction, not just whether they've absorbed information — the goal is confidence grounded in realistic understanding, not a memorized set of facts about how recruiting works.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This conversation draws on the same reframing-through-understanding approach [Advisor Coaching Guide](/docs/interview-intelligence/advisor-coaching-guide) in Interview Intelligence uses for interview-related anxiety, applied here to the recruiter relationship specifically.

Ground the conversation in the candidate's actual situation, use this module's general knowledge to reframe confusing moments as ordinary rather than alarming, take any reported red flag seriously and proportionately, and close by confirming genuine orientation rather than information transfer alone.
