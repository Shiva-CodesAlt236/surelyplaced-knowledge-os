# Internal vs. Agency Recruiters

> This article is strictly about the employer's own hiring function — an internal recruiter employed by the hiring company, or a third-party agency recruiter working on that employer's behalf to fill the role. It has no connection to Surely Placed or to any program a candidate might separately be engaged with, which is covered instead in [Already Working With a Consultancy](/docs/objections/already-working-with-a-consultancy).

## Purpose

A candidate contacted by two different people about what looks like similar work — one an employee of the company, one clearly working for multiple companies at once — sometimes isn't sure what the difference means for them. This article gives advisors a plain, neutral explanation to offer.

## Scope

This article describes the general structural difference between these two roles. It does not claim one is more trustworthy, more effective, or preferable to work with than the other — both are common, legitimate parts of how employers fill roles, and which one a candidate encounters says nothing in itself about the role or the employer.

## Core Content

An internal recruiter is directly employed by the hiring company and typically works exclusively on that company's open roles. They tend to have deeper, ongoing context on the specific team and hiring manager, since that relationship is part of their day-to-day job.

An agency recruiter works for a separate recruiting or staffing firm and is typically engaged by one or more employers to help fill specific roles, sometimes across several companies at once. An agency recruiter's day-to-day relationship with any single hiring manager may be more limited, but they often have broader visibility across multiple open roles a candidate might be a fit for.

Neither structure determines the quality of a specific opportunity or a specific recruiter's professionalism — both are ordinary parts of how hiring works, and a candidate shouldn't read anything into which one they happen to be dealing with beyond understanding the structural difference itself.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article gives context that makes [Recruiter Workflow](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/recruiter-workflow)'s general process more concrete — an agency recruiter and an internal recruiter both follow a broadly similar workflow, but starting from a different vantage point relative to the employer.

## Advisor Guidance

Explain this distinction neutrally if a candidate asks or seems confused about who they're actually talking to. Never suggest that one type of recruiter is preferable, and keep this conversation entirely separate from any discussion of Surely Placed's own relationship to other programs.
