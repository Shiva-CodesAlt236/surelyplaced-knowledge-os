# Candidate Screening Process

## Purpose

The early screening stage can feel like a black box to a candidate. This article gives advisors a general, honest picture of what a recruiter is typically looking at and for during that stage, so a candidate can prepare sensibly rather than guess.

## Scope

This article covers the general screening stage in broad terms. It does not describe a specific employer's actual screening criteria, and it does not claim any single factor determines whether a candidate advances.

## Core Content

Before any conversation happens, a recruiter is typically reviewing whatever artifacts a candidate has already made available — commonly a resume and a LinkedIn profile — for a first impression of fit against the role's stated requirements. This is exactly the material [Resume Reading Framework](/docs/resume-intelligence/resume-reading-framework) and [LinkedIn Profile Analysis](/docs/linkedin-intelligence/linkedin-profile-analysis) already help a candidate prepare well.

During an initial screening conversation, a recruiter is generally listening for basic fit, communication clarity, and logistics — availability, authorization, general expectations — more than deep technical or business substance, which usually gets evaluated later by someone else. A candidate who communicates their background clearly and answers logistics questions directly tends to move through this stage smoothly, regardless of how the deeper evaluation later goes.

This article does not claim to know exactly what any specific recruiter weighs most heavily, since that varies by person, role, and employer — general clarity and preparation are the most useful, universally applicable things a candidate can control.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article draws directly on the review technique already built in [Resume Reading Framework](/docs/resume-intelligence/resume-reading-framework) and [LinkedIn Profile Analysis](/docs/linkedin-intelligence/linkedin-profile-analysis), applied here from the recruiter's side of the same review.

## Advisor Guidance

Help candidates see this stage as fit-and-clarity focused rather than a deep evaluation, and connect it back to the resume and LinkedIn preparation they've likely already done. Avoid any claim about exactly what a specific recruiter is weighing.

## Key Takeaways

Initial screening generally centers on a recruiter's first-pass review of a candidate's existing materials plus a fit-and-clarity conversation, not deep technical assessment — the most useful, universal preparation a candidate can control is a clear resume, a coherent LinkedIn profile, and direct communication.
