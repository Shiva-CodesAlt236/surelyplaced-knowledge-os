# Recruiter Red Flags

## Purpose

Most recruiter interactions are ordinary and professional, but a candidate benefits from knowing what genuinely warrants caution versus what's simply a normal, if occasionally frustrating, part of a hiring process. This article draws that line.

## Scope

This article covers what to notice in a candidate's reported recruiter interactions and how an advisor should respond. It does not make a legal determination about any specific interaction, and it does not treat ordinary process friction as evidence of a problem.

## Core Content

**Worth a careful, direct conversation:**
- A recruiter pressuring a candidate to make a fast decision under artificial urgency, especially around accepting an offer or providing sensitive information immediately.
- Vague or shifting answers about the actual employer, role, or compensation structure when a candidate asks direct, reasonable questions.

**Worth routing rather than resolving directly:**
- A recruiter or purported employer asking a candidate to pay money, provide banking details, or purchase equipment as a condition of the process — this is a known pattern worth taking seriously and escalating rather than advising on informally.
- Anything touching work authorization eligibility framed in a way that conflicts with what the candidate already understands from [Visa Red Flags](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-red-flags) — route to that article and qualified immigration counsel rather than resolving it here.
- Any indication the recruiter or employer contact may not be who they claim to be.

**Not actually a red flag:**
- Slow response times, generic outreach messages, or a recruiter who doesn't yet have full details about the role — these are common and don't by themselves indicate a problem with the recruiter, the employer, or the candidate's standing.
- A recruiter asking direct, standard logistics questions — timeline, authorization status, compensation expectations — which is ordinary and expected, not intrusive.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article follows the same escalate-rather-than-resolve pattern already established in [Resume Red Flags](/docs/resume-intelligence/resume-red-flags), [LinkedIn Red Flags](/docs/linkedin-intelligence/linkedin-red-flags), and [Interview Red Flags](/docs/interview-intelligence/interview-red-flags), applied here to the recruiter relationship specifically.

## Advisor Guidance

Take a payment request, pressure tactic, or identity concern seriously and route it rather than resolving it informally. Reassure candidates that slow responses and generic-sounding early outreach are ordinary and not something to read into.
