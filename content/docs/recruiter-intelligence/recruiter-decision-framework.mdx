# Recruiter Decision Framework

> **Illustrative Only:** This article describes general, commonly cited categories of consideration in recruiting. It does not state that any specific factor is weighted a specific way, does not claim to predict a specific recruiter's decision, and does not guarantee that meeting any of these considerations produces advancement.

## Purpose

Candidates often want to know "what recruiters are actually looking for." This article gives advisors an honest, general answer built from broad categories rather than a false formula.

## Scope

This article covers general categories of consideration, described at a conceptual level. It does not rank these categories, does not assign them relative weight, and does not claim they apply identically across employers, roles, or individual recruiters.

## Core Content

**Fit against the stated role.** A recruiter is generally checking whether a candidate's background plausibly matches what the hiring manager described during intake — not a perfect match, but a reasonable one worth exploring further.

**Communication clarity.** Especially in earlier stages, how clearly a candidate can describe their own background and answer direct questions tends to factor into whether a recruiter feels confident presenting them internally.

**Logistics and availability.** Timeline, location or remote-work fit, and — where relevant — work authorization status are practical considerations a recruiter typically needs resolved before moving a candidate forward, independent of how strong the rest of the fit looks.

**Internal coordination factors.** As covered in [Hiring Manager Collaboration](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/hiring-manager-collaboration), a recruiter's own read on a candidate is often checked against the hiring manager's, and against other candidates already in the pipeline — factors entirely outside a candidate's control or visibility.

None of these categories is presented as more important than another, and this article does not claim completeness — a specific recruiter or process may weigh things this article doesn't anticipate.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article synthesizes [Candidate Screening Process](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/candidate-screening-process) and [Hiring Manager Collaboration](/docs/recruiter-intelligence/hiring-manager-collaboration) into the general categories a candidate might reasonably think about, without turning either into a formula.

## Advisor Guidance

Share these categories as a way to help a candidate feel prepared and grounded, never as a checklist that guarantees advancement. Be explicit that factors outside the candidate's control — internal coordination, other candidates in the pipeline — are real and not something preparation alone can resolve.

## Key Takeaways

A recruiter's decision generally involves role fit, communication clarity, logistics, and internal coordination factors largely outside a candidate's control — none of these is weighted or ranked here, and none is presented as a guarantee of any outcome.
