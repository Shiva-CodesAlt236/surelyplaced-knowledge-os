# Candidate Qualification

## Purpose

Not every candidate who calls Surely Placed is a fit, and that's not a failure of marketing — it's just true, and it's the advisor's job to find out quickly and honestly, not to talk everyone into believing they're a fit. This article covers the qualification pass every discovery call needs: confirming work authorization, confirming an actual IT background, and getting an honest read on the candidate's current situation, before any conversation about goals or program fit goes further.

Qualification is not the same as the deeper visa conversation planned for the Visa & Work Authorization module. This article is about the broad gate — is this someone we can plausibly help at all — and it hands off to that more detailed conversation whenever authorization specifics need to be worked through carefully.

## Learning Objectives

- Confirm a candidate's basic eligibility (authorization, IT background) without making the conversation feel like an interrogation
- Recognize the difference between a candidate who isn't a fit right now and one who isn't a fit at all
- Ask about current employment status in a way that gets an honest answer instead of a guarded one
- Know when to pause qualification and hand off to a dedicated work authorization conversation
- Say "this isn't a fit" clearly and kindly when that's the honest answer

## Candidate Psychology

Qualification questions can feel, from the candidate's side, like being screened out — and a lot of candidates have been screened out of things before, sometimes for reasons that felt arbitrary or unfair. That history means some candidates answer qualification questions a little defensively, or round their situation up slightly, hoping to clear a bar they're not sure they'll actually clear.

This is worth naming plainly: some candidates will describe their work authorization status more favorably than it actually is, not out of dishonesty exactly, but out of hope, or because they genuinely don't fully understand their own status yet. Others will describe their IT background more broadly than it really is, for similar reasons. Neither of these is unusual, and neither should be treated as a red flag on its own — it's just something to gently and specifically clarify.

There's also a real fear underneath a lot of these calls that goes beyond "will I qualify" — it's closer to "will this person think less of me if I don't." Good qualification questions are asked in a tone that makes it clear the answer, whatever it is, doesn't change how seriously the candidate is being taken.

## Advisor Objective

Get a clear, honest picture of whether the candidate holds valid work authorization in a country Surely Placed serves and has a real IT background to build on — and if either one is genuinely missing, say so directly rather than continuing a conversation that isn't going anywhere real.

## When To Use

- Early in every discovery call, generally right after the opening and before goals are discussed in depth
- Any time a candidate's stated background or status sounds vague or inconsistent with something they said earlier
- Before recommending any next step, track, or program detail — qualification always comes first
- When a candidate asks directly whether they're "a good fit" — that's an invitation to run this conversation explicitly, not to reassure vaguely

## Discovery Questions

1. "Just so I have this right — what's your current work authorization status, in your own words?"
2. "And that's for which country — US, Canada, or UK?"
3. "Tell me about your IT background — what have you actually worked on, day to day, and for how long?"
4. "What's your current employment situation — working, between roles, something else?"
5. "Is there anything about your eligibility or background that you're personally unsure about?"

## Why Each Question Matters

The first question is the actual gate. Surely Placed works with candidates who already hold valid work authorization — this isn't a soft preference, it's the basic premise of the whole relationship, and it has to be confirmed early and honestly rather than assumed from context.

The second question matters because "work authorization" alone doesn't mean much without knowing the country — a status that's solid in one country says nothing about eligibility in another, and candidates sometimes assume more portability than actually exists.

The third question separates candidates with real, hands-on IT experience from candidates who are IT-adjacent or IT-curious but don't yet have a background to build a placement conversation around. This isn't a judgment — it just changes what kind of conversation makes sense next.

The fourth question grounds the conversation in the candidate's actual day-to-day reality, which affects both urgency and capacity — someone unemployed and job searching full-time is in a very different position than someone employed and exploring carefully.

The fifth question is a deliberate invitation for the candidate to raise their own doubts before the advisor has to raise them — and it often produces more honest, useful information than the four questions before it combined.

## How to Interpret Candidate Answers

If a candidate answers the first question with confidence and specificity ("I'm on a Skilled Worker visa, sponsored by my current employer"), you likely have a clear, workable starting point — proceed normally, and route any deeper specifics to the dedicated visa-eligibility process only if something later in the call raises a question.

If the answer is vague or uses a status name that doesn't quite match anything real ("I have a work visa, I think it's fine"), don't guess and don't assume it'll work out — this is the exact moment to pause and hand off to a focused work authorization conversation before continuing.

On the IT background question, listen for concrete detail — tools, responsibilities, actual project work — versus general enthusiasm without substance. A candidate who says "I've always been interested in tech" is telling you something different from a candidate who says "I've spent two years doing manual QA on a mobile app team." Both are worth a real conversation, but they're different conversations.

On employment status, listen for what's underneath the label. "Between roles" can mean anything from "laid off yesterday" to "left on good terms six months ago and I've been traveling." The label alone doesn't tell you enough — ask.

## Follow-up Questions

- If authorization status sounds uncertain: "It sounds like there might be some uncertainty there — would it help to walk through exactly what you do know, so we're clear on what still needs confirming?"
- If IT background sounds thin: "What's the most hands-on technical work you've done so far, even if it wasn't your main job?"
- If employment status sounds emotionally loaded: "How long has that been the case, and how are you feeling about it at this point?"
- After question five, regardless of the answer: "Is there anything else, even something small, that's been sitting in the back of your mind about whether this makes sense for you?"

## Common Mistakes

- Accepting a vague authorization answer and moving on instead of clarifying it directly
- Treating a thin IT background as disqualifying without exploring what related experience might actually exist
- Asking qualification questions in a flat, checklist tone that makes the candidate feel like they're being processed
- Skipping straight to program details before qualification is genuinely settled
- Avoiding the honest "this isn't a fit" conversation when it's clearly warranted, because it feels uncomfortable to say

## Things Never To Say

- "Don't worry about the visa stuff, we'll figure it out later." (Work authorization is the foundational gate — it doesn't get deferred, and implying otherwise sets a false expectation early.)
- "Everyone with any tech interest can make this work." (Not true, and saying it undermines the honest qualification this whole conversation depends on.)
- Any suggestion that Surely Placed sponsors work authorization or can resolve an authorization problem — we work with candidates who already hold valid status; we don't create it.

## Conversation Example

> **Advisor:** "Just so I have this right — what's your current work authorization status, in your own words?"
> **Candidate:** "I'm on OPT, I think it's still valid, I graduated about eight months ago."
> **Advisor:** "Okay — and just to clarify, do you know if it's your initial OPT period, or have you moved into a STEM extension?"
> **Candidate:** "Honestly, I'm not totally sure. I think my school mentioned something about an extension but I never filed anything."
> **Advisor:** "That's really good to know, and I appreciate you being upfront instead of guessing. This is actually one of those things worth getting clear on properly before we go much further, since it affects your timeline in a real way. Can we come back to that in a few minutes with a more focused conversation, and in the meantime, tell me about your IT background?"
> **Candidate:** "Sure — I did a computer science degree, and I've been doing freelance web development on the side, mostly small business sites."
> **Advisor:** "Good, that's real, concrete experience to work with. We'll come back to nail down the OPT question properly, but that gives me a solid starting point on the background side."

## Advanced Advisor Tips

Experienced advisors learn to separate two very different situations that can sound similar on the surface: a candidate who's genuinely not a fit, and a candidate who's a fit but doesn't yet know enough about their own situation to present it clearly. The first requires an honest, kind "no." The second requires patience and a willingness to slow down and help the candidate get clear before qualification can really be assessed at all.

It's also worth building the instinct to ask the "anything you're personally unsure about" question even when a candidate has answered everything else confidently — some of the most important information in a qualification conversation only surfaces when a candidate is given direct permission to raise their own doubts.

## Summary

Qualification is the honest gate every discovery call has to pass through before anything else matters — confirmed work authorization in a country Surely Placed serves, and a real IT background to build on. Ask directly, listen for vagueness rather than accepting it, and hand off to a focused visa conversation whenever authorization specifics need more care than a quick confirmation. When qualification genuinely doesn't hold up, say so plainly — that's not a failed call, it's an honest one.
