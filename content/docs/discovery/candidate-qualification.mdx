# Candidate Qualification

Not every candidate who calls is a fit. That's not a bad sales day — that's just true. Your job is to find out fast and honestly, not talk everyone into believing they qualify.

## Learning Objective

- Confirm work authorization without it feeling like an interrogation
- Get a real, concrete picture of IT background — not just enthusiasm
- Say "this isn't a fit" clearly and kindly when that's the honest answer

## Business Importance

- We work with candidates who already hold valid work authorization — we don't create it
- A vague "yeah I have a work visa I think" answer, left unclarified, blows up two calls later
- Candidates sometimes round their status up out of hope, not dishonesty — your job is to clarify, not accuse
- Continuing a conversation that isn't going anywhere wastes the candidate's time as much as yours
- Getting this wrong early is far more expensive than getting a "no" early

## Real Conversation

**Sales Executive:**

Just so I have this right — what's your current work authorization status, in your own words?

**Candidate:**

I'm on OPT.

I think it's still valid — I graduated about eight months ago.

**Sales Executive:**

Okay — and do you know if that's your initial OPT period, or have you moved into the STEM extension?

**Candidate:**

Honestly...

I'm not totally sure.

I think my school mentioned something about an extension, but I never actually filed anything.

**Sales Executive:**

That's really good to know — and I appreciate you being upfront instead of guessing.

This is one of those things worth getting clear on properly, because it affects your timeline in a real way.

Can we come back to that in a few minutes, and for now — tell me about your IT background?

**Candidate:**

Sure.

I did a computer science degree.

And I've been doing freelance web development on the side — mostly small business sites.

**Sales Executive:**

Good — that's real, concrete experience to work with.

We'll come back and nail down the OPT question properly. But that gives me a solid starting point on the background side.

**Candidate:**

Okay, that's fair. I was actually worried you'd just brush past the visa thing.

**Sales Executive:**

No chance. That part doesn't get skipped — ever.

## Why Every Sentence Works

**"What's your current work authorization status, in your own words?"**

Why?
"In your own words" matters — it surfaces what the candidate actually understands, not what they think the correct-sounding answer is.

**"Do you know if that's your initial OPT period, or have you moved into the STEM extension?"**

Why?
A specific follow-up, not a vague one. Vague authorization questions get vague answers. Specific ones get real information — or reveal exactly where the gap is.

**"That's really good to know, and I appreciate you being upfront instead of guessing."**

Why?
Rewards honesty immediately. A candidate who admits uncertainty needs to feel that was the right move, not a mistake — or they'll start rounding up next time.

**"Can we come back to that in a few minutes?"**

Why?
Doesn't let an unclear answer sit unresolved, but also doesn't derail the whole call on it right now. Parking it, by name, keeps momentum without dropping it.

**"That gives me a solid starting point on the background side."**

Why?
Confirms what's real and usable before moving forward — concrete detail ("freelance web development, small business sites") gets acknowledged specifically, not generically praised.

## Common Mistakes

❌ Accepting a vague authorization answer and moving on
❌ Treating a thin IT background as disqualifying without asking what else exists
❌ Asking qualification questions in a flat, checklist tone
❌ Skipping straight to program details before qualification is settled
❌ Avoiding the honest "this isn't a fit" conversation because it's uncomfortable

## Best Practices

- Ask authorization status in the candidate's own words first
- Always confirm which country the authorization applies to
- Listen for concrete detail on IT background, not just enthusiasm
- Ask directly: "is there anything about your eligibility you're personally unsure about?"
- If it's not a fit, say so plainly — that's a good call, not a failed one

## Roleplay

### Roleplay 1 — Easy

**Scenario**
Candidate answers confidently and specifically on the first try.

**Candidate Profile**
Divya, 27, on a Skilled Worker visa in the UK, sponsored by current employer, clear on her status.

**Conversation**

**Sales Executive:**
What's your current work authorization status?

**Candidate:**
I'm on a Skilled Worker visa, sponsored by my current employer. That's confirmed, no issues there.

**Decision Point**
Take the confident answer and move straight to background, or verify once more?

**Best Response**
"Great, that's a clear starting point. Tell me about your IT background — what have you actually worked on?"

**Why**
When an answer is specific and confident, over-verifying wastes goodwill. Move forward, but keep the door open to return if anything later contradicts it.

### Roleplay 2 — Medium

**Scenario**
Candidate's IT background sounds thin at first, but has more underneath than the first answer suggests.

**Candidate Profile**
Karan, 30, describes himself as "IT-curious," has actually done two years of hands-on manual QA that he didn't think to mention.

**Conversation**

**Sales Executive:**
Tell me about your IT background — what have you actually worked on, day to day?

**Candidate:**
Um, honestly, I've just always been interested in tech. Nothing major.

**Decision Point**
Accept "nothing major" and move to disqualify, or dig one layer deeper?

**Best Response**
"Interested in tech is a start — but what's the most hands-on technical work you've done so far, even if it wasn't your main job title?"

**Why**
Candidates often undersell real experience because they don't think it "counts." Asking for the most hands-on thing, specifically, surfaces experience that a generic question misses entirely.

### Roleplay 3 — Difficult

**Scenario**
Candidate asks directly about H1B and gets defensive when the answer isn't what they hoped.

**Candidate Profile**
Farhan, 29, currently on F1, no OPT filed yet, asks about H1B sponsorship immediately, tone turns skeptical when told SurelyPlaced doesn't sponsor.

**Conversation**

**Sales Executive:**
What's your current work authorization status?

**Candidate:**
I'm on F1 right now. But can you guys sponsor H1B for me?

**Sales Executive:**
We don't sponsor work authorization — we work with candidates who already hold valid status. So for you, the real first step is OPT, not H1B yet.

**Candidate:**
So what's even the point of this call then? Everyone keeps saying they'll "figure it out later."

**Decision Point**
Reassure vaguely that "it'll work out," or stay precise about what's actually true right now?

**Best Response**
"I get why that's frustrating to hear again. I won't say it'll figure itself out — here's exactly what's true: once your OPT is filed and approved, that opens real options. Before that, there isn't a placement conversation to have yet. I'd rather tell you that straight than waste your time."

**Why**
This candidate has clearly heard false reassurance before. Matching that pattern — even gently — confirms his skepticism. Precision, even when it's not the answer he wanted, is what actually earns trust here.

## Practice Exercise

1. **Rewrite the opening** — Write your own version of the work authorization question for a candidate who already sounds defensive about being asked.
2. **Respond to the objection** — Candidate says: "Why do you even need to know my visa status right now?" Write your response.
3. **Continue the conversation** — Candidate says: "I think I have OPT but I'm honestly not 100% sure what stage." Write the next 3 lines.

## Cheat Sheet

- Ask authorization status in the candidate's own words
- Always confirm which country it applies to
- Vague answer = pause and clarify, never guess
- Ask for concrete IT detail, not general enthusiasm
- "Anything you're personally unsure about?" often surfaces the real gap
- Never say "don't worry about the visa stuff, we'll figure it out later"
- Never say "everyone with any tech interest can make this work"
- We don't sponsor authorization — we work with candidates who already have it
- A thin background isn't automatically disqualifying — ask what else exists
- If it's genuinely not a fit, say so directly and kindly
