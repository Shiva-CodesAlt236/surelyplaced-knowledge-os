# Candidate Qualification

Not every candidate who calls is a fit. Your job is to find out fast and honestly — not talk everyone into believing they qualify.

## Lesson Objective

- Confirm work authorization without it feeling like an interrogation
- Get a real, concrete picture of IT background — not just enthusiasm
- Say "this isn't a fit" clearly and kindly when that's the honest answer

## Estimated Time

⏱ 8–10 minutes

## Conversation Timeline

| Time | Phase |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Authorization status, in candidate's own words |
| 0:20–0:50 | Specific follow-up — decision point |
| 0:50–1:20 | Park the gap, move to IT background |
| 1:20–1:40 | Confirm concrete experience |

## Full Conversation Viewer

**Sales Executive (Aayush):**

Just so I have this right — what's your current work authorization status, in your own words?

**Candidate (Priya):**

I'm on H1B. My employer's my sponsor.

**Sales Executive:**

Good, that's clear. And that's for the US specifically?

**Candidate:**

Yes, US.

**Sales Executive:**

Perfect. Tell me about your IT background — what have you actually worked on, day to day?

**Candidate:**

I've been doing QA for about three years. Manual testing mostly, some automation with Selenium recently.

**Sales Executive:**

That's solid, concrete experience. Is there anything about your eligibility or background you're personally unsure about?

**Candidate:**

Actually... yeah. I keep hearing H1B holders can't really switch tracks easily. Is that true?

## Decision Point

⏸ **PAUSE. What should Aayush say next?**

A. "Don't worry about that, we'll figure it out."
B. "That's a fair question — H1B does add real constraints around timing and sponsorship that we should walk through honestly, not wave away."
C. "Everyone can make a switch work with the right approach."

## Correct Answer

**B.**

**Sales Executive:**

That's a fair question, and I'd rather answer it properly than wave it away.

H1B does add real constraints — around timing, around sponsorship — that we should walk through honestly.

It doesn't mean it's off the table. It means we plan around it specifically, not generally.

**Candidate:**

Okay, that's actually reassuring, weirdly. Nobody's been that direct about it.

## Coaching Notes

- Don't rush past a direct eligibility question — it's the moment candidates are testing your honesty
- Say "constraints," not "problems" — accurate, not alarming
- Keep tone matter-of-fact, not apologetic

## Why This Worked

- Doesn't minimize a real constraint just to keep the mood positive
- Reframes "off the table" vs. "planned around specifically" — precise, not vague
- Directness here is what earns the "nobody's been that direct" reaction

## Common Mistakes

❌ "Don't worry about that, we'll figure it out" — vague reassurance, not information
❌ "Everyone can make a switch work" — not true, and it undercuts every honest answer around it
❌ Treating a thin IT background as disqualifying without asking what else exists
❌ Skipping straight to program details before qualification is settled

## Better Alternative

Weaker response: *"Everyone can make a switch work with the right approach."*

Why it's weaker: Sounds encouraging but isn't true, and candidates who've been burned before can usually tell. It trades one moment of good feeling for the whole call's credibility.

## Easy Roleplay

**Scenario:** Candidate answers confidently and specifically on the first try.

**Candidate:** Emily — US Citizen, no visa complexity, clear background.

**Conversation:**

**Sales Executive:** What's your current work authorization status?

**Emily:** US citizen, no restrictions on my end.

**Decision Point:** Move straight to background, or verify further?

**Best Response:** "Great, that's a clean starting point. Tell me about your IT background."

**Why:** No ambiguity here — over-verifying wastes goodwill. Move forward.

## Medium Roleplay

**Scenario:** Candidate isn't sure which stage of OPT they're in.

**Candidate:** Rahul — F1 OPT, graduated 8 months ago, hasn't filed the STEM extension.

**Conversation:**

**Sales Executive:** Do you know if that's your initial OPT period, or have you moved into the STEM extension?

**Rahul:** Honestly, I'm not totally sure. My school mentioned an extension but I never filed anything.

**Decision Point:** Accept the ambiguity and move on, or pause to flag it directly?

**Best Response:** "That's good to know — I appreciate you being upfront instead of guessing. This one's worth getting clear before we go further. Can we park it and come back in a few minutes?"

**Why:** Rewarding honesty, then parking (not dropping) the gap, keeps momentum without leaving it unresolved.

## Difficult Roleplay

**Scenario:** Candidate is skeptical that her status even counts, and doesn't fully trust the answer.

**Candidate:** Neha — H4 EAD, previously told by another agency that her status "doesn't really work" for placement.

**Conversation:**

**Sales Executive:** What's your current work authorization status?

**Neha:** H4 EAD. But honestly, another agency told me that basically doesn't work for most placements. So I don't know why I'm even on this call.

**Sales Executive:** I hear that — and I want to be precise, not just reassuring. H4 EAD is valid work authorization; it does come with dependency on your spouse's H1B status, which is a real factor, not a disqualifier.

**Neha:** So it does work?

**Decision Point:** Say "yes it works" flatly, or stay precise about what "works" actually means here?

**Best Response:** "It's not a flat yes or no — it means we look at your spouse's H1B timeline as part of the picture. I'd rather walk through that specifically than give you a one-word answer that turns out to be wrong."

**Why:** She's been given a flat, wrong answer before. Precision — not reassurance — is what rebuilds trust with a candidate who's already been misled once.

## Quick Reference Cheat Sheet

- Ask authorization status in the candidate's own words
- Always confirm which country it applies to
- Vague answer = pause and clarify, never guess
- Ask for concrete IT detail, not general enthusiasm
- "Anything you're personally unsure about?" surfaces the real gap
- Never say "don't worry about the visa stuff, we'll figure it out later"
- Never say "everyone can make this work"
- We don't sponsor authorization — we work with candidates who already have it
- If it's genuinely not a fit, say so directly and kindly
