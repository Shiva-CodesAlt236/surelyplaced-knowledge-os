# Discovery Call Checklist

## Purpose

This article ties the rest of the Discovery Calls module together into something you can actually run against, mid-call or right after. It's not a new set of skills — it's a way of checking, before you end a call or move a candidate forward, that the opening, rapport, qualification, and goals work actually happened, and happened well enough to build on.

The checklist itself:

1. Did the candidate understand, in their own words, what this call was for?
2. Did I hear a real answer to "what made you reach out now" — not just a polite one?
3. Is work authorization status confirmed, specific, and clear — not assumed or rounded up?
4. Do I have a real, concrete picture of the candidate's IT background, not just enthusiasm?
5. Do I understand the actual need behind the candidate's stated goal, not just the goal itself?
6. Did I surface any real constraints — timing, finances, family, location — that shape what's realistic?
7. Did the candidate's answers stay open and specific through the whole call, or did they get shorter partway through?
8. Have I told the candidate anything today that wasn't fully honest, even if it would have been easier to say?
9. Do I know what happens next, and does the candidate know it too?

If you can't answer one of these with a clear yes, that's not a failed call — it's information about where the next conversation needs to start.

## Learning Objectives

- Use the checklist above as a live diagnostic during a call, not just an after-the-fact review
- Recognize which checklist items are missing based on the shape of the conversation so far
- Ask a small number of targeted questions late in the call to close specific gaps
- Avoid the trap of treating checklist completion as more important than the quality of what's actually been discussed
- End a discovery call with a clear, shared understanding of what happens next, every time

## Candidate Psychology

Candidates can tell, even if they couldn't name it directly, when a call was thorough versus when it was rushed through. A call that ends with the advisor circling back to fill obvious gaps ("actually, wait, I should ask about your visa status") reads as disorganized, and disorganization quietly erodes the trust built earlier in the call — even if everything up to that point went well.

On the other hand, a candidate can also tell when a checklist is being run mechanically rather than genuinely — when questions start to feel like boxes being ticked rather than things the advisor actually wants to know. The goal of this checklist is to catch real gaps, not to turn the end of a good conversation into a stiff formality. Candidates respond well to "let me make sure I've got a full picture before we wrap up" — it reads as care, not procedure — as long as it doesn't feel like a script being read off a page.

## Advisor Objective

Confirm, before the call ends, that every checklist item above has genuinely been covered — and if something's missing, close the gap with a direct, specific question rather than letting the call end with an unspoken hole in it.

## When To Use

- In the last several minutes of every discovery call, as a genuine check rather than a formality
- Mid-call, silently, as a way of tracking what's already been covered and what still needs attention
- When a call has been interrupted, rescheduled, or run unusually short, to identify exactly what still needs to happen before moving forward
- When handing a candidate off to another advisor or to the next stage — the checklist doubles as a clean way to brief the next conversation

## Discovery Questions

1. "Before we wrap up, let me just check a couple of things — does that work?"
2. "Is there anything about your work authorization that's still unclear to either of us?"
3. "Did I get a real sense of what you're actually looking for, or is there something I might have missed?"
4. "Is there anything you expected to talk about today that we didn't get to?"
5. "What's your understanding of what happens next?"

## Why Each Question Matters

The first question is a transition, not filler — it tells the candidate the call is entering a different mode, which helps them shift from open conversation to confirming specifics without feeling like they're being tested.

The second question exists because authorization is the one checklist item that can never be left ambiguous. If there's any lingering uncertainty by the end of the call, this is the last chance to catch it before next steps get discussed.

The third question is a direct invitation for the candidate to correct the advisor's understanding, which is often more reliable than the advisor simply reviewing their own notes internally — candidates will tell you what you missed if you ask them plainly.

The fourth question catches anything the candidate came in wanting to cover that never came up — sometimes because the conversation went somewhere more important, but sometimes just because it got missed.

The fifth question confirms alignment on next steps, which matters because a call can go well in every other respect and still fail if the candidate leaves unsure what happens after they hang up.

## How to Interpret Candidate Answers

If the candidate answers question three with confident agreement ("yeah, you got it"), take that as a real signal, but don't treat it as the only check — pair it with your own honest read of the checklist above, since candidates sometimes agree out of politeness rather than genuine confirmation.

If question four surfaces something the candidate expected to cover, don't rush through it just because the call is nearly over — a rushed answer to something the candidate specifically flagged as important undoes some of the trust built over the rest of the call.

Pay close attention to how confidently the candidate answers question five. A clear, accurate answer means alignment is real. A vague or slightly wrong answer means the next-steps conversation needs to happen again, more clearly, before the call ends — don't assume it landed just because you said it once.

## Follow-up Questions

- If authorization still feels unclear after question two: "Let's not leave that open — can we spend two more minutes making sure we're both clear on where things stand?"
- If something is missing in question three: "Tell me more about that — I'd rather hear it now than assume I already know."
- If question four surfaces an unaddressed topic: "That's worth covering properly — do we have time now, or should we set up a specific follow-up for it?"
- If question five reveals misalignment: "Let me say that back clearly, so we're both on the same page before we end the call."

## Common Mistakes

- Running through the checklist mentally but never actually confirming the gaps out loud with the candidate
- Treating the checklist as a rigid script to read verbatim rather than a set of things to genuinely confirm in whatever order fits the conversation
- Ending a call with an unresolved authorization question because it felt awkward to bring back up
- Assuming a candidate's earlier silence on a topic meant they had nothing to add, rather than checking directly
- Rushing the "what happens next" confirmation because the call has already run long

## Things Never To Say

- "We're basically out of time, so let's just wrap up." (If a real gap exists — especially around authorization — running out of time is a reason to schedule a short follow-up, not a reason to leave it unresolved.)
- "I think we covered everything." (Said without actually checking — this defeats the entire purpose of the checklist and risks ending the call on an assumption instead of a confirmation.)
- Any version of promising a specific next-step outcome that hasn't actually been decided — be precise about what happens next, not optimistic about it.

## Conversation Example

> **Advisor:** "Before we wrap up, let me just check a couple of things — that okay?"
> **Candidate:** "Sure."
> **Advisor:** "Is there anything about your work authorization that's still unclear to either of us? Earlier you mentioned you weren't sure if you'd filed for the STEM extension."
> **Candidate:** "Oh, right — no, I still need to check on that. I don't actually know for sure."
> **Advisor:** "Good that we caught that again — I don't want us moving forward on an assumption there. I'll flag that as something to nail down before we go further. Did I get a real sense of what you're actually looking for today, or is there something I might have missed?"
> **Candidate:** "No, I think you got it — the stability thing, more than the specific title."
> **Advisor:** "Good. Last thing — what's your understanding of what happens next?"
> **Candidate:** "I think... you're going to follow up once I've confirmed my OPT status?"
> **Advisor:** "Exactly right. Once that's clear, we'll pick this back up properly."

## Advanced Advisor Tips

Experienced advisors run this checklist continuously in the background throughout the call, not just at the end — which means the wrap-up rarely surfaces a real surprise, because gaps get caught and closed while the conversation is still naturally on that topic. Treat the end-of-call version less as a discovery exercise and more as a final confirmation of things you've already been tracking.

It's also worth resisting the urge to treat a fully checked box as license to stop paying attention to that topic for the rest of the call. A candidate's situation can shift information mid-conversation — a goal that seemed settled ten minutes ago can look different by the end, and it's worth a quick mental re-check rather than assuming nothing's changed.

## Summary

A discovery call is only as strong as its weakest confirmed detail, and this checklist exists to make sure nothing important gets left as an assumption. Check authorization clarity, confirm the candidate's actual goal in their own words, ask directly about anything that might have been missed, and never end a call without both people agreeing on what happens next. Run it quietly throughout the conversation, and use the final minutes to confirm out loud, not to discover for the first time.
