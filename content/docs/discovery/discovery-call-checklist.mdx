# Discovery Call Checklist

A discovery call is only as strong as the weakest thing you assumed instead of confirmed. This is how you close one out without leaving a gap.

## Learning Objective

- Run the checklist as a live diagnostic, not just an after-the-fact review
- Close specific gaps with one targeted question, not a full re-interview
- End every call with the candidate knowing exactly what happens next

## Business Importance

- A call that ends with an obvious gap ("wait, I forgot to ask about your visa") erodes trust built over the whole call
- Candidates can tell when a wrap-up is genuine versus mechanical box-ticking
- Unresolved authorization uncertainty at the end of a call is the single costliest thing to leave open
- A candidate who doesn't know what happens next won't show up to the next call
- This is your last real chance to catch what got missed

## Real Conversation

**Sales Executive:**

Before we wrap up — let me just check a couple of things. That okay?

**Candidate:**

Sure.

**Sales Executive:**

Is there anything about your work authorization that's still unclear to either of us?

Earlier you mentioned you weren't sure if you'd filed for the STEM extension.

**Candidate:**

Oh, right.

No, I still need to check on that. I don't actually know for sure.

**Sales Executive:**

Good that we caught that again — I don't want us moving forward on an assumption there.

I'll flag that as something to nail down before we go further.

Did I get a real sense of what you're actually looking for today? Or is there something I might have missed?

**Candidate:**

No, I think you got it.

The stability thing, more than the specific title.

**Sales Executive:**

Good.

Last thing — what's your understanding of what happens next?

**Candidate:**

I think...

You're going to follow up once I've confirmed my OPT status?

**Sales Executive:**

Exactly right.

Once that's clear, we'll pick this back up properly.

## Why Every Sentence Works

**"Before we wrap up — let me just check a couple of things. That okay?"**

Why?
A transition, not filler. Signals the call is entering a different mode, so the candidate shifts from open conversation to confirming specifics without feeling tested.

**"Is there anything about your work authorization that's still unclear to either of us?"**

Why?
"To either of us" matters — it's not accusing the candidate of hiding something, it's framing this as a shared gap to close together.

**"Good that we caught that again."**

Why?
Reframes catching a gap as a win, not a failure. A candidate who feels caught out gets defensive; a candidate who feels like a gap was "caught together" stays open.

**"Did I get a real sense of what you're actually looking for? Or is there something I might have missed?"**

Why?
Directly invites correction. Candidates will tell you what you missed if you ask plainly — this is more reliable than reviewing your own notes silently.

**"What's your understanding of what happens next?"**

Why?
Confirms alignment by making the candidate say it back, not just nod along. A call can go well in every other respect and still fail if the candidate hangs up unsure what's next.

## Common Mistakes

❌ Running the checklist mentally but never confirming gaps out loud
❌ Reading it like a rigid script instead of a natural conversation
❌ Ending a call with unresolved authorization because it felt awkward to reopen
❌ Assuming earlier silence on a topic meant nothing to add
❌ Rushing "what happens next" because the call already ran long

## Best Practices

- Run this checklist quietly throughout the call, not just at the end
- Always close any authorization uncertainty before ending the call
- Ask the candidate to state their understanding of next steps out loud
- Treat a fully checked item as still worth a quick re-check if something shifts later
- If a real gap exists, schedule a specific follow-up — don't leave it open

## Roleplay

### Roleplay 1 — Easy

**Scenario**
Everything on the call went cleanly — this is a straightforward confirmation pass.

**Candidate Profile**
Priya, gave clear, confident answers throughout the call, no ambiguity anywhere.

**Conversation**

**Sales Executive:**
Before we wrap up, let me just confirm a couple of things — that work?

**Candidate:**
Of course.

**Sales Executive:**
Is there anything about your authorization or background that's still unclear to either of us?

**Candidate:**
No, I think we covered everything.

**Decision Point**
Accept "we covered everything" at face value, or still run the full checklist?

**Best Response**
Run the full checklist anyway — quickly, but completely.

**Why**
Even a confident, easy candidate deserves the full close-out. "I think we covered everything" is a candidate's read, not a substitute for your own quick verification.

### Roleplay 2 — Medium

**Scenario**
Candidate's understanding of "what happens next" is slightly off from what was actually agreed.

**Candidate Profile**
Karan, engaged throughout the call, but misremembers a detail about the follow-up timing.

**Conversation**

**Sales Executive:**
What's your understanding of what happens next?

**Candidate:**
I think you'll call me back next month sometime?

**Sales Executive:**
Actually, let me be precise — I'll follow up in three days, once you've checked your OPT status. Not next month.

**Decision Point**
Let the small misunderstanding slide since he's engaged, or correct it precisely?

**Best Response**
Correct it precisely, right there, before the call ends.

**Why**
A vague or slightly wrong answer to "what happens next" means the alignment isn't real yet — even with an otherwise engaged candidate. Don't assume it landed just because you said it once earlier.

### Roleplay 3 — Difficult

**Scenario**
Candidate is rushing to end the call and tries to wave off an unresolved authorization gap.

**Candidate Profile**
Farhan, call has run long, candidate is now distracted and just wants to hang up, downplays the open authorization question.

**Conversation**

**Sales Executive:**
Before we finish — we still haven't nailed down your STEM extension status. Can we spend two more minutes on that?

**Candidate:**
Honestly it's probably fine, can we just figure that out later? I really have to go.

**Decision Point**
Let it go to respect his time, or hold the line on resolving it now?

**Best Response**
"I hear you, and I don't want to hold you — but this one specifically I can't leave open, it affects your whole timeline. Can I text you two questions right now instead, so we close it without needing more call time?"

**Why**
Running out of time is a reason to find a faster way to close the gap — not a reason to leave it open. Offering a lighter-weight way to resolve it (a text, not more call time) respects both the urgency of the gap and the candidate's real constraint.

## Practice Exercise

1. **Rewrite the opening** — Write your own version of the transition into wrap-up mode for a call that's run long.
2. **Respond to the objection** — Candidate says: "Can we just sort the visa thing out later?" Write your response.
3. **Continue the conversation** — Candidate gives a vague answer to "what happens next." Write the next 3 lines to get a precise one.

## Cheat Sheet

- Signal the transition into wrap-up mode clearly
- Always close authorization uncertainty before the call ends
- Ask "is there anything I might have missed?" directly
- Make the candidate state next steps back in their own words
- Never say "we're basically out of time, so let's just wrap up"
- Never say "I think we covered everything" without actually checking
- A rushed candidate still needs the gap closed — find a faster way, not a skipped one
- Run the checklist throughout the call, not only at the end
- A fully checked item can still shift — quick re-check if something changes
- Schedule a specific follow-up for any real gap, never leave it vague
