# Discovery Call Checklist

A discovery call is only as strong as the weakest thing you assumed instead of confirmed. This is how you close one out clean.

## Lesson Objective

- Run the checklist as a live diagnostic, not just an after-the-fact review
- Close specific gaps with one targeted question, not a full re-interview
- End every call with the candidate stating what happens next, in their own words

## Estimated Time

⏱ 5–7 minutes

## Conversation Timeline

| Time | Phase |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | Signal the transition to wrap-up |
| 0:15–0:45 | Authorization re-check — decision point |
| 0:45–1:10 | Goals confirmed in candidate's words |
| 1:10–1:30 | Next steps stated back by candidate |

## Full Conversation Viewer

**Sales Executive (Aayush):**

Before we wrap up — let me just check a couple of things. That okay?

**Candidate (Neha):**

Sure.

**Sales Executive:**

Is there anything about your work authorization that's still unclear to either of us?

Earlier you mentioned you weren't fully sure how your H4 EAD ties to your husband's renewal timeline.

**Candidate:**

Oh — right. No, I still need to check his exact renewal date. I don't know it off the top of my head.

## Decision Point

⏸ **PAUSE. What should Aayush say next?**

A. "No problem, we'll sort it out later."
B. "Good that we caught that again — I don't want us moving forward on an assumption there. I'll flag that as something to confirm before we go further."
C. "That's fine, it probably won't matter much anyway."

## Correct Answer

**B.**

**Sales Executive:**

Good that we caught that again — I don't want us moving forward on an assumption there.

I'll flag that as something to confirm before we go further.

Did I get a real sense of what you're actually looking for today? Or is there something I might have missed?

**Candidate:**

No, I think you got it — the stability thing, more than the specific title.

**Sales Executive:**

Good. Last thing — what's your understanding of what happens next?

**Candidate:**

I think... you'll follow up once I have my husband's renewal date confirmed?

**Sales Executive:**

Exactly right. Once that's clear, we'll pick this back up properly.

## Coaching Notes

- Don't let "I'll sort it out later" energy creep into your own tone — the gap still needs a name and an owner
- Ask "what's your understanding of what happens next" and actually wait for the full answer
- Keep this section brisk — it's a confirmation pass, not a new discovery round

## Why This Worked

- Reframes catching a gap as "good that we caught it" — teamwork, not a fault
- Directly invites correction instead of reviewing your own notes silently
- Makes the candidate say next steps back, not just nod along

## Common Mistakes

❌ "We'll sort it out later" — leaves a real gap open with no owner
❌ Downplaying an authorization gap as unlikely to matter
❌ Ending the call without the candidate stating next steps themselves
❌ Rushing the close because the call already ran long

## Better Alternative

Weaker response: *"That's fine, it probably won't matter much anyway."*

Why it's weaker: Authorization gaps are never "probably fine" — this is the one category of gap that can't be left to chance. Downplaying it here undoes the honesty this whole call was built on.

## Easy Roleplay

**Scenario:** Everything on the call went cleanly — straightforward confirmation pass.

**Candidate:** Emily — gave clear, confident answers throughout, no ambiguity anywhere.

**Conversation:**

**Sales Executive:** Before we wrap up, let me just confirm a couple of things — that work?

**Emily:** Of course.

**Sales Executive:** Is there anything about your background that's still unclear to either of us?

**Emily:** No, I think we covered everything.

**Decision Point:** Accept "we covered everything," or still run the full checklist?

**Best Response:** Run the full checklist anyway — quickly, but completely.

**Why:** "I think we covered everything" is the candidate's read, not a substitute for your own quick verification.

## Medium Roleplay

**Scenario:** Candidate's understanding of "what happens next" is slightly off.

**Candidate:** Rahul — engaged throughout, but misremembers the follow-up timing.

**Conversation:**

**Sales Executive:** What's your understanding of what happens next?

**Rahul:** I think you'll call me back next month sometime?

**Decision Point:** Let the small misunderstanding slide since he's engaged, or correct it precisely?

**Best Response:** "Actually, let me be precise — I'll follow up in three days, once you've checked your STEM extension status. Not next month."

**Why:** A vague or wrong answer to "what happens next" means alignment isn't real yet, even with an engaged candidate.

## Difficult Roleplay

**Scenario:** Candidate is rushing to end the call and tries to wave off an unresolved gap.

**Candidate:** Akash — call has run long, distracted, wants to hang up, downplays an open question.

**Conversation:**

**Sales Executive:** Before we finish — we still haven't confirmed your notice period. Can we spend two more minutes on that?

**Akash:** Honestly it's probably fine, can we just figure that out later? I really have to go.

**Decision Point:** Let it go to respect his time, or hold the line on resolving it now?

**Best Response:** "I hear you, and I don't want to hold you — but this one I can't leave open, it affects your timeline. Can I text you two questions right now instead, so we close it without needing more call time?"

**Why:** Running out of time is a reason to find a faster way to close the gap — not a reason to leave it open.

|---|---|---|
| Wrap-up signaled clearly | Transition named, not abrupt | |
| Authorization re-checked | No open gap left unnamed | |
| Goals confirmed in candidate's words | Not just advisor's own summary | |
| Next steps stated by candidate | Not just advisor announcing them | |
| Time pressure handled | Gap closed fast, not skipped | |

## Quick Reference Cheat Sheet

- Signal the transition into wrap-up mode clearly
- Always close authorization uncertainty before the call ends
- Ask "is there anything I might have missed?" directly
- Make the candidate state next steps back in their own words
- Never say "we're basically out of time, so let's just wrap up"
- Never say "I think we covered everything" without actually checking
- A rushed candidate still needs the gap closed — find a faster way, not a skipped one
- Schedule a specific follow-up for any real gap, never leave it vague
