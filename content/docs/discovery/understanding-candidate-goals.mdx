# Understanding Candidate Goals

"I want to get into cloud" is not a goal. It's the first sentence of one. Find out what's underneath it before you build anything on top — especially when the candidate's situation has changed since you last spoke.

## Lesson Objective

- Tell the difference between a stated goal and the real need driving it
- Force prioritization when a candidate names several goals at once
- Notice when a re-engaged candidate's goal has actually shifted since the last conversation

## Coaching Notes

<CoachingNotes title="Coaching Notes">
Don't validate the surface goal before testing what's underneath it, especially with a re-engaged candidate whose circumstances have shifted since the last conversation.
</CoachingNotes>

<CoachingNotes title="Why This Worked">
"Why that, specifically" is where a rehearsed answer cracks open. Forcing a single priority surfaced what actually changed for Akash since he last spoke to Aayush — the layoff, not the industry trend.
</CoachingNotes>
