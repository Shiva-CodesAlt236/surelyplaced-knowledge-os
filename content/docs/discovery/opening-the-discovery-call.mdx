# Opening the Discovery Call

The first 60 seconds decide whether this candidate tells you the truth for the next 20 minutes, or just tells you what they think you want to hear. Get this right and everything after it gets easier.

## Learning Objective

- Open a call so the candidate relaxes instead of bracing for a pitch
- Get permission before you take control of the conversation
- Ask one question that tells you why they actually called

## Business Importance

- A guarded candidate gives short, safe answers — you can't qualify or recommend anything off that
- Candidates who've been burned before are testing you in the first 60 seconds, whether they say so or not
- A bad opening costs you the whole call, not just the first minute
- Surely Placed doesn't guarantee placement — the opening has to feel honest, not just sound honest
- First impressions here directly decide show-up rates for the next call

## Real Conversation

**Sales Executive:**

Good afternoon, Rahul.

This is Aayush, calling from SurelyPlaced.

Did I catch you at a good time?

**Candidate:**

Yeah, yeah, I have some time.

**Sales Executive:**

Perfect.

Before I say anything about us —

I just want to tell you how I'd like to run this call. That okay?

**Candidate:**

Sure, go ahead.

**Sales Executive:**

So honestly, my job today is not to convince you of anything.

It's to actually understand your situation.

By the end, we'll both know if this even makes sense for you.

If it doesn't — I'll tell you straight. No sugar-coating.

**Candidate:**

Okay... that's actually different. Usually people just start pitching.

**Sales Executive:**

Ha, yeah, I get that a lot.

So let's start there — what made you reach out to us, Rahul? Instead of just applying on your own?

**Candidate:**

See honestly...

I've been applying since like four months. LinkedIn, Indeed, everywhere.

Maybe two, three calls total. Nothing serious.

I saw your post, thought let me just try, what's the harm.

**Sales Executive:**

Four months and almost nothing back — that's genuinely frustrating, I won't pretend it isn't.

Have you spoken to any other consultancy or program before this?

**Candidate:**

Ya actually, one place, last month.

But they were like... "sign up today, seats filling fast" type thing.

Didn't even ask me what my background is.

**Sales Executive:**

Got it. I'll do the complete opposite of that today.

So before I say one word about what we do —

tell me what's actually going on with your search right now.

**Candidate:**

Okay so basically...

## Why Every Sentence Works

**"Did I catch you at a good time?"**

Why?
Gets permission. Removes pressure. Candidate answers "yes," and now they've made a small commitment to actually be present.

**"I just want to tell you how I'd like to run this call. That okay?"**

Why?
This is not really a question — it's a heads-up dressed as a question. It hands the candidate a small piece of control before you take any.

**"My job today is not to convince you of anything."**

Why?
Directly kills the candidate's default prediction — *this is going to be a pitch*. Say it early or they'll spend the whole call waiting for it anyway.

**"If it doesn't — I'll tell you straight."**

Why?
Proof, not a promise. Anyone can say "we're honest." Saying you'll tell them "no" if it's a no is the only version of that claim that actually means something.

**"What made you reach out to us? Instead of just applying on your own?"**

Why?
The real trigger tells you everything — urgency, patience level, what's actually at stake. "I want a better job" and "I got laid off three weeks ago" are two completely different calls.

**"Have you spoken to any other consultancy or program before this?"**

Why?
Tells you what you're being compared to. And naming, out loud, that you know this space has people who oversell — before the candidate even brings it up — is worth more than almost anything else you can say early.

## Common Mistakes

❌ Talking too much in the first two minutes
❌ Pitching before asking a single real question
❌ Asking three questions back to back without letting an answer breathe
❌ Ignoring "nothing serious" or "didn't go anywhere" — that's a wound, not small talk
❌ Using the exact same opening on a rushed candidate as a relaxed one

## Best Practices

- Ask permission before you set the agenda
- Get to "why did you actually call" inside the first two minutes
- Name the competitor comparison before the candidate has to
- Match their energy — slow down an eager candidate, don't rush a guarded one
- Never promise an outcome in the opening. Ever.

## Roleplay

### Roleplay 1 — Easy

**Scenario**
Candidate applied directly on the website, responded to your call on the first ring, sounds relaxed.

**Candidate Profile**
Priya, 26, laid off two months ago from a QA role in Pune, actively job searching, no prior consultancy experience.

**Conversation**

**Sales Executive:**
Hi Priya, this is Neha from SurelyPlaced, is now an okay time?

**Candidate:**
Yes, perfect actually, I'm free.

**Sales Executive:**
Great — quick heads up on how I like to run these calls, that fine?

**Candidate:**
Sure sure, go ahead.

**Sales Executive:**
So today I'm not here to sell you anything. I want to actually understand your situation, and by the end we'll both know if this makes sense. What made you reach out to us?

**Candidate:**
Honestly I just saw the ad and it looked genuine, so I thought let me try.

**Decision Point**
Do you accept "it looked genuine" as enough, or dig one layer deeper?

**Best Response**
"Genuine is good to hear — but what's actually going on with your search right now? Applying much?"

**Why**
An easy candidate will still only give you the surface answer unless you ask a second time in a different way. One extra question here costs nothing and gets you the real trigger.

### Roleplay 2 — Medium

**Scenario**
Candidate picks up sounding distracted, gives one-word answers at first.

**Candidate Profile**
Vikram, 31, currently employed but unhappy, exploring quietly, worried about being overheard at work.

**Conversation**

**Sales Executive:**
Hi Vikram, Aayush calling from SurelyPlaced — good time to talk?

**Candidate:**
Uh — kind of. Two minutes maybe.

**Sales Executive:**
No problem, I'll keep it tight. Quick context on how I run these calls — that okay?

**Candidate:**
Yeah fine, go.

**Sales Executive:**
I'm not here to pitch you. I want to understand your situation, that's it. What made you reach out?

**Candidate:**
I mean... I'm still working right now, just looking around. Can't really talk long, I'm at my desk.

**Decision Point**
Push forward with your normal opening, or adjust to the two-minute window he just gave you?

**Best Response**
"Got it — let's not do this properly right now then. Can we grab 15 minutes this evening when you're not at your desk? I'd rather actually hear you out than rush it."

**Why**
Ignoring a stated constraint to get through your script is exactly the mistake this lesson warns against. Respecting "I'm at my desk" earns more trust than any opening line could.

### Roleplay 3 — Difficult

**Scenario**
Candidate answers annoyed, has clearly been called by multiple agencies this week already.

**Candidate Profile**
Farhan, 29, OPT holder, been contacted by three different consultancies in the past ten days, defensive from the first word.

**Conversation**

**Sales Executive:**
Hi Farhan, this is Rohan from SurelyPlaced, good time?

**Candidate:**
Ugh — another one? Look, I've had like three of these calls already this week.

**Sales Executive:**
Yeah, I believe that, and I get why you're tired of it.

**Candidate:**
So what's different about you guys?

**Sales Executive:**
Honestly? Maybe nothing, until you actually talk to me. I'm not going to tell you we're different — I'd rather show you in the next two minutes, and you decide. Fair?

**Candidate:**
...Fine. Two minutes.

**Sales Executive:**
Deal. So instead of me pitching — tell me what those other three calls got wrong, so I don't repeat it.

**Decision Point**
Defend SurelyPlaced against the other agencies, or let the candidate vent first?

**Best Response**
Let him vent first. Ask what went wrong with the others before saying anything about your own program.

**Why**
An annoyed candidate doesn't want to hear why you're different — they want to be heard first. Asking what went wrong elsewhere does two things: it gets you real competitive intel, and it proves you're actually listening instead of just waiting for your turn to talk.

## Cheat Sheet

- Ask permission before setting the agenda
- State plainly: you're not here to convince them
- Promise to say "no" if it's a no — then actually do it
- Ask "what made you reach out" in the first two minutes
- Ask about prior consultancy experience before they bring it up
- Match their energy — don't run the same opening on everyone
- Never say "this will only take a few minutes" if it won't
- Never say "I already know what you need"
- Never imply an outcome is guaranteed
- If they name a constraint (time, privacy), respect it immediately
