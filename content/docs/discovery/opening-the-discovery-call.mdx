# Opening the Discovery Call

The first 60 seconds decide if this candidate tells you the truth for the next 20 minutes — or just tells you what they think you want to hear.

## Lesson Objective

- Open a call so the candidate relaxes instead of bracing for a pitch
- Get permission before you take control of the conversation
- Ask one question that surfaces why they actually called

## Estimated Time

⏱ 8–10 minutes

## Conversation Timeline

| Time | Phase |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | Greeting + permission check |
| 0:15–0:35 | Agenda framing — "not here to convince you" |
| 0:35–1:00 | Trigger question — why did you actually call |
| 1:00–1:45 | Prior-experience check + decision point |

## Full Conversation Viewer

**Sales Executive (Aayush):**

Good afternoon, Rahul.

This is Aayush, calling from SurelyPlaced.

Did I catch you at a good time?

**Candidate (Rahul):**

Yeah, yeah, I have some time.

**Sales Executive:**

Perfect.

Before I say anything about us —

I just want to tell you how I'd like to run this call. That okay?

**Candidate:**

Sure, go ahead.

**Sales Executive:**

So honestly, my job today is not to convince you of anything.

It's to actually understand your situation.

By the end, we'll both know if this even makes sense for you.

If it doesn't — I'll tell you straight. No sugar-coating.

**Candidate:**

Okay... that's actually different. Usually people just start pitching.

**Sales Executive:**

Ha, yeah, I get that a lot.

So let's start there — what made you reach out to us, Rahul? Instead of just applying on your own?

**Candidate:**

See honestly...

I've been applying since like four months. LinkedIn, Indeed, everywhere.

Maybe two, three calls total. Nothing serious.

I saw your post, thought let me just try, what's the harm.

**Sales Executive:**

Four months and almost nothing back — that's genuinely frustrating, I won't pretend it isn't.

Have you spoken to any other consultancy or program before this?

**Candidate:**

Ya actually, one place, last month.

But they were like... "sign up today, seats filling fast" type thing.

Didn't even ask me what my background is.

## Decision Point

⏸ **PAUSE. What should Aayush say next?**

A. "Don't worry, we're nothing like that."
B. "Got it. I'll do the complete opposite of that today — before I say one word about what we do, tell me what's actually going on with your search right now."
C. "That's unfortunate. So let me tell you what makes SurelyPlaced different."

## Correct Answer

**B.**

**Sales Executive:**

Got it. I'll do the complete opposite of that today.

So before I say one word about what we do —

tell me what's actually going on with your search right now.

## Coaching Notes

- Say this at normal pace — rushing it undoes the calm you just built
- Let "what's actually going on" land as a genuine open question, not a transition line
- Don't smile-talk past "nothing serious" — that phrase is doing a lot of hiding

## Why This Worked

- Names the competitor's mistake without trashing them by name
- Promises different behavior instead of just claiming to be different
- Hands the floor back to the candidate before any pitch happens

## Common Mistakes

❌ "Don't worry, we're nothing like that" — a claim, not proof
❌ Defending SurelyPlaced before the candidate finished venting
❌ Jumping straight into program details after "nothing serious"
❌ Talking over the pause after "what's the harm"

## Better Alternative

Weaker response: *"That's unfortunate. So let me tell you what makes SurelyPlaced different."*

Why it's weaker: Starts the pitch exactly one sentence too early. The candidate just told you what he's tired of — being pitched at without being asked anything first. This response repeats the pattern he just complained about.

## Easy Roleplay

**Scenario:** Candidate applied directly on the website, picks up on the first ring, sounds relaxed.

**Candidate:** Emily — US Citizen, no visa complexity, browsing options casually.

**Conversation:**

**Sales Executive:** Hi Emily, this is Aayush from SurelyPlaced — is now an okay time?

**Emily:** Yes, perfect, I'm free.

**Sales Executive:** Great — quick heads up on how I like to run these calls, that fine?

**Emily:** Sure, go ahead.

**Sales Executive:** I'm not here to sell you anything today. I want to understand your situation, and by the end we'll both know if this makes sense. What made you reach out?

**Emily:** Honestly the ad looked genuine, so I thought I'd try it.

**Decision Point:** Accept "it looked genuine" as enough, or dig one layer deeper?

**Best Response:** "Genuine is good to hear — but what's actually going on with your search right now?"

**Why:** Even an easy, relaxed candidate only gives the surface answer unless asked twice, differently. One extra question costs nothing.

## Medium Roleplay

**Scenario:** Candidate picks up distracted, gives short answers, clearly at work.

**Candidate:** Priya — H1B, QA Engineer, exploring quietly during work hours.

**Conversation:**

**Sales Executive:** Hi Priya, Aayush calling from SurelyPlaced — good time to talk?

**Priya:** Uh — kind of. Two minutes maybe, I'm at my desk.

**Sales Executive:** No problem, I'll keep it tight. I'm not here to pitch you — just want to understand your situation. What made you reach out?

**Priya:** I mean, I'm employed right now, just quietly looking. Can't really talk long here.

**Decision Point:** Push through your normal opening, or adjust to her stated constraint?

**Best Response:** "Got it — let's not do this properly right now then. Can we grab 15 minutes this evening when you're not at your desk?"

**Why:** Ignoring a stated time constraint to finish your script is the exact mistake this lesson warns against.

## Difficult Roleplay

**Scenario:** Candidate answers annoyed — third agency call this week.

**Candidate:** Akash — recently laid off, defensive, fatigued from repeated cold calls.

**Conversation:**

**Sales Executive:** Hi Akash, this is Aayush from SurelyPlaced, good time?

**Akash:** Ugh — another one? I've had like three of these calls already this week.

**Sales Executive:** Yeah, I believe that, and I get why you're tired of it.

**Akash:** So what's different about you guys?

**Decision Point:** Defend SurelyPlaced directly, or let him vent first?

**Best Response:** "Honestly? Maybe nothing, until you actually talk to me. I'd rather show you than tell you — instead of pitching, tell me what those other three calls got wrong, so I don't repeat it."

**Why:** An annoyed candidate wants to be heard before he wants to be convinced. Asking what went wrong elsewhere gets real intel and proves you're listening.

## Quick Reference Cheat Sheet

- Ask permission before setting the agenda
- State plainly: not here to convince them
- Promise to say "no" if it's a no — then do it
- Ask "what made you reach out" in the first 2 minutes
- Ask about prior consultancy experience before they bring it up
- Match their energy — don't run the same opening on everyone
- Never say "this will only take a few minutes" if it won't
- Never say "I already know what you need"
- Respect a stated constraint (time, privacy) immediately
