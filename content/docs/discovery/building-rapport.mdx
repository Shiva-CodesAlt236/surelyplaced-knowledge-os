# Building Rapport

Rapport isn't small talk. It's what happens when a candidate says something a little vulnerable, and watches how you react.

## Lesson Objective

- Respond to an answer in a way that proves you were listening
- Use silence on purpose instead of filling it out of habit
- Notice the exact moment a candidate's answers start getting shorter

## Estimated Time

⏱ 7–9 minutes

## Conversation Timeline

| Time | Phase |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Candidate discloses something vulnerable, downplays it |
| 0:20–0:50 | Advisor decision point — acknowledge or move on |
| 0:50–1:30 | Depth question, real answer surfaces |
| 1:30–1:50 | Reflect back, confirm read |

## Full Conversation Viewer

**Candidate (Neha):**

Yeah, so... it's a bit complicated. I'm on H4 EAD.

My husband's on H1B, so my status depends on his, kind of.

It's fine, it's manageable.

**Sales Executive (Aayush):**

Can you say more about that?

I don't want to just note it and move on if there's more to it.

**Candidate:**

I mean...

It's just, every time his H1B renewal comes up, I get nervous too. Even though it's his process, not mine.

It's exhausting honestly, always feeling like a plus-one on my own work authorization.

## Decision Point

⏸ **PAUSE. What should Aayush say next?**

A. "Don't worry, H4 EAD is pretty stable these days."
B. "That makes sense — depending on someone else's renewal for your own status sounds exhausting in a way that doesn't really go away. What's that actually been like for you day to day?"
C. "Okay, noted. So, moving on — what's your IT background?"

## Correct Answer

**B.**

**Sales Executive:**

That makes sense.

Depending on someone else's renewal for your own status — that sounds exhausting in a way that doesn't really go away.

What's that actually been like for you, day to day?

**Candidate:**

Honestly? I second-guess every career decision through that lens. Like, "will this even matter if his status changes."

**Sales Executive:**

That's a really common feeling among H4 EAD candidates, and it's useful for me to know — because part of today is figuring out what's actually in your control, separate from what depends on his timeline.

**Candidate:**

Yeah... yeah, nobody's actually said that to me before.

## Coaching Notes

- Don't reassure with facts ("H4 EAD is stable") before you've acknowledged the feeling
- Let "it's fine, it's manageable" register as a downplay, not a closed topic
- Slow your pace here — this is not a moment to move quickly through

## Why This Worked

- "Can you say more about that" costs nothing and signals the answer mattered
- Turns a fact (H4 EAD status) into a story (what it's actually like)
- Names the pattern as common without minimizing her specific experience

## Common Mistakes

❌ Reassuring with facts before acknowledging the feeling
❌ Moving straight to the next scripted question after a disclosure
❌ Saying "I totally understand exactly how you feel"
❌ Comparing her to another candidate's H4 situation

## Better Alternative

Weaker response: *"Okay, noted. So, moving on — what's your IT background?"*

Why it's weaker: Logs the disclosure like a form field instead of a real moment. Neha will notice the shift instantly, and her answers get shorter for the rest of the call.

## Easy Roleplay

**Scenario:** Candidate opens up without much prompting, naturally talkative.

**Candidate:** Rahul — F1 OPT, nervous but forthcoming.

**Conversation:**

**Sales Executive:** What's been the hardest part of the search so far?

**Rahul:** Honestly the silence. I apply and then nothing. Not even a rejection email half the time.

**Decision Point:** Move to the next question, or sit with this one beat longer?

**Best Response:** "That silence is honestly one of the worst parts of this whole process. What's that been like, day to day?"

**Why:** Even a talkative candidate deserves one beat of acknowledgment before you move forward.

## Medium Roleplay

**Scenario:** Candidate gives a short, guarded answer after opening up earlier in the call.

**Candidate:** Emily — mentioned a difficult family situation earlier, has gone quieter since.

**Conversation:**

**Sales Executive:** Going back to timelines — how soon are you hoping to make a move?

**Emily:** I don't know. Soon I guess.

**Decision Point:** Take the short answer at face value, or check in on the shift?

**Best Response:** "I want to check in — is this pace working for you, or would it help to slow down a bit?"

**Why:** The answer got shorter — that's the actual signal, more than tone of voice.

## Difficult Roleplay

**Scenario:** Candidate shuts a sensitive topic down entirely, turns cold.

**Candidate:** Akash — mentioned being laid off, then refuses to elaborate further.

**Conversation:**

**Sales Executive:** You mentioned the layoff earlier — is that shaping your timeline at all?

**Akash:** I'd rather not get into that, honestly.

**Sales Executive:** Of course — I hear you, we don't need to.

**Akash:** ...Okay. Thanks.

**Decision Point:** Circle back later "just to be sure," or drop it completely?

**Best Response:** Drop it completely. Don't return to it unless he brings it up himself.

**Why:** Respecting a stated boundary fully — not "for now" — is what actually rebuilds trust here.

## Practice Exercise

1. A candidate mentions a rough patch at home. Write your next line.
2. Candidate says: "Why does this even matter for a job search?" after you ask what a layoff was like. Write your response.
3. Candidate gives a one-word answer ("Fine.") to a question that deserves more. Write your next 3 lines.

## Manager Review Scorecard

| Criteria | What to Listen For | Score (1–5) |
|---|---|---|
| Acknowledgment before moving on | Named the disclosure, didn't skip it | |
| Depth question used | "What was that actually like" or equivalent | |
| Boundary respected | Dropped topic fully when declined | |
| Silence tolerance | Let a pause sit before filling it | |
| Rapport-slip awareness | Noticed and named shorter answers | |

## Quick Reference Cheat Sheet

- Acknowledge before you move forward, every time
- "Can you say more about that?" works almost everywhere
- Ask what something was "actually like," not just what happened
- Offer an exit from sensitive topics — don't force depth
- Shorter answers mid-call = rapport slipping, check in
- Never say "I totally understand exactly how you feel"
- Never compare one candidate's situation to another's
- If they decline to elaborate, drop it completely
