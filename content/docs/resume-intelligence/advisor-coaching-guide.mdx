# Advisor Coaching Guide

> This title appears in two other modules with two different meanings. [Sales Coaching's Coach Review Framework](/docs/sales-coaching/coach-review-framework) covers reviewing an advisor's own sales-call skill. [Interview Intelligence's Advisor Coaching Guide](/docs/interview-intelligence/advisor-coaching-guide) covers coaching a candidate ahead of an employer interview. This article covers a distinct, earlier conversation: coaching a candidate through their resume.

## Purpose

Everything the rest of this module establishes about reading and framing a resume becomes useful only once it's put into an actual conversation with a candidate. This article covers how to run that conversation constructively.

## Scope

This article covers the advisor's own conversational approach to a resume-coaching conversation. It does not cover editing or producing the document itself, and it does not describe a specific resume-review program, turnaround commitment, or session structure this repository doesn't define.

## Core Content

Open by asking the candidate to walk through their own resume before offering any observation of your own. A candidate's own explanation often surfaces the narrative — or the gap in one — faster than an advisor's outside read would, and starting this way keeps the conversation collaborative rather than corrective from the first sentence.

When raising an observation from [Resume Reading Framework](/docs/resume-intelligence/resume-reading-framework), lead with what's already working before what needs attention — a resume conversation that opens entirely with problems tends to make a candidate defensive rather than receptive, even when every point raised is fair.

When a red flag from [Resume Red Flags](/docs/resume-intelligence/resume-red-flags) needs to be raised, do it directly and without alarm — most turn out to have an ordinary explanation, and treating every one as a serious concern erodes trust faster than a calm, direct question would.

Close by confirming the candidate can now state their own narrative clearly — per [Resume Storytelling](/docs/resume-intelligence/resume-storytelling) — rather than simply confirming the document itself looks better. The goal of this conversation is a candidate who can speak to their own background confidently, not just a resume that reads well.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This conversation precedes and directly sets up the work [Interview Intelligence's Advisor Coaching Guide](/docs/interview-intelligence/advisor-coaching-guide) covers for the interview stage itself, and follows the same constructive, non-evaluative tone [Coach Review Framework](/docs/sales-coaching/coach-review-framework) established for its own, differently scoped coaching conversation.

## Advisor Guidance

Lead with the candidate's own explanation, balance any critique with what's already working, raise red flags calmly rather than urgently, and close by confirming the candidate can articulate their own story — not just that the document looks polished.

Run the resume coaching conversation candidate-led and constructive — their explanation first, balanced observations, calm handling of any red flag, and a close that confirms the candidate can state their own narrative, not just that the page itself looks better.
