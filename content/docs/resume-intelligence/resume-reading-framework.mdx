# Resume Reading Framework

## Purpose

Every other article in this module assumes an advisor is already reading a resume the same consistent way. This article defines that approach so the format-specific and audience-specific articles that follow can build on it rather than each reinventing it.

## Scope

This framework covers how to read a resume before a coaching conversation happens — not how to write one, edit one, or produce a finished document on a candidate's behalf. It's a lens for noticing what's there and what's missing, applied by the advisor privately before the conversation, not a script recited to the candidate.

## Core Content

**Structure first.** Before reading a single line of content, notice the shape: is the layout easy to scan in under a minute, is the most relevant experience near the top, and is there a clear, logical order to how roles and skills are presented? A resume that's hard to scan quickly is worth flagging regardless of how strong the underlying experience is.

**Substance second.** Read each role for what it actually demonstrates — specific responsibilities, concrete outcomes, technologies or tools genuinely used — rather than generic duty statements that could describe almost anyone in that title. Vague language is usually a sign the candidate hasn't yet connected their own experience to a clear narrative, not evidence the experience itself is weak.

**Signal third.** Step back and ask what a reader unfamiliar with the candidate would conclude about their trajectory, seniority, and focus area within the first thirty seconds. If that impression doesn't match what the advisor already knows about the candidate from prior conversations, that gap is the most useful thing to raise directly.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This three-part approach mirrors the way [Interview Preparation Framework](/docs/interview-intelligence/interview-preparation-framework) structures its own shared approach for a different stage of the same candidate relationship, and draws its raw material from the same [Career Background](/docs/candidate-intelligence/reference-profile/career-background) and [Technology Profile](/docs/candidate-intelligence/reference-profile/technology-profile) illustrative layers already established in the Candidate Intelligence Framework.

## Advisor Guidance

Apply all three passes before saying anything to the candidate about their resume — a reaction formed after only the first pass tends to focus on cosmetic issues at the expense of substance. Keep the tone constructive throughout; a resume conversation is diagnostic, not evaluative of the person.

## Key Takeaways

Read a resume in three passes — structure, substance, then signal — before discussing it with a candidate, and treat any gap between the resume's overall impression and what the advisor already knows about the candidate as the most useful thing to raise in that conversation.
