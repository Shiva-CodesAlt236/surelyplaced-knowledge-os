# International Student Resumes

> This article covers resume formatting and framing conventions only. It does not state or interpret any work authorization rule, category requirement, or eligibility determination — those questions always route to [Visa Discovery Framework](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-discovery-framework) and, where genuinely uncertain, to qualified immigration counsel.

## Purpose

A candidate building a resume around an international academic background — often limited US, Canadian, or UK work history alongside strong academic credentials — faces framing questions a more conventional resume doesn't raise. This article gives advisors a consistent, non-legal way to help with that framing.

## Scope

This article addresses how academic projects, coursework, research, and limited work history are commonly presented — not whether a candidate is eligible to work under a specific visa category, which this repository never determines. Any authorization-specific question is routed per [Visa Discovery Framework](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-discovery-framework) rather than answered here.

## Core Content

Academic and research experience often carries real weight when a candidate's formal work history is limited by visa timing rather than by choice, and a resume can present coursework projects, research assistantships, and academic publications using the same outcome-focused language a work-history bullet would use — what was built, what problem it solved, what the candidate's specific role was.

Where a resume needs to reflect a candidate's current work authorization status, the specific wording of that statement is a factual and often legally sensitive detail this repository doesn't define — an advisor helping with resume framing should not draft or approve that specific language and should instead point the candidate to the latest approved internal process or appropriate legal guidance.

Employment gaps that trace to visa processing timelines, program transitions, or authorization renewal periods are common in this population and aren't inherently something to disguise — see [Resume Red Flags](/docs/resume-intelligence/resume-red-flags) for the distinction between a gap worth a conversation and a gap that's simply ordinary.

## Advisor Guidance

Help with framing and presentation confidently; defer anything touching authorization status, eligibility, or legally sensitive wording to the appropriate resource every time, without exception. A candidate's academic and research background is often a genuine strength worth presenting clearly, not a gap to apologize for.

Academic and research experience can be framed with the same outcome-focused language as work history, employment gaps tied to visa timing are usually ordinary rather than concerning, and any question touching authorization status or eligibility routes to Visa Discovery Framework rather than being answered as a resume-formatting question.
