# ATS-Friendly Resumes

> **Illustrative Only:** This article describes general applicant tracking system behavior at a conceptual level. It does not name, endorse, or compare specific ATS products, and it does not claim to know how any particular employer's system is configured.

## Purpose

Candidates frequently ask whether their resume will "get through the system," and an advisor needs a way to answer that's honest about the real uncertainty involved rather than offering false confidence about a process the advisor can't see into.

## Scope

This article covers formatting and structural choices that generally support machine readability, in broad terms. It does not claim a specific format guarantees passage through any system, does not name or compare ATS vendors, and does not treat parsing behavior as uniform across employers — it varies, and this article says so throughout.

## Core Content

Applicant tracking systems generally parse resumes for text content, so a resume built primarily from images, complex multi-column layouts, or embedded graphics for core content risks losing information a simpler layout would preserve. A conventional single-column structure with clearly labeled sections tends to be more reliably read than a heavily designed layout, though exactly how any given system handles a specific format isn't something this article — or the advisor — can verify.

Standard section headings ("Experience," "Education," "Skills") are generally recognized more reliably than creative alternatives. Beyond that general principle, this article does not prescribe an exact template, since format effectiveness depends on factors — the specific system, the specific employer's configuration — that vary and aren't documented anywhere in this repository.

## Advisor Guidance

Be direct with candidates that no one, including the advisor, can predict exactly how a specific employer's system will process a given resume — general formatting principles reduce risk, they don't eliminate it. Avoid any language that implies a guaranteed pass-through, and never cite a specific pass-rate or statistic, since none is documented here.
