# Resume Red Flags

## Purpose

Not everything unusual in a resume is a problem, and not everything that looks minor is safe to wave past. This article separates the signals genuinely worth a careful conversation or an escalation from the ones an advisor can address with routine coaching.

## Scope

This article covers what to notice in a resume's content and how to respond — it does not make a legal, immigration, or employment-history determination on the advisor's own authority. Anything touching work authorization or a candidate's factual employment history beyond ordinary formatting judgment gets routed onward rather than resolved in the conversation itself.

## Core Content

**Worth a careful, direct conversation:**
- Unexplained gaps of substantial length between roles, which are often ordinary but are worth understanding rather than ignoring.
- Job titles or responsibilities that shift dramatically and inconsistently between what the resume states and what the candidate has described verbally.
- Dates that don't align internally — overlapping roles presented as sequential, or a graduation date inconsistent with the work history around it.

**Worth routing rather than resolving directly:**
- Anything touching work authorization status, visa category, or eligibility to work for a specific employer type — this belongs with [Visa Red Flags](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-red-flags) and qualified immigration counsel, never an advisor's own interpretation.
- A candidate asking whether it's acceptable to omit or reword something in a way that could mislead an employer — this is a conversation about honesty and judgment, not resume formatting, and connects to the standard already set in [Ethical Selling](/docs/sales-constitution/ethical-selling).

**Not actually a red flag:**
- A non-linear career path, a career change, or a resume that doesn't follow a conventional single-track trajectory. Variation in background is common and isn't itself a credibility problem.
- Formatting choices that differ from a specific template preference — this repository doesn't define one correct resume format, and treating a stylistic choice as a defect risks discouraging a candidate over something that doesn't actually matter.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article follows the same escalate-rather-than-resolve discipline [Interview Red Flags](/docs/interview-intelligence/interview-red-flags) and [Visa Red Flags](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-red-flags) already establish for their own stages, applied here to what surfaces specifically while reading a resume.

## Advisor Guidance

When something in the first two categories comes up, ask about it directly and calmly rather than assuming the worst — most gaps and inconsistencies have an ordinary explanation the candidate can supply immediately. When something in the second category comes up, pause and route it; guessing at an answer helps no one.

## Key Takeaways

Most unusual resume signals deserve a direct, calm conversation rather than concern, a smaller set touching work authorization or honesty must be routed rather than resolved by the advisor alone, and a genuinely non-linear career path or unconventional formatting choice isn't a red flag at all.
