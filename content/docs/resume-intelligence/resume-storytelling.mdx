# Resume Storytelling

## Purpose

A resume that lists accurate facts can still fail to tell a coherent story — and a candidate who can't articulate their own throughline struggles in exactly the conversations, like interviews, where that throughline matters most. This article covers how an advisor helps a candidate find and express that connective narrative, grounded strictly in what's already true.

## Scope

This article covers narrative framing of existing, factual experience — never inventing an achievement, responsibility, or motivation the candidate didn't actually have. The line between framing and fabrication matters throughout: framing chooses which true things to emphasize and how to connect them; fabrication adds things that aren't true. This article only ever means the former.

## Core Content

Ask what actually connects the roles on the resume, rather than assuming a single tidy theme exists. Sometimes the throughline is a consistent technical focus; sometimes it's a recurring type of problem the candidate keeps choosing to solve; sometimes it's a skill the candidate has been deliberately building across otherwise different roles. The advisor's job is to help the candidate notice which of these, if any, is genuinely present — not to impose one that isn't.

Once a real throughline is identified, help the candidate practice stating it in a single sentence they could say out loud in a conversation. A resume can imply a narrative through ordering and emphasis; a candidate needs to be able to say it directly and naturally when asked, which the resume itself won't do for them.

Where a candidate's path genuinely doesn't have a single clean throughline — a career change, a return after a gap, a shift between fields — the honest framing is to acknowledge the shift directly and explain the reasoning behind it, rather than forcing a false continuity onto a story that doesn't have one. An honest, well-explained pivot generally lands better than a strained attempt to make an inconsistent history look seamless.

## Advisor Guidance

Help a candidate find a narrative that's genuinely true of their experience, and be equally comfortable helping them articulate an honest pivot when no single throughline exists. Never suggest framing that implies an achievement or responsibility beyond what the underlying facts support.
