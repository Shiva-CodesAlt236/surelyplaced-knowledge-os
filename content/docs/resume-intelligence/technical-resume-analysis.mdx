# Technical Resume Analysis

## Purpose

A resume listing technical experience raises a specific temptation: assessing whether the candidate is actually skilled. That judgment isn't the advisor's to make. This article covers what an advisor can responsibly evaluate instead — clarity and credibility of presentation — while staying out of technical assessment entirely.

## Scope

This article covers how technical experience is described on a resume, not whether the underlying skill claims are accurate or sufficient for a given role. Any question requiring genuine technical judgment is routed to an internal subject matter expert, consistent with the standard already set for technical content elsewhere in this repository.

## Core Content

Look for specificity over generality: a line naming the actual technologies, tools, or systems used in a role reads as more credible than a broad claim like "extensive technical experience" with nothing underneath it. This is a presentation judgment, not a technical one — the advisor isn't verifying the technology was used correctly, only noticing whether the resume gives a reader enough to form an impression at all.

Look for consistency between the technical details on the resume and what the candidate has said in conversation. A resume that lists a technology never mentioned in discovery, or omits one the candidate discussed at length, is worth a clarifying question — not because either is inherently wrong, but because the gap itself is useful to understand before the resume goes further.

Look for appropriate depth relative to seniority: an entry-level candidate listing broad exposure to many tools reads differently than a senior candidate expected to show depth in a narrower set — but this article does not define what "appropriate" depth looks like for any specific role, since that varies and is best assessed by someone with direct technical context.

## Advisor Guidance

Stay at the level of clarity and consistency, never skill verdict. If a candidate or a colleague asks whether the technical content on a resume is "good enough," redirect that question to an internal subject matter expert rather than answering it directly — this is the same escalation discipline already established for industry-specific technical questions.

## Key Takeaways

Evaluate a technical resume for specificity, consistency with what the candidate has said elsewhere, and presentation relative to seniority — never for whether the underlying technical skill itself is sufficient, which is a question to route to a subject matter expert rather than answer directly.
