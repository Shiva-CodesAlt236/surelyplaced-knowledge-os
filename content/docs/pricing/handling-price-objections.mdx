# Handling Price Objections

"It's too expensive" can mean four genuinely different things. Responding to the wrong one wastes the conversation.

**Why this matters:** one rehearsed rebuttal applied to all four shapes of "too expensive" solves the wrong problem most of the time.
**When to use this:** any time cost objection language comes up, after Investment Psychology and Presenting Investment Confidently have already been applied.
**What skill this builds:** distinguishing affordability, comparison, value-uncertainty, and proxy objections, and matching each to a genuinely different response.
**What happens next:** the correctly identified variant gets its specific response — a proxy for guilt or fear gets handled differently than a genuine affordability constraint.

## Lesson Objective

- Distinguish affordability, comparison, value-uncertainty, and proxy objections
- Match each variant to a genuinely different response, not one rehearsed rebuttal
- Recognize when cost is standing in for guilt or fear, not a real financial constraint

## Coaching Notes

<CoachingNotes title="Coaching Notes">
Your real skill here is diagnostic, not persuasive. Figuring out which variant is in play matters more than any rebuttal you could give.
</CoachingNotes>

<CoachingNotes title="Why This Worked">
Rahul's objection turned out to be value-uncertainty, not affordability or comparison. Responding with reassurance about affordability, which would have been the reflexive move, would have completely missed it.
