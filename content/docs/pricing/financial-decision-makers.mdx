# Financial Decision-Makers

A genuine financial co-decision-maker isn't an obstacle to route around. Including them directly tends to produce a stronger decision, not a slower one.

**Why this matters:** routing around a genuine co-decision-maker instead of including them tends to produce a slower, weaker decision, not a faster one.
**When to use this:** any time it becomes clear someone besides the candidate is genuinely part of the financial decision.
**What skill this builds:** recognizing a real co-decision-maker, offering to include them directly, and holding the same honesty standard for both people.
**What happens next:** the decision moves forward with both people genuinely informed, rather than one relaying secondhand information to the other.

## Lesson Objective

- Recognize a genuine financial co-decision-maker, not just an approval-giver
- Offer to include them directly rather than leaving the candidate to relay everything
- Hold the exact same honesty standard for both people in the conversation

## Coaching Notes

<CoachingNotes title="Coaching Notes">
Normalize a co-decision-maker immediately, without treating it as a complication. Households and support networks make financial decisions together all the time.
</CoachingNotes>

<CoachingNotes title="Why This Worked">
Asking directly whether Akash's brother was "part of it" or "just aware" surfaced a real financial stake, not just a courtesy heads-up — and that distinction changed what the right next step was.
</CoachingNotes>
