# Pricing Red Flags

The job here isn't to close more skillfully. It's to slow down, ask honestly, and sometimes gently discourage a decision that doesn't look sound.

## Lesson Objective

- Distinguish ordinary price hesitation from genuine financial vulnerability
- Recognize urgency that seems disconnected from a candidate's actual circumstances
- Escalate a question beyond advisor authority instead of answering from best judgment

## Coaching Notes

<CoachingNotes title="Coaching Notes">
A short-term close that leaves a candidate deciding on someone else's timeline is not a success by any definition that matters here. Slowing down is the correct outcome.
</CoachingNotes>

<CoachingNotes title="Why This Worked">
Naming the mismatch directly — "this sounds like your brother's timeline, not yours" — gave Akash room to admit what was actually true, rather than pushing forward on borrowed urgency.
</CoachingNotes>
