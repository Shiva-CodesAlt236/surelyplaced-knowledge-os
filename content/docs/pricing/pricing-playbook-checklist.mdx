# Pricing Playbook Checklist

> This is a sales playbook, not a pricing document. Nothing on this checklist states or implies a specific price, discount, financing term, or guarantee — those live in the latest approved Surely Placed pricing policy.

## Purpose

The eight articles in this module each cover a distinct piece of the investment conversation — mindset, delivery, objection variants, ROI framing, payment psychology, discounts, co-decision-makers, and risk signals. This checklist gathers the disciplines from all of them into a single working reference an advisor can consult before or during a financial conversation, without needing to reopen every individual article.

## Learning Objectives

- Confirm readiness across the full set of pricing-conversation disciplines in one pass
- Use this checklist as a self-review tool after a call involving cost or payment
- Recognize which article to return to when a specific gap is identified
- Reinforce the module's core theme: confidence and honesty are not in tension with each other

## Candidate Mindset

Candidates experience the investment conversation as a single, continuous moment, even though this module breaks it into separate skills for teaching purposes. A candidate doesn't notice whether an advisor is drawing on "ROI framing" or "confident delivery" specifically — they notice whether the whole conversation feels steady, honest, and genuinely centered on their decision.

## Advisor Mindset

This checklist is a reference for review, not a script to recite in the moment. Reading through it before a call that's expected to involve cost, and again afterward as a self-review, builds the underlying instincts faster than trying to consciously apply every item live.

## When To Use

- Before any call where cost, payment, or a discount question is expected to come up
- Immediately after a call as a self-review, to notice what went well and what to revisit
- When onboarding a newer advisor to this module, as a summary companion to the full articles

## Recommended Conversation Framework

Mindset and psychology
- [ ] I understand why candidates experience spending on themselves differently than other purchases (see [Investment Psychology](/docs/pricing/investment-psychology))
- [ ] I recognize guilt, fear, and hope as normal undercurrents in this conversation, not obstacles to eliminate

Delivery
- [ ] I can state cost at a steady, unhurried pace without rushing past it (see [Presenting Investment Confidently](/docs/pricing/presenting-investment-confidently))
- [ ] I resist the urge to immediately over-explain or justify the number the moment it's said

Objections and value
- [ ] I diagnose which variant of a price objection is actually present before responding (see [Handling Price Objections](/docs/pricing/handling-price-objections))
- [ ] I frame value qualitatively and never cite a specific ROI figure, payback period, or outcome statistic (see [ROI Conversations](/docs/pricing/roi-conversations))

Payment and decision-makers
- [ ] I answer payment questions accurately and flag anything that needs confirmation rather than guessing (see [Payment Conversations](/docs/pricing/payment-conversations))
- [ ] I offer to include a genuine financial co-decision-maker directly rather than leaving the candidate to relay the conversation alone (see [Financial Decision-Makers](/docs/pricing/financial-decision-makers))

Discounts and risk
- [ ] I never invent, imply, or estimate a discount and always confirm through the correct channel (see [Discount Guidelines](/docs/pricing/discount-guidelines))
- [ ] I recognize genuine financial vulnerability or an out-of-authority question and respond with care and escalation, not persuasion (see [Pricing Red Flags](/docs/pricing/pricing-red-flags))

## Discovery Questions

1. "Looking back at this call, was there a moment involving cost where my pace or tone shifted?"
2. "Did I diagnose the actual objection, or did I respond to the words alone?"
3. "Did I state anything — a figure, a discount, a guarantee — that I wasn't fully certain was accurate and current?"
4. "Was there a signal in this call, financial or otherwise, that deserved more care than I gave it?"

## Buying Signals

- The candidate's tone and pace stayed steady through the cost conversation, consistent with the advisor's own composure
- The candidate's objection, once correctly diagnosed, resolved through honest conversation rather than repeated pressure
- The candidate engaged with the value description on its own terms rather than pressing only for a number

## Risk Signals

- Repeated discomfort or rushed pacing specifically at the moment cost is introduced, call after call
- A pattern of guessing at discount, payment, or policy specifics instead of confirming them
- A missed financial co-decision-maker or an unaddressed genuine affordability concern

## Common Mistakes

- Treating this checklist as a script to read from live, rather than a review tool
- Skipping the self-review after a difficult financial conversation, missing the chance to improve
- Using the diagnostic skill from this module to pressure rather than to genuinely understand a candidate's concern

## Ethical Guidelines

Every item on this checklist supports honesty and candidate wellbeing, never persuasion at the expense of either. Confidence, structure, and skill are meant to make the conversation clearer and more honest for the candidate — not more effective at overriding a legitimate hesitation or concern.
