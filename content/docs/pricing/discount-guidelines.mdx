# Discount Guidelines

> **Read this note before anything else.** This article does not contain a discount policy, does not state what discretion (if any) an advisor holds, and does not state any figure, percentage, or condition under which a discount might be offered. None of that exists in this repository. Advisors must follow the latest approved Surely Placed pricing policy for any question about discounts, and must never improvise, estimate, or infer an answer from this article or from a past conversation. If that policy is unclear or hasn't been provided to you, escalate the question internally before responding to the candidate — do not guess.

## Purpose

Candidates ask for discounts. This is a completely normal part of sales conversations across every industry, and it deserves a calm, professional response — but the substance of that response depends entirely on Surely Placed's actual, current pricing policy, which is not documented in this repository. This article covers the *conversation skill* of handling a discount request professionally; it does not and cannot tell an advisor what they're authorized to offer.

## Learning Objectives

- Respond to a discount request calmly and professionally without guessing at an answer
- Understand why improvising a discount, or implying one might be possible, creates real risk for the candidate and for Surely Placed
- Know exactly what to say when a discount question can't be answered on the spot
- Distinguish between acknowledging a request and granting or implying one
- Escalate a discount question through the correct internal channel rather than resolving it independently

## Candidate Mindset

Asking for a discount is often not a sign of bad faith — many candidates ask as a matter of habit, because it's a normal thing to try in any purchase decision, and some see it as a reasonable step in any negotiation. Others ask because they're genuinely at the edge of what they can afford and are hoping for some flexibility. The advisor generally cannot tell which is which from the request alone, and it's not necessary to know in order to respond appropriately.

## Advisor Mindset

The single most important discipline in this entire module lives in this article: an advisor must never invent, imply, or estimate a discount, exception, or special arrangement, under any circumstance, regardless of how reasonable the request sounds or how much rapport has been built in the call. This isn't about rigidity — it's about protecting the candidate from being told something inaccurate, and protecting the integrity of whatever the actual, approved policy is.

An advisor who doesn't know the current policy is not expected to know it from memory — they're expected to say so honestly and find out, rather than guess.

## When To Use

- Any time a candidate directly or indirectly asks for a discount, a lower price, a waiver, or any nonstandard financial arrangement
- When a candidate references a discount or promotion they believe another candidate or program received
- When training a newer advisor on how to handle this conversation without being given information not contained in this article

## Recommended Conversation Framework

Acknowledge the request warmly and without defensiveness — there's nothing wrong with a candidate asking.

State plainly that you want to give them an accurate answer rather than a guess, and that this specific question needs to go through the correct internal process.

Follow up with the accurate, current answer as quickly as possible, rather than leaving the candidate waiting indefinitely on an unresolved question central to their decision.

Never fill the gap in the moment with something that sounds reasonable — a rough estimate, a "that's usually possible," or any other soft implication that an exception is likely.

## Discovery Questions

1. "Can I ask what's prompting that — is it about the number itself, or is there something specific making this feel out of reach right now?"
2. "Is there something specific you heard, from us or elsewhere, about a discount or promotion that I should look into?"
3. "Would it help if I found out the accurate answer to that and followed up directly, rather than me guessing right now?"

## Buying Signals

- The candidate accepts the honest "let me confirm that" response without it damaging the conversation's momentum
- The candidate's underlying concern, once understood, turns out to be addressable through the broader investment conversation rather than a discount specifically
- The candidate appreciates the advisor's honesty about not guessing, consistent with the broader trust-building pattern in [Trust and Credibility](/docs/objections/trust-and-credibility)

## Risk Signals

- The candidate describes a level of financial strain that suggests the real issue isn't the discount, but genuine affordability — see [Pricing Red Flags](/docs/pricing/pricing-red-flags)
- The candidate references a specific discount or arrangement supposedly offered elsewhere that sounds inconsistent with anything the advisor has been told
- The advisor notices their own temptation to offer something informally just to keep the conversation moving

## Common Mistakes

- Guessing at whether a discount is possible instead of saying plainly that it needs to be confirmed
- Implying flexibility through tone or soft language ("I might be able to work something out") without any basis to say so
- Treating this as a rare edge case rather than a normal, expected part of sales conversations that deserves a prepared, honest response
- Failing to follow up promptly once a discount question has been escalated, leaving the candidate in limbo

## Ethical Guidelines

Never offer, imply, or estimate a discount, waiver, or special arrangement without explicit, current authorization to do so. This protects the candidate from inaccurate information and protects the integrity of Surely Placed's actual pricing structure, whatever it currently is. When in doubt, say "let me confirm that for you" — every time, without exception.

## Key Takeaways

This article intentionally contains no discount policy, no pricing authority, and no numbers — because none of that exists in this repository, and inventing it here would put both the candidate and Surely Placed at risk. Respond to any discount request calmly and warmly, confirm the real answer through the correct internal channel, and never guess, imply, or estimate.
