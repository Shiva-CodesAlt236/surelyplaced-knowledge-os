# Presenting Surely Placed

Two candidates can hear the exact same facts and walk away with completely different impressions — depending on whether those facts were tied to them, or delivered as a generic pitch.

**Why this matters:** the same accurate facts, delivered as a generic pitch instead of a tailored recommendation, can make a candidate disengage even when nothing you said was wrong.
**When to use this:** right after Discovery's goals are understood and the diagnostic part of Discussion is complete — this is where you actually introduce Surely Placed.
**What skill this builds:** opening a recommendation with the candidate's own words instead of a program description, and recovering cleanly if it still lands as generic.
**What happens next:** once the recommendation is tailored and landed, the conversation moves into explaining exactly what the process involves.

## Lesson Objective

- Open the recommendation with the candidate's own words, not a program description
- Describe what SurelyPlaced provides — never an outcome that can't be promised
- Recover cleanly when a recommendation sounds "generic" to the candidate

<ManagerTip from="Aayush" priority="critical">
The instant a candidate says "that's what everyone says," do not repeat your pitch louder or more confident. That's the single fastest way to confirm you actually are generic. Ask what a real answer would sound like to them, and let their answer tell you what to say next.
</ManagerTip>

## Coaching Notes

<CoachingNotes title="Coaching Notes">
Reuse Priya's own phrase — "switching tracks feels genuinely uncertain" — almost verbatim. Hearing your own words reflected back is one of the fastest ways a candidate registers they were actually heard.
</CoachingNotes>

<CoachingNotes title="Why This Worked">
Naming the limitation ("not a guarantee") in the same breath as genuine confidence didn't undercut the pitch — it made it more credible. A recommendation that's confident and honest about its limits lands better than one that's confident and silent about them.
</CoachingNotes>
