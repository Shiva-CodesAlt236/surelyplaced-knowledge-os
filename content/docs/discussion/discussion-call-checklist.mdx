# Discussion Call Checklist

## Purpose

This article closes out the Discussion Calls module the same way the Discovery Call Checklist closes out Discovery — as a practical check against everything the module covers, run near the end of a call to confirm the conversation actually did its job before the candidate moves toward a closing call. It isn't new material. It's a way of catching, in the moment, whether the four-stage structure from [Structuring the Discussion Call](/docs/discussion/structuring-the-discussion-call) actually held up in practice.

The checklist itself:

1. Did I open by reflecting discovery back accurately, and did the candidate confirm it?
2. Was the recommendation I presented specific to this candidate, or could it have been said to anyone?
3. Did I explain the process honestly, including what's expected of the candidate, not just what they receive?
4. Did I state only current, confirmed information — nothing recited from memory that I wasn't sure was accurate?
5. Was cost introduced only after recommendation and process were genuinely understood?
6. If hesitation came up anywhere in the call, did I find out what was actually behind it before responding?
7. Does the candidate have a clear, accurate sense of what happens next?
8. Is there anything today I said with more confidence than I actually had?

If any answer isn't a clear yes, that's specific, useful information about where to go before this candidate moves toward a closing conversation — not a reason to consider the call a failure.

## Learning Objectives

- Use this checklist as an active check throughout the call, not only as a final review
- Identify, from how a call unfolded, which checklist items are most likely to need attention
- Ask a small number of direct questions late in the call to close any real gaps before ending it
- Avoid letting checklist completion become more important than the honesty of what was actually discussed
- Hand a candidate off to a closing conversation only once this checklist genuinely holds up

## Candidate Psychology

By the end of a discussion call, most candidates have made an internal judgment about whether this conversation was worth their time — and that judgment is heavily shaped by whether the call felt coherent. A discussion call that wandered, repeated itself, or left a cost question hanging unanswered creates a lingering sense of disorganization that follows the candidate into whatever comes next, even if they don't consciously connect the feeling to a specific cause.

Candidates are also, by this stage, more invested than they were during discovery — they've heard a real recommendation, understood a real process, and possibly discussed real money. That investment means the end of a discussion call carries more weight than the end of a discovery call did. A vague or rushed close to this particular call risks undoing trust built carefully across the previous forty-five minutes, right at the moment it matters most.

## Advisor Objective

Confirm, before the call ends, that the four-stage structure genuinely held — recap, recommendation, process, investment — and that the candidate has an accurate, specific understanding of what happens next, closing any gap directly rather than letting the call end on an assumption.

## When To Use

- In the final several minutes of every discussion call, as a genuine check rather than a formality
- Mid-call, to track in real time whether each of the four structural stages actually landed before moving to the next
- When a discussion call has been unusually long, interrupted, or emotionally difficult (for example, one that surfaced real financial hesitation), to make sure the ending doesn't get rushed as a result
- When preparing a warm handoff into a closing conversation, either later in the same call or in a follow-up

## Discussion Flow

Move through the checklist quickly but out loud where it matters — some items are internal checks the advisor runs silently (was the recommendation specific enough), and others need to be confirmed directly with the candidate (does the candidate have a clear sense of next steps).

For the items that need direct confirmation, ask plainly rather than assuming: "just so we're aligned, what's your understanding of what happens next?" does more work than an advisor simply summarizing and hoping it landed.

If a gap turns up — a hesitation that wasn't fully explored, a process detail left vague — decide in the moment whether it can be closed in the minutes remaining or whether it genuinely needs a short, specific follow-up call of its own. Don't force a real gap closed just to end the call cleanly.

## Conversation Examples

**Confirming next steps directly at the end of a call:**

> **Advisor:** "Before we finish up, I want to check — what's your understanding of what happens next?"
> **Candidate:** "I think... I look over the cost details you're sending, and then we talk again in a few days?"
> **Advisor:** "Exactly right. I'll get you those details today, and let's actually pick a specific time now rather than leaving it open-ended — does later this week work?"

**Catching an unresolved gap before ending the call:**

> **Advisor:** "One thing I want to go back to — earlier, when we talked about cost, you mentioned things felt tight right now. I don't want to just move past that. Is that still sitting with you, or did the rest of the conversation change how you're thinking about it?"
> **Candidate:** "Honestly, it's still there a bit. I think I need to actually look at my numbers before I can say for sure."
> **Advisor:** "That's completely fair, and I'd rather know that now than assume it's resolved. Let's build that into when we talk next, so we're addressing it directly instead of hoping it's gone away."

## Decision Points

- IF the candidate can accurately restate the recommendation and next steps in their own words →
  THEN the call has done its job — proceed to scheduling or handing off toward a closing conversation.
- IF the candidate's restatement is vague or slightly off →
  THEN clarify directly before ending the call, rather than assuming a close-enough answer is good enough.
- IF a real, unresolved hesitation surfaced earlier in the call and wasn't fully addressed →
  THEN name it directly before the call ends, even if it means the call runs a little longer or ends with a specific, scheduled follow-up rather than a clean close.
- IF you're uncertain whether something you said during the call was fully accurate →
  THEN flag it to yourself now, confirm it afterward, and follow up proactively with the candidate rather than letting an uncertain claim stand unchecked.
- IF the call is running long and there's real pressure to wrap up quickly →
  THEN prioritize confirming next steps and any unresolved hesitation over anything else on the checklist — those two matter most if time is short.

## Common Mistakes

- Ending a discussion call with a summary delivered by the advisor alone, without checking that the candidate's understanding actually matches it
- Letting a real hesitation raised during the investment conversation go unaddressed because the call is running long
- Treating this checklist as something to run through silently, without ever confirming the key items out loud with the candidate
- Rushing the ending of a call that went well, on the assumption that a good call doesn't need a careful close
- Scheduling a vague follow-up ("we'll touch base soon") instead of a specific time, right when specificity matters most

## Things Never To Say

- "I think we're basically done here." (Said without actually confirming next steps — replace with a direct check, not an assumption.)
- "Don't worry about the cost thing, it'll probably work itself out." (Dismisses a real, stated concern instead of treating it as something worth resolving honestly.)
- Any promise about what a closing call will produce — the discussion call can set up a closing conversation clearly, but it doesn't get to decide its outcome in advance.

## Advanced Advisor Tips

Experienced advisors treat this checklist as a running background process throughout the entire discussion call, the same way they would with the discovery checklist — which means very little needs to be caught cold in the final minutes. The end-of-call pass becomes confirmation, not discovery.

It's also worth building the discipline to flag your own uncertain statements to yourself in real time, even mid-sentence, rather than only catching them in review. A brief "let me actually confirm that rather than guess" mid-call costs very little and prevents an inaccurate claim from ever needing correction later.

## Summary

A discussion call is complete when the four-stage structure has genuinely held and the candidate can accurately describe both the recommendation and what happens next. Confirm this directly rather than assuming it from a good overall feeling about the call, and don't let a real, unresolved hesitation — especially around cost — go unaddressed just because the call is ending. Close specifically, not vaguely, every time.
