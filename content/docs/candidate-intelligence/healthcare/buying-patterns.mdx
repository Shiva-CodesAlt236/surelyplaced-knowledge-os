# Buying Patterns

## Purpose

This article carries the mission-and-caution mindset from [Candidate Behaviour](/docs/candidate-intelligence/healthcare/candidate-behaviour) into concrete buying signals, hesitation patterns, and decision style for `ROLE-HEALTH`, using the [Buyer Persona Schema](/docs/candidate-intelligence/BUYER_PERSONA_SCHEMA) taxonomy.

## Scope

The patterns described below apply at the role-family level; a working-pattern article flags it directly if its buying behavior departs from this baseline. Nothing here states a clinical or regulatory fact.

## Core Content

This population's buying pattern sits closest to `PERSONA-RISK-AVERSE`, shaped by the regulatory caution described in Candidate Behaviour — this population evaluates a decision carefully and structurally, and tends to be uncomfortable with anything that feels rushed or under-explained. A structured, transparent conversation builds trust considerably faster here than an enthusiastic but vague one.

Hesitation frequently traces back to an unresolved compliance or licensure question raised earlier and not properly routed to an expert — this population is unlikely to proceed comfortably with that kind of question left open, even if everything else about the conversation went well. A second, distinct source of hesitation is mission-alignment: a candidate who doesn't feel a recommendation connects meaningfully to why they entered the field may hesitate even when the practical terms are otherwise strong.

A related consideration specific to [Healthcare IT Project Coordinator](/docs/candidate-intelligence/healthcare/healthcare-it-project-coordinator) candidates: because that pattern sits close to general Project Management work, a decision may hinge partly on whether the healthcare-specific or the general framing feels like the better long-term fit.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article maps [Buyer Persona Schema](/docs/candidate-intelligence/BUYER_PERSONA_SCHEMA)'s general taxonomy onto this role family's mission-and-caution mindset, following the same extension pattern used by [Buying Patterns](/docs/candidate-intelligence/supply-chain/buying-patterns) in the Supply Chain collection.

## Advisor Guidance

Resolve any compliance or licensure question by routing it to the right expert rather than leaving it open, connect the recommendation explicitly to the candidate's stated motivation, and clarify framing directly for candidates near the Project Management boundary.

## Key Takeaways

Healthcare IT candidates evaluate decisions carefully and structurally, hesitate most when a compliance or licensure question is left unresolved or when a recommendation doesn't connect to their underlying motivation, and Project Coordinator-pattern candidates may weigh a decision partly on healthcare-specific versus general framing.
