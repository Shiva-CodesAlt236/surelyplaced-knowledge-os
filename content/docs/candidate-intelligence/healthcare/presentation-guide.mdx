# Presentation Guide

## Purpose

This guide applies the existing Discussion module to `ROLE-HEALTH`, continuing this collection's four-stage advisor playbook. Nothing in this guide states a clinical or regulatory fact.

## Scope

This guide covers presentation-call approach for Healthcare IT candidates broadly, building on [Presenting Surely Placed](/docs/discussion/presenting-surely-placed) and [Explaining the Process](/docs/discussion/explaining-the-process).

## Core Content

Present the recommendation in a structured, specific way, per [Candidate Behaviour](/docs/candidate-intelligence/healthcare/candidate-behaviour) — this population is a cautious evaluator uncomfortable with vague or overly casual claims, and a structured presentation builds credibility faster than an enthusiastic but loosely organized one.

When explaining the process per [Explaining the Process](/docs/discussion/explaining-the-process), connect it explicitly back to the candidate's stated motivation if a mission-driven element came up during discovery — grounding the practical steps in why they matter to this specific candidate tends to land better than a purely logistical walkthrough.

Be comfortable saying "I'm not the right person to answer that" if a licensure, regulatory, or clinical question comes up at this stage — per the [Healthcare Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/healthcare-playbook), this population respects that far more than a confident guess.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This guide applies [Presenting Surely Placed](/docs/discussion/presenting-surely-placed) and [Explaining the Process](/docs/discussion/explaining-the-process), shaped by this collection's mission-and-caution findings.

## Advisor Guidance

Present in a structured, specific way, connect the recommendation back to the candidate's stated motivation, and say plainly when a question needs to go to a real expert rather than guessing.

Presenting to this role family works best when it's structured and specific, explicitly connected to the candidate's underlying motivation, and honest about the limits of what the advisor can answer directly.
