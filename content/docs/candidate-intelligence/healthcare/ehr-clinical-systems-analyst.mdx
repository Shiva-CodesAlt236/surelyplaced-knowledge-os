# EHR / Clinical Systems Analyst

## Purpose

This article describes the EHR / Clinical Systems Analyst working pattern within `ROLE-HEALTH` — candidates who configure, implement, and support the electronic health record and clinical systems that clinical staff rely on directly.

## Scope

This article covers what typically distinguishes a systems-configuration-focused candidate from the other three working patterns in this collection, for sales-conversation purposes only. Nothing here states a clinical or regulatory fact.

## Core Content

Candidates in this pattern describe their work in terms of system configuration, workflow builds, and troubleshooting within an electronic health record or related clinical platform — work that sits close to end users whose daily work depends directly on the system functioning correctly. Many candidates in this pattern describe a heightened sense of stakes compared to general enterprise IT work, since a clinical system issue can affect how care is delivered, not just internal productivity.

This pattern is distinct from [Health Informatics Analyst](/docs/candidate-intelligence/healthcare/health-informatics-analyst) in orientation — that pattern works with the data these systems produce, while this pattern builds and maintains the systems themselves.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This pattern gives specific shape to the health information systems dimension the [Healthcare Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/healthcare-playbook) names generally, and its stakes-awareness echoes the reliability-focused themes found elsewhere in this framework, applied here to systems that directly touch patient care.

## Advisor Guidance

Recognize that this pattern's description of "downtime" or "system issues" often carries a different weight than in general enterprise IT — take that seriously rather than treating it as routine technical language.

## Key Takeaways

EHR / Clinical Systems Analysts configure and support the systems clinical staff use directly, often carry a heightened sense of stakes compared to general enterprise IT work, and are distinguished from Health Informatics Analysts by building the systems rather than analyzing the data those systems produce.
