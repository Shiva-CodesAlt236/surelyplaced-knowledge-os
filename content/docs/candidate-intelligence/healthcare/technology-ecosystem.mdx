# Technology Ecosystem

> Every product named below illustrates a category only — none is compared, ranked, or endorsed over another. Nothing here states a regulatory or compliance fact.

## Purpose

This article gives an advisor a working vocabulary of the systems a Healthcare IT candidate might reference, in line with [Technology Mapping](/docs/candidate-intelligence/TECHNOLOGY_MAPPING). It is not clinical, regulatory, or systems training, and it evaluates no specific product.

## Scope

Four categories of tooling common across this role family are covered below, spanning clinical systems through analytics. No single tool receives its own dedicated treatment, and no regulatory framework is described as fact.

## Core Content

**Electronic health record platforms** — the core clinical systems used to document and manage patient care information. Epic and Cerner are two illustrative examples of what sits in this category, mentioned only to show its shape, not to compare or endorse either.

**Interoperability and data exchange platforms** — systems supporting the secure exchange of health information between organizations. Redox and Datavant are two illustrative examples of what sits in this category, mentioned only to show its shape, not to compare or endorse either.

**Practice management and scheduling systems** — platforms supporting administrative operations like scheduling and billing workflows. Athenahealth and Allscripts are two illustrative examples of what sits in this category, mentioned only to show its shape, not to compare or endorse either.

**Clinical reporting and analytics tools** — dashboarding and data tools used to analyze healthcare operational and quality data. Qlik and Looker are two illustrative examples of what sits in this category, mentioned only to show its shape, not to compare or endorse either.

A candidate might just as easily name a system not listed here, or a direct competitor to one that is — treat this as a starting vocabulary, not an exhaustive inventory of the field.
