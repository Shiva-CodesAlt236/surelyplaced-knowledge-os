# Buying Patterns

> These are financial decision-making tendencies observed generally across the Data & AI role family — not a prediction about any specific person. This article states no price, discount, or return figure.

## Purpose

This article generalizes the financial decision-making patterns established in Pricing across the four working patterns within `ROLE-DATA`, extending the general behavioral tendencies covered in [Candidate Behaviour](/docs/candidate-intelligence/data-and-ai/candidate-behaviour) into the specific context of cost and investment conversations.

## Scope

This article covers general buying-pattern tendencies across Data & AI candidates. It does not state or imply any actual pricing, discount, or return figure, and does not claim these patterns apply to every individual.

## Core Content

A direct request for a specific ROI figure is common across this role family, and [Machine Learning Engineer](/docs/candidate-intelligence/data-and-ai/machine-learning-engineer) noted a distinct nuance worth generalizing here: candidates across this role family who work professionally with quantified uncertainty — a description that fits data analysts and machine learning engineers particularly well — often respond better to a transparent "here's why I can't give you a precise number" than to a confident but unsubstantiated figure. This makes the discipline in [ROI Conversations](/docs/pricing/roi-conversations) not just necessary but often genuinely well-received by this role family specifically.

Comparison objections are common, particularly given how fast-moving and crowded some parts of this field are (especially AI engineering) — apply [Handling Price Objections](/docs/pricing/handling-price-objections)' comparison-objection framework, focusing on Surely Placed's own documented approach rather than disputing claims about alternatives.

Career stage interacts with financial decision-making here similarly to other role families — an earlier-career candidate may be facing a major independent financial decision for the first time, heightening the dynamic described in [Investment Psychology](/docs/pricing/investment-psychology), while [Financial Decision-Makers](/docs/pricing/financial-decision-makers) may be more relevant for this group than for more established professionals, though this should always be confirmed directly.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article generalizes [ROI Conversations](/docs/pricing/roi-conversations), [Handling Price Objections](/docs/pricing/handling-price-objections), [Investment Psychology](/docs/pricing/investment-psychology), and [Financial Decision-Makers](/docs/pricing/financial-decision-makers) across the Data & AI role family, introducing no new pricing concept.

## Advisor Guidance

Lean into transparent acknowledgment of uncertainty when discussing ROI with this role family, particularly for analytically-minded patterns — this tends to build credibility rather than undermine it. Calibrate sensitivity to first-time financial decisions and co-decision-makers based on the candidate's actual stated career stage.

## Key Takeaways

Data & AI candidates broadly request ROI figures directly and often respond better to transparent acknowledgment of uncertainty than to an unsubstantiated confident number, given many patterns' professional comfort with probabilistic reasoning — while comparison objections and financial-decision-maker relevance vary by specific pattern and career stage.
