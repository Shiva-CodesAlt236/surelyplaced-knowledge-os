# Buying Patterns

## Purpose

This article extends [Candidate Behaviour](/docs/candidate-intelligence/qa-testing/candidate-behaviour) into specific buying signals, hesitation patterns, and decision style for `ROLE-QA`, using the [Buyer Persona Schema](/docs/candidate-intelligence/BUYER_PERSONA_SCHEMA) taxonomy.

## Scope

This article describes buying-pattern tendencies that hold across the role family as a whole; a working-pattern article flags it directly if that pattern departs from what's described here.

## Core Content

The credibility-sensitivity described in Candidate Behaviour predicts a buying pattern closest to `PERSONA-VALUE-UNCERTAIN` — candidates genuinely unsure whether a program will represent their specific specialization accurately, rather than skeptical of the service itself. This uncertainty is less about affordability and more about representation: a candidate is often silently asking whether an advisor, and by extension a future employer conversation, will describe their work with the technical seriousness it deserves.

Hesitation in this population often surfaces around whether to invest further in developing new skills — for example a manual tester weighing whether to build automation skills — versus pursuing a role at their current skill level. This isn't reluctance to move forward; it's a genuine open question about direction that an advisor can help clarify rather than needing to resolve immediately.

A related signal specific to [SDET](/docs/candidate-intelligence/qa-testing/sdet) candidates: because that pattern sits at the boundary with Software Engineering, these candidates may be simultaneously evaluating both fields, and a decision may hinge as much on which identity feels like the better fit as on any specific opportunity's details.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article maps [Buyer Persona Schema](/docs/candidate-intelligence/BUYER_PERSONA_SCHEMA)'s general taxonomy onto this role family's specific credibility-sensitivity, following the same extension pattern used by [Buying Patterns](/docs/candidate-intelligence/cloud-devops/buying-patterns) in the Cloud & DevOps collection.

## Advisor Guidance

Address representation directly — confirm explicitly that the candidate's specific specialization will be described accurately going forward. Help clarify open direction questions (such as whether to build new skills) rather than treating them as objections to overcome.

## Key Takeaways

QA Testing candidates tend toward a representation-uncertain buying style rooted in whether their specific specialization will be taken seriously, often weigh genuine open questions about skill direction rather than raise outright objections, and SDET candidates in particular may be deciding between two fields at once.
