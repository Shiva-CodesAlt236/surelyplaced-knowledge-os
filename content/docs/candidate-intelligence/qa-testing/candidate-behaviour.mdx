# Candidate Behaviour

## Purpose

This article describes the mindset and communication style that most consistently shows up across the four QA Testing working patterns, giving this collection's sales intelligence a distinct anchor separate from the Software Engineering, Data & AI, Cybersecurity, and Cloud & DevOps collections.

## Scope

This article describes the baseline mindset shared across the role family as a whole. Where a specific working pattern diverges from this baseline, its own article calls that out directly.

## Core Content

Where Cloud & DevOps candidates are most consistently defined by an ownership identity and Cybersecurity candidates carry a trained instinct for spotting inconsistency, QA Testing candidates are most consistently defined by a heightened sensitivity to being underestimated. Many have encountered, directly or indirectly, a perception that testing work is a lesser or purely entry-level technical path compared to development — and that history shapes how closely they listen for whether a conversation is quietly reinforcing that perception, even unintentionally.

This sensitivity tends to show up as careful attention to word choice: a candidate in this population may notice, and react to, language that implies their work is a stepping stone to "real" engineering rather than a legitimate specialization in its own right. A second consistent trait follows directly from the field's own internal range — because backgrounds here vary from manual testing through fully code-driven SDET work, candidates are often quietly evaluating whether the person they're speaking with actually understands the distinction between these working patterns, or is treating "QA" as one undifferentiated category.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article extends the general mindset and buying-behaviour observations already made at the industry level in the [QA & Testing Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/qa-testing-playbook), giving them a more specific behavioural frame consistent with how the Software Engineering, Data & AI, Cybersecurity, and Cloud & DevOps collections each anchor their own sales intelligence in a distinct theme.

## Advisor Guidance

Treat this population's work as a genuine technical specialization from the outset, be precise about which working pattern a candidate actually describes rather than defaulting to a generic "QA" framing, and stay alert to language that could unintentionally imply testing is a lesser or transitional path.
