# Candidate Behaviour

## Purpose

This article names the mindset that most reliably shows up across the four SAP working patterns, giving this collection its own distinct anchor apart from the collections before it: process-rigor evaluation.

## Scope

What follows applies broadly across this role family. Any working pattern that departs meaningfully from this baseline says so in its own article.

## Core Content

Because large enterprise implementations run on structured phases with defined gates — requirements, blueprint, build, testing, go-live — candidates in this population tend to think about any process, including a hiring or placement process, through a similar structured lens. An advisor who can describe what happens at each stage of the process clearly, and in what order, tends to build credibility with this population faster than one who speaks only in general reassurance.

A second, related trait: this population is often quick to notice when a process feels ad hoc, disorganized, or inconsistently explained, and treats that as a meaningful signal about how a receiving organization actually operates — not necessarily a disqualifying one, but one worth naming and addressing directly rather than glossing over. This isn't the same as the risk-averse caution documented elsewhere in this framework; it's specifically a structural, process-literacy lens this population applies almost reflexively.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article gives the SAP collection its own behavioural anchor, following the same distinct-theme discipline every preceding collection in this framework applies to itself.

## Advisor Guidance

Describe each stage of the process clearly and in order rather than relying on general reassurance, and if the candidate notices something that seems inconsistently explained, address it directly rather than moving past it.

SAP ecosystem candidates are most reliably defined by a structured, process-rigor evaluation lens carried over from working within phase-gated enterprise implementations, and they respond best to a clearly sequenced explanation of process over general reassurance.
