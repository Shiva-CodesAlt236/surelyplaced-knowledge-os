# Buying Patterns

## Purpose

This article carries the process-rigor evaluation mindset from [Candidate Behaviour](/docs/candidate-intelligence/sap/candidate-behaviour) into concrete buying signals, hesitation patterns, and decision style for this role family, using the [Buyer Persona Schema](/docs/candidate-intelligence/BUYER_PERSONA_SCHEMA) taxonomy.

## Scope

The buying signals below hold generally across this role family. Where a working pattern's buying behavior meaningfully diverges, its own article notes that directly.

## Core Content

This population's buying pattern sits closest to `PERSONA-RISK-AVERSE`, though the driver is somewhat distinct from the general caution documented elsewhere in this framework — it stems specifically from a structured, phase-literate way of evaluating any process, carried over directly from enterprise-implementation experience. A candidate who can see the shape of the process ahead of them, sequenced and explained clearly, tends to move forward with real confidence.

Hesitation most often traces back to a step in the process feeling skipped, out of order, or inconsistently explained relative to what was said earlier — this population notices that kind of inconsistency quickly, given how central sequencing and phase discipline are to their own professional evaluation lens, and hesitation here reflects a process-literacy concern rather than distrust of the opportunity itself. Naming the sequence explicitly and correcting any inconsistency tends to resolve it directly.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article maps [Buyer Persona Schema](/docs/candidate-intelligence/BUYER_PERSONA_SCHEMA)'s general taxonomy onto this role family's process-rigor mindset, following the same extension pattern used by [Buying Patterns](/docs/candidate-intelligence/salesforce/buying-patterns) in the Salesforce collection.

## Advisor Guidance

Lay out the process sequence clearly and consistently from the first conversation onward, and if hesitation appears, check first whether it traces back to a sequencing inconsistency before assuming it reflects a deeper concern about the opportunity itself.

## Key Takeaways

SAP candidates move forward most confidently when the process ahead is laid out clearly and sequentially, hesitate most when a step feels skipped or inconsistently explained, and that hesitation reflects this population's process-literacy lens rather than distrust of the opportunity itself.
