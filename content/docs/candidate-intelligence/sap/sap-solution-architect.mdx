# SAP Solution Architect

## Purpose

This article describes the SAP Solution Architect working pattern — candidates responsible for the overall technical and process design of an implementation program spanning multiple modules and business areas.

## Scope

This article is meant to help an advisor tell a senior, design-focused candidate apart from the other three working patterns in this collection. It stops short of naming any specific certification level.

## Core Content

Candidates in this pattern describe setting the overall solution design for a program — how modules integrate, how the technical landscape is structured, and how the implementation lifecycle is governed from blueprint through go-live and beyond. Most carry substantial prior experience in one or more of this collection's other three patterns, and often describe the shift into architecture as one from owning a defined scope to being accountable for how an entire program holds together procedurally and technically.

This population tends to speak in governance and lifecycle terms — phase gates, landscape strategy, long-term maintainability — rather than a single module's requirements, and often has direct experience reviewing the work of functional analysts, developers, and process consultants on the same program.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This pattern sits at the senior end of the spectrum this collection documents, drawing on the module-configuration, custom-development, and process-design focuses of the other three working patterns without being defined by any single one of them.

## Advisor Guidance

Find out which of the other three patterns shaped the candidate's path into architecture, since this population almost always carries direct experience in at least one of them. Direct discovery toward program-level scope and lifecycle governance rather than a specific technical task, since that's how this population tends to describe its own value.

## Key Takeaways

SAP Solution Architects set overall solution design and lifecycle governance across a program rather than owning one module or task, almost always grew out of one of this collection's other three patterns, and describe their value in terms of program-level structure rather than a single deliverable.
