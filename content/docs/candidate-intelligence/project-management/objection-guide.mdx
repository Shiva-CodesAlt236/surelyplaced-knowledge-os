# Objection Guide

> Nothing below describes an actual conversation, and no price, discount, or guaranteed outcome is named anywhere in this guide.

## Purpose

This guide applies the existing Objection Handling module to `ROLE-PM`, continuing this collection's four-stage advisor playbook.

## Scope

This guide covers objection-handling approach for Project Management candidates broadly, building on [No Guarantee Concern](/docs/objections/no-guarantee-concern) and [Need Time to Think](/docs/objections/need-time-to-think).

## Core Content

Per [Candidate Behaviour](/docs/candidate-intelligence/project-management/candidate-behaviour), [No Guarantee Concern](/docs/objections/no-guarantee-concern) tends to surface here framed as a risk-management question rather than pure skepticism — this population manages risk professionally and naturally applies that same lens to their own decision. Respond the way this population would want risk addressed in their own work: honestly, specifically, without overselling certainty that doesn't exist.

If [Scrum Master / Agile Delivery Lead](/docs/candidate-intelligence/project-management/scrum-master-agile-delivery-lead) candidates raise confusion about how their role fits the recommendation, address the facilitation-versus-ownership distinction directly rather than treating the objection as unrelated to role framing.

Watch for hesitation that traces back to an earlier moment of vagueness — per [Buying Patterns](/docs/candidate-intelligence/project-management/buying-patterns), this population is trained to notice gaps between what's promised and what's plausible, so an objection here often has a specific, identifiable root cause worth tracing back to rather than treating as generic reluctance.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This guide applies [No Guarantee Concern](/docs/objections/no-guarantee-concern) and [Need Time to Think](/docs/objections/need-time-to-think), shaped by this collection's plan-and-risk-transparency findings.

## Advisor Guidance

Treat a guarantee-related objection as a legitimate risk-management question and respond with honesty rather than overselling certainty, clarify facilitation-versus-ownership confusion directly for Scrum Master-pattern candidates, and trace an objection back to its likely root rather than treating it as generic hesitation.
