# Buying Patterns

## Purpose

This article carries the plan-and-risk-transparency mindset from [Candidate Behaviour](/docs/candidate-intelligence/project-management/candidate-behaviour) into concrete buying signals, hesitation patterns, and decision style for `ROLE-PM`, using the [Buyer Persona Schema](/docs/candidate-intelligence/BUYER_PERSONA_SCHEMA) taxonomy.

## Scope

What follows applies to the role family as a whole; where a specific working pattern diverges meaningfully, its own article says so explicitly.

## Core Content

This population's buying pattern sits closest to `PERSONA-COMPARE`, evaluated against a self-authored mental checklist much like a project plan — specific milestones, realistic timing, and honest tradeoffs rather than a single persuasive pitch. Pressure tactics or artificial deadlines tend to produce the opposite of urgency here: a candidate trained to spot unrealistic project plans applies the same scrutiny to a sales conversation, and a manufactured deadline often reads as a credibility problem rather than a reason to act quickly.

Hesitation in this population is frequently structural rather than emotional — a genuine, specific question about how a step in the process actually works, mirroring how this population would ask about a project dependency before signing off on a plan. Treating that kind of question as an objection to overcome, rather than information the candidate genuinely needs, tends to undercut trust that took real effort to build.

A related consideration specific to [Program Manager](/docs/candidate-intelligence/project-management/program-manager) candidates: because that pattern already operates at a strategic, cross-project level, a decision may hinge partly on the scope and visibility of comparable opportunities, not solely on the immediate role's day-to-day content.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article maps [Buyer Persona Schema](/docs/candidate-intelligence/BUYER_PERSONA_SCHEMA)'s general taxonomy onto this role family's plan-and-risk-transparency mindset, following the same extension pattern used by [Buying Patterns](/docs/candidate-intelligence/business-analysis/buying-patterns) in the Business Analysis collection.

## Advisor Guidance

Avoid manufactured urgency entirely — it tends to backfire with this population specifically. Treat a specific process question as genuine information-gathering rather than resistance, and address program scope directly for candidates whose pattern points toward senior, portfolio-level work.

Project Management candidates evaluate a decision against a project-plan-like mental checklist, respond to manufactured urgency with suspicion rather than motivation, and specific process questions from this population are usually genuine information-gathering rather than objections.
