# Program Manager

## Purpose

This article describes the Program Manager working pattern within `ROLE-PM` — candidates who oversee multiple related projects at once, managing dependencies and tradeoffs across them rather than owning a single workstream.

## Scope

This article covers what typically distinguishes a portfolio-level candidate from the other three working patterns in this collection, for sales-conversation purposes only.

## Core Content

Candidates in this pattern describe their work in terms of coordinating across several projects that depend on one another, making tradeoff decisions when priorities compete, and reporting up to executive or senior stakeholders on program-level health rather than a single project's status. This pattern represents the collection's most senior end and is most often reached by candidates who progressed through [IT Project Manager](/docs/candidate-intelligence/project-management/it-project-manager) or a comparable single-project role first.

Because this pattern sits closest to organizational strategy, candidates here may weigh a career move partly on the scope of programs they'd be responsible for and their visibility to senior leadership, not solely on the specific technical domain involved.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This pattern represents the senior end of the spectrum the [Project Manager Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/project-manager-playbook) describes only generally, paralleling [Cloud Architect](/docs/candidate-intelligence/cloud-devops/cloud-architect) in the Cloud & DevOps collection and [QA Lead / Test Manager](/docs/candidate-intelligence/qa-testing/qa-lead-test-manager) in the QA Testing collection as this collection's senior-tier pattern.

## Advisor Guidance

Ask about program scope and executive visibility, not just technical domain, when discovery touches this pattern. Recognize that a candidate here may be evaluating a move partly on organizational influence rather than day-to-day task content alone.
