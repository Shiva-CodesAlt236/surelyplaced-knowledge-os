# Technology Ecosystem

> Every tool named below exists purely to illustrate a category — none is being compared, ranked, or recommended against another.

## Purpose

This article orients an advisor to the kinds of tools a Business Analysis candidate might reference in conversation, in keeping with [Technology Mapping](/docs/candidate-intelligence/TECHNOLOGY_MAPPING). Nothing here amounts to technical training, and no specific product is being evaluated.

## Scope

Four broad tool categories are covered below, each common across this role family regardless of which working pattern a candidate identifies with most. No single product gets its own dedicated write-up.

## Core Content

**Requirements and documentation management** — platforms for capturing, tracking, and organizing requirements over the life of a project. Jira and Confluence are two illustrative examples of products in this category, named only to indicate what the category looks like, not to compare or endorse either.

**Process modeling and diagramming** — tools used to visually map workflows and business processes. Visio and Lucidchart are two illustrative examples of products in this category, named only to indicate what the category looks like, not to compare or endorse either.

**Wireframing and prototyping** — tools for sketching early versions of a screen, workflow, or interaction before a build begins. Balsamiq and Figma are two illustrative examples of products in this category, named only to indicate what the category looks like, not to compare or endorse either.

**Reporting and data-context tools** — spreadsheet and dashboard software referenced when a candidate is defining what a report or metric should measure, not building the underlying data pipeline. Excel and Power BI are two illustrative examples of products in this category, named only to indicate what the category looks like, not to compare or endorse either.

Candidates commonly reference products outside this list entirely, or a direct competitor to one of these — treat this list as a starting vocabulary, not an exhaustive catalog.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article applies the same non-evaluative, category-based approach [Technology Mapping](/docs/candidate-intelligence/TECHNOLOGY_MAPPING) sets out generally, matching the pattern already used in the equivalent article across the Software Engineering, Data & AI, Cybersecurity, Cloud & DevOps, and QA Testing collections.

## Advisor Guidance

Rely on these categories only to recognize a candidate's vocabulary — never to judge whether their specific tool choices were the right ones. Comparing or ranking a named tool against another, or implying a preference for one product over a competitor, has no place in this article or in a live conversation.
