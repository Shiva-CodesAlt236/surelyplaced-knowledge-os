# Technical/Systems Business Analyst

## Purpose

This article describes the Technical/Systems Business Analyst working pattern within `ROLE-BA` — candidates whose work sits closest to engineering teams, translating business requirements into specifications a technical team can build against.

## Scope

This article covers what typically distinguishes a technically-oriented candidate from the other three working patterns in this collection, for sales-conversation purposes only.

## Core Content

Candidates in this pattern describe their work in terms of requirements documentation, systems specifications, and close day-to-day collaboration with developers or engineering teams. Many can speak comfortably about technical concepts without being the one who writes the code, and they often serve as a bridge that reduces miscommunication between business stakeholders and a technical build team.

What distinguishes this pattern most clearly from [Process & Operations Business Analyst](/docs/candidate-intelligence/business-analysis/process-operations-business-analyst) is the audience their work is ultimately written for — this pattern's output is consumed primarily by a technical team, while the process-focused pattern's output is consumed primarily by business stakeholders and operational teams.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This pattern maps to the technical end of the spectrum the [Business Analyst Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/business-analyst-playbook) describes generally, and shares some vocabulary with [SDET](/docs/candidate-intelligence/qa-testing/sdet) in the QA Testing collection without being the same working pattern — both sit at a boundary with technical teams, but this pattern documents requirements rather than testing them.

## Advisor Guidance

Ask specifically how closely a candidate's day-to-day work involves engineering teams, rather than assuming a fixed level of technical depth from the title alone. Confirm early where this candidate sits on the technical-to-stakeholder-facing spectrum this collection is organized around.

Technical/Systems Business Analysts translate business requirements into specifications for a technical build team, sit closest to engineering work of any pattern in this collection, and are distinguished from the more process-focused pattern by who their output is ultimately written for.
