# Process & Operations Business Analyst

## Purpose

This article describes the Process & Operations Business Analyst working pattern within `ROLE-BA` — candidates focused on mapping, evaluating, and improving how a business actually operates day to day.

## Scope

This article covers what typically distinguishes a process-focused candidate from the other three working patterns in this collection, for sales-conversation purposes only.

## Core Content

Candidates in this pattern describe their work in terms of workflow mapping, identifying inefficiency, and redesigning how a team or department operates rather than specifying requirements for a software build. Their output is usually a recommendation, a redesigned process, or a documented workflow rather than a technical specification, and their stakeholders are typically operational or departmental leaders rather than an engineering team.

This pattern sits at the opposite end of the collection's spectrum from [Technical/Systems Business Analyst](/docs/candidate-intelligence/business-analysis/technical-systems-business-analyst) — where that pattern's work is judged by a technical team's ability to build from it, this pattern's work is judged by whether an operational change actually improves how a business runs.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This pattern maps to the stakeholder-facing end of the spectrum the [Business Analyst Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/business-analyst-playbook) describes generally, giving it more specific shape as the collection's clearest non-technical working pattern.

## Advisor Guidance

Don't assume this pattern's work is less rigorous because it's less technical — process analysis requires its own real discipline, and treating it as a lesser skill set will likely read as dismissive to a candidate who takes real pride in that work.

Process & Operations Business Analysts map and improve how a business actually operates, sit at the stakeholder-facing end of this collection's spectrum, and deserve the same technical respect as the collection's more systems-oriented patterns despite working in a different discipline.
