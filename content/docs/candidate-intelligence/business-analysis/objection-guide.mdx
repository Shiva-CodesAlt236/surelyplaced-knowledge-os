# Objection Guide

> No real conversation is described anywhere in this guide, and it names no price, discount, or guaranteed result.

## Purpose

This guide applies the existing Objection Handling module to `ROLE-BA`, continuing this collection's four-stage advisor playbook.

## Scope

This guide covers objection-handling approach for Business Analysis candidates broadly, building on [Need Time to Think](/docs/objections/need-time-to-think) and [Trust and Credibility](/docs/objections/trust-and-credibility).

## Core Content

Per [Buying Patterns](/docs/candidate-intelligence/business-analysis/buying-patterns), [Need Time to Think](/docs/objections/need-time-to-think) surfaces often and genuinely in this population — treat it as this population's professional habit of thorough evaluation showing up in the sales conversation, not as reluctance to move forward. Give a specific, concrete answer to whatever the candidate says they need time to consider rather than a general reassurance, since specificity is what this population responds to.

A version of [Trust and Credibility](/docs/objections/trust-and-credibility) can surface if a candidate doesn't feel their specific version of the role — where they actually sit on the technical-to-stakeholder-facing spectrum — was understood earlier in the conversation. Address that directly by naming their specific working pattern back to them rather than responding with generic reassurance.

Watch for a disorganized or inconsistent explanation on the advisor's part triggering an objection that sounds unrelated on the surface — this population's professional skill is spotting exactly that kind of gap, so an objection here may trace back to a moment of imprecision earlier in the call.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This guide applies [Need Time to Think](/docs/objections/need-time-to-think) and [Trust and Credibility](/docs/objections/trust-and-credibility), shaped by this collection's structured-evaluation findings.

## Advisor Guidance

Treat a request for time to think as genuine and respond with specifics rather than reassurance, name the candidate's specific working pattern back to them if a trust concern surfaces, and consider whether an unrelated-seeming objection traces back to an earlier moment of imprecision.
