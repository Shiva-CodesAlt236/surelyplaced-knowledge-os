# Logistics & Operations Coordinator

## Purpose

This article describes the Logistics & Operations Coordinator working pattern within `ROLE-SUPPLY` — candidates responsible for the day-to-day movement of goods through transportation, warehousing, and fulfillment.

## Scope

This article covers what typically distinguishes an execution-focused candidate from the other three working patterns in this collection, for sales-conversation purposes only.

## Core Content

Candidates in this pattern describe their work in terms of daily execution — keeping shipments, warehouses, and fulfillment processes running on schedule, and reacting quickly when something doesn't. This pattern operates on a shorter time horizon than [Supply Chain Planner](/docs/candidate-intelligence/supply-chain/supply-chain-planner), whose work looks further ahead; a coordinator's success is measured largely by whether today's operation ran without a disruption.

Because disruption is a routine part of this pattern's work, candidates here often develop a practical, unflappable communication style — describing a real operational failure isn't unusual for this pattern, and it's often a more useful discovery signal than a story where nothing went wrong.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This pattern maps to the operational end of the spectrum the [Supply Chain Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/supply-chain-playbook) describes generally, and shares the QA Testing collection's willingness to discuss failure directly — see [Manual QA Tester](/docs/candidate-intelligence/qa-testing/manual-qa-tester) — though grounded here in physical operations rather than software.

## Advisor Guidance

Ask for an example of a disruption this candidate handled, not just a smooth operation — this population tends to describe that kind of story candidly, and it reveals more about their actual capability than a success story would.

Logistics & Operations Coordinators manage the daily execution of moving goods, operate on a shorter time horizon than planning-focused patterns, and tend to describe operational disruption candidly — a trait worth drawing out directly in discovery.
