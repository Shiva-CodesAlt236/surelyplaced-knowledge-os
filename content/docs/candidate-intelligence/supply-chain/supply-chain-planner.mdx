# Supply Chain Planner

## Purpose

This article describes the Supply Chain Planner working pattern within `ROLE-SUPPLY` — candidates who forecast demand and plan inventory levels ahead of when goods are actually needed.

## Scope

This article covers what typically distinguishes a forward-looking, planning-focused candidate from the other three working patterns in this collection, for sales-conversation purposes only.

## Core Content

Candidates in this pattern describe their work in terms of forecasting, inventory targets, and balancing the cost of holding too much stock against the risk of running short — a set of tradeoffs made under real uncertainty about future demand. Where [Logistics & Operations Coordinator](/docs/candidate-intelligence/supply-chain/logistics-operations-coordinator) reacts to what's happening today, this pattern is judged on how well they anticipated what would happen weeks or months ahead.

This pattern often works closely with data, even without necessarily identifying as analytically-focused, and candidates here are frequently in the middle of deciding whether to formalize that into a more systems- and analytics-heavy direction — a genuinely open career question worth surfacing rather than assuming.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This pattern maps to the planning dimension the [Supply Chain Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/supply-chain-playbook) names generally, and sits adjacent to but distinct from [Supply Chain Analytics & Systems Specialist](/docs/candidate-intelligence/supply-chain/supply-chain-analytics-systems-specialist), which this article treats as a possible next step rather than a documentation overlap.

## Advisor Guidance

Ask directly whether this candidate is looking to lean further into data and systems work or stay more focused on planning as it currently stands — that direction is often genuinely undecided rather than already settled.

## Key Takeaways

Supply Chain Planners forecast demand and manage inventory tradeoffs under real uncertainty, are judged on anticipation rather than same-day execution, and frequently face a genuinely open question about whether to lean further into analytics and systems work.
