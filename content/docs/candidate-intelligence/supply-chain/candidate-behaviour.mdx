# Candidate Behaviour

## Purpose

This article names the mindset that most reliably shows up across the four Supply Chain working patterns, giving this collection its own distinct anchor apart from the seven collections before it.

## Scope

This baseline is meant to hold across the role family in general; a working-pattern article notes explicitly wherever it departs from what's described here.

## Core Content

Supply Chain professionals spend their careers judging commitments by whether they actually hold up, not by how confidently they're made — a supplier's promise means little until it's been tested, and a plan is only as good as its weakest dependency. That same evaluative habit carries directly into how this population sizes up a sales conversation: track record and specific, verifiable claims land far better here than enthusiasm or general reassurance.

A second consistent trait follows from the field's practical culture: this population tends to be plainspoken and outcomes-focused, with relatively low tolerance for a conversation that feels abstract or disconnected from concrete results. A third trait reflects the field's own internal shift — because supply chain work increasingly spans from purely operational to heavily analytical, candidates are often quietly checking whether an advisor understands that range rather than treating the field as one undifferentiated category.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article gives more specific behavioural shape to the practical, outcomes-focused mindset the [Supply Chain Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/supply-chain-playbook) already names at the industry level, following the pattern each preceding collection uses to anchor its own sales intelligence in a distinct theme.

## Advisor Guidance

Lead with specifics and evidence rather than enthusiasm, keep the conversation concrete and outcomes-focused, and confirm where a candidate actually sits on the operational-to-analytical range rather than assuming.
