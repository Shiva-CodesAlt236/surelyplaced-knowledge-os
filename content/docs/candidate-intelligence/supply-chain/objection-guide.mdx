# Objection Guide

> This guide invents no real conversation and states no price, discount, or guaranteed result at any point.

## Purpose

This guide applies the existing Objection Handling module to `ROLE-SUPPLY`, continuing this collection's four-stage advisor playbook.

## Scope

This guide covers objection-handling approach for Supply Chain candidates broadly, building on [Price Objection](/docs/objections/price-objection) and [Trust and Credibility](/docs/objections/trust-and-credibility).

## Core Content

Per [Candidate Behaviour](/docs/candidate-intelligence/supply-chain/candidate-behaviour), [Price Objection](/docs/objections/price-objection) tends to surface here in ROI terms rather than as a general affordability concern — this population evaluates a decision the way they'd evaluate any operational investment, weighing concrete return. Respond with specifics rather than general reassurance about value.

A version of [Trust and Credibility](/docs/objections/trust-and-credibility) can surface if a candidate feels their specific position on the operational-to-analytical spectrum wasn't genuinely understood — address that directly by naming their specific working pattern back to them.

Watch for hesitation rooted in unresolved framing for [Supply Chain Analytics & Systems Specialist](/docs/candidate-intelligence/supply-chain/supply-chain-analytics-systems-specialist) candidates — an objection here may really be about whether the recommendation reflects a supply chain identity or a data-and-systems identity, and resolving that distinction directly often resolves the underlying concern.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This guide applies [Price Objection](/docs/objections/price-objection) and [Trust and Credibility](/docs/objections/trust-and-credibility), shaped by this collection's reliability-focused findings.

## Advisor Guidance

Respond to a price-related objection with concrete ROI specifics rather than general reassurance, confirm the candidate's specific working pattern was genuinely understood if a trust concern surfaces, and resolve any unclear framing directly for candidates near the Data & AI boundary.

Objections in this role family are usually ROI questions in disguise, best handled with concrete specifics rather than reassurance, and framing confusion at the Data & AI boundary is often the real concern behind an otherwise unrelated-sounding objection.
