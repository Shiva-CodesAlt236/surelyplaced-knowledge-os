# Reference Profile: Candidate Mindset

> This article is part of the Gold Standard reference profile — an illustrative archetype, never a real person. Every mindset pattern below is drawn from an existing module; nothing here is a psychological claim about a real individual.

## Purpose

This article demonstrates how to populate the `persona_signals` field from [Buyer Persona Schema](/docs/candidate-intelligence/BUYER_PERSONA_SCHEMA) for a specific archetype, showing future profile-writers what a properly grounded mindset section looks like.

## Layer 1 — Candidate Identity

Not the primary focus of this article — see [Career Background](/docs/candidate-intelligence/reference-profile/career-background) for the identity layer this mindset builds on.

## Layer 2 — Sales Intelligence

Two persona signals are commonly relevant to this archetype. First, `PERSONA-SKEPTIC` — consistent with [Software Engineering Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/software-engineering-playbook), an early-career software engineer often evaluates a service with the same comparison-driven, evidence-seeking approach they'd apply to a technical decision, and responds better to specific, verifiable answers than to general reassurance. Second, the OPT timeline described in [F1/OPT Playbook](/docs/visa-playbooks/f1-opt-playbook) introduces a real, bounded urgency that sits alongside — and shouldn't be confused with — the technical skepticism, consistent with the dual-pressure pattern illustrated in [Composite Case Study: OPT Software Engineer](/docs/sales-coaching/composite-case-study-opt-software-engineer).

Where investment specifically comes up, [Investment Psychology](/docs/pricing/investment-psychology) applies generally: an early-career candidate spending on their own career for the first time often experiences the fear/guilt/hope dynamic described there more acutely than a candidate with more established financial footing, though this varies by individual and should never be assumed without the candidate's own signals.

## Layer 3 — Advisor Playbook

An advisor should expect specific, verification-seeking questions rather than general reassurance-seeking ones, and should be prepared to acknowledge the OPT timeline honestly without letting either pressure (technical skepticism or timeline urgency) override honest evaluation of the other, consistent with [Advisor Mindset](/docs/sales-constitution/advisor-mindset).

## Cross-Module Alignment

This mindset section combines `PERSONA-SKEPTIC` from [Buyer Persona Schema](/docs/candidate-intelligence/BUYER_PERSONA_SCHEMA), the OPT urgency pattern from [F1/OPT Playbook](/docs/visa-playbooks/f1-opt-playbook), and the general investment psychology from [Investment Psychology](/docs/pricing/investment-psychology) — each already documented, none newly invented for this profile.

## Key Takeaways

The reference candidate's mindset combines a skepticism-first, comparison-driven evaluation style with real but bounded OPT timeline urgency — two distinct pressures that shouldn't be collapsed into one another. Every element of this mindset traces back to a pattern already documented in Pricing, Visa Playbooks, or Sales Coaching.
