# Reference Profile: Buying Signals

> This article is part of the Gold Standard reference profile — an illustrative archetype, never a real person. Every signal below already appears in an existing module's "Buying Signals" section; this article only gathers the ones most relevant to this specific archetype.

## Purpose

Several modules document buying signals independently, in their own stage context. This article shows how a completed profile pulls the subset relevant to one archetype into a single, quick-reference list — modeling that synthesis for future profile-writers.

## Layer 1 — Candidate Identity

Not the primary focus of this article — see [Overview](/docs/candidate-intelligence/reference-profile/overview) for the identity this signal list applies to.

## Layer 2 — Sales Intelligence

For this archetype, the following signals are most likely to indicate genuine forward momentum:

Specific, verification-seeking questions that get more detailed as the conversation progresses, rather than staying general — consistent with the skepticism-first pattern in [Software Engineering Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/software-engineering-playbook) and the buying-signal pattern in [Handling Price Objections](/docs/pricing/handling-price-objections).

Direct acknowledgment of the OPT timeline as a real factor shaping their decision-making pace, rather than avoidance of the topic — consistent with the timeline-awareness pattern in [F1/OPT Playbook](/docs/visa-playbooks/f1-opt-playbook).

Engagement with the qualitative value description in a cost conversation, rather than continuing to press only for a specific number — consistent with the buying signal described in [ROI Conversations](/docs/pricing/roi-conversations).

## Layer 3 — Advisor Playbook

An advisor should track these signals across the conversation rather than waiting for a single decisive moment, consistent with the four-layer reading framework in [Reading a Sales Conversation](/docs/sales-coaching/reading-a-sales-conversation), and should resist reading routine technical skepticism itself as a negative signal — for this archetype, detailed questioning is often exactly what engagement looks like.

## Cross-Module Alignment

Every signal in this article is drawn directly from an existing "Buying Signals" section in Pricing, Industry Playbooks, or Visa Playbooks — this reference profile introduces no new signal category, only a curated subset relevant to this specific archetype.

## Key Takeaways

For this archetype, genuine forward momentum tends to look like increasingly specific questions, direct engagement with the OPT timeline, and engagement with qualitative value rather than persistent demand for a number — all signals already documented elsewhere in this Knowledge OS, gathered here for quick reference.
