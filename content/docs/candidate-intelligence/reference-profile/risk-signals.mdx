# Reference Profile: Risk Signals

> This article is part of the Gold Standard reference profile — an illustrative archetype, never a real person. Every signal below already appears in an existing module's risk-signal guidance; this article gathers the subset most relevant to this specific archetype.

## Purpose

This article shows how a completed profile pulls relevant risk signals from across the Knowledge OS into one place, so an advisor preparing for a call with this archetype has a quick-reference view of what to watch for.

## Layer 1 — Candidate Identity

Not the primary focus of this article — see [Overview](/docs/candidate-intelligence/reference-profile/overview) for the identity these signals apply to.

## Layer 2 — Sales Intelligence

For this archetype, three risk categories are most relevant. Timeline pressure overriding sound decision-making — the OPT window is finite, and urgency can push a candidate (or an advisor) toward a rushed decision that hasn't been genuinely evaluated, consistent with the caution described in [Visa Red Flags](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-red-flags). Unresolved technical skepticism disguised as agreement — an early-career, comparison-driven candidate may nod along without their underlying verification-seeking questions actually being resolved, consistent with the risk pattern in [Handling Price Objections](/docs/pricing/handling-price-objections). Genuine financial strain specifically because this is often a candidate's first significant independent financial decision, consistent with the vulnerability described in [Pricing Red Flags](/docs/pricing/pricing-red-flags).

## Layer 3 — Advisor Playbook

An advisor should slow down rather than accelerate when OPT timeline pressure is present, confirm that technical questions have actually been resolved rather than assuming silence means agreement, and ask directly about financial comfort rather than assuming readiness from enthusiasm alone — consistent with [Pricing Red Flags](/docs/pricing/pricing-red-flags) and [Internal Escalation Guidelines](/docs/sales-operations/internal-escalation-guidelines) for any situation that needs to be escalated rather than resolved independently.

## Cross-Module Alignment

Every signal in this article is drawn directly from an existing risk-signal or red-flag section in Visa Playbooks, Pricing, or Objections — no new risk category is introduced here.

## Key Takeaways

For this archetype, the risks most worth watching are OPT timeline pressure overriding sound judgment, unresolved technical skepticism disguised as agreement, and genuine financial strain on what may be a candidate's first major independent financial decision — each drawn from risk guidance already documented elsewhere in this Knowledge OS.
