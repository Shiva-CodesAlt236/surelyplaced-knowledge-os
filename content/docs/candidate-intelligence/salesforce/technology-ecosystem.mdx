# Technology Ecosystem

> Every product named below illustrates a category only, within the Salesforce ecosystem specifically — none is compared, ranked, or endorsed, and nothing here claims this platform holds a leading market position over any alternative.

## Purpose

This article gives an advisor a working vocabulary of the systems a Salesforce candidate might reference, in line with [Technology Mapping](/docs/candidate-intelligence/TECHNOLOGY_MAPPING). No specific product is evaluated here, and this platform is never set against another.

## Scope

Four categories of tooling common within this ecosystem are covered below, spanning core clouds through the surrounding productivity layer. None gets a standalone deep dive, and this platform is never held up against a rival one at any point in this article.

## Core Content

**Core relationship-management clouds** — the primary applications most candidates in this ecosystem build or configure on top of. Sales Cloud and Service Cloud are two illustrative examples of what sits in this category, mentioned only to show its shape.

**Declarative configuration and automation tooling** — the point-and-click tools administrators and consultants use to adapt the platform without custom code. Flow and process automation tooling are illustrative examples of what sits in this category.

**Integration and data-movement tooling** — systems supporting the exchange of data between this platform and outside systems. MuleSoft and a general data-loading utility are illustrative examples of what sits in this category.

**Surrounding productivity and analytics layer** — tools candidates commonly reference as part of the broader ecosystem around their core platform work. Tableau and Slack are illustrative examples of what sits in this category.

A candidate could easily reference something absent from this list — read it as a starting point for vocabulary, not a complete inventory of the ecosystem.
