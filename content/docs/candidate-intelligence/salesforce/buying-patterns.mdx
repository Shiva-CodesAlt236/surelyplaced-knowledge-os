# Buying Patterns

## Purpose

This article carries the platform-fluency self-positioning mindset from [Candidate Behaviour](/docs/candidate-intelligence/salesforce/candidate-behaviour) into concrete buying signals, hesitation patterns, and decision style for this role family, using the [Buyer Persona Schema](/docs/candidate-intelligence/BUYER_PERSONA_SCHEMA) taxonomy.

## Scope

What follows describes buying behavior at the role-family level. A working-pattern article says so explicitly wherever its own buying signals diverge from this baseline.

## Core Content

This population's buying pattern sits closest to `PERSONA-COMPARE`, though the comparison this population runs is most often internal to the ecosystem — weighing whether a role matches their actual working pattern and scope — rather than a price comparison against a competing offer. A candidate who feels accurately labeled and positioned early in a conversation tends to engage far more readily than one left to guess whether the advisor actually understands the distinction between their pattern and an adjacent one.

Hesitation most often traces back to a role being described in a way that flattens or undersells the candidate's actual scope — an Administrator-pattern candidate offered something that sounds like pure Developer work, or a Consultant-pattern candidate whose stakeholder-facing experience wasn't acknowledged, can each stall a conversation that would otherwise progress. Naming the mismatch directly and correcting it tends to resolve the hesitation quickly, since it isn't rooted in the opportunity itself.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article maps [Buyer Persona Schema](/docs/candidate-intelligence/BUYER_PERSONA_SCHEMA)'s general taxonomy onto this role family's platform-fluency mindset, following the same extension pattern used by [Buying Patterns](/docs/candidate-intelligence/healthcare/buying-patterns) in the Healthcare collection.

## Advisor Guidance

Confirm early that the role description matches the candidate's actual working pattern and scope, and if hesitation appears, check first whether it traces back to a labeling or scope mismatch before assuming it reflects a concern about the opportunity itself.

## Key Takeaways

Salesforce candidates engage most readily when a role is described in a way that matches their actual working pattern, hesitate most when that description flattens or undersells their scope, and that kind of hesitation typically resolves quickly once the mismatch is named and corrected directly.
