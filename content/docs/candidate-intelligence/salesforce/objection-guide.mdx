# Objection Guide

> No real conversation is described anywhere in this guide, and it names no price, discount, guaranteed result, or platform comparison.

## Purpose

This guide applies the existing Objection Handling module to the Salesforce role family, continuing this collection's four-stage advisor playbook.

## Scope

This guide covers objection-handling approach for Salesforce candidates broadly, building on [Trust and Credibility](/docs/objections/trust-and-credibility) and [Already Applying Myself](/docs/objections/already-applying-myself).

## Core Content

Per [Candidate Behaviour](/docs/candidate-intelligence/salesforce/candidate-behaviour), an objection that sounds like [Trust and Credibility](/docs/objections/trust-and-credibility) sometimes actually traces back to a labeling or scope concern from earlier in the conversation — check for a mismatch before responding as though the concern is about the advisor's general credibility.

Candidates who have been actively networking within the ecosystem's own community may raise something similar to [Already Applying Myself](/docs/objections/already-applying-myself) — acknowledge that effort directly, since this population's professional community is often genuinely active and candidates take real pride in their own visibility within it.

Watch for hesitation that traces back to feeling their working pattern was undersold, per [Buying Patterns](/docs/candidate-intelligence/salesforce/buying-patterns) — naming and correcting the mismatch directly usually resolves it faster than restating the opportunity's other merits.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This guide applies [Trust and Credibility](/docs/objections/trust-and-credibility) and [Already Applying Myself](/docs/objections/already-applying-myself), shaped by this collection's platform-fluency findings.

## Advisor Guidance

Check for an underlying labeling or scope mismatch before treating an objection as a general trust concern, acknowledge independent community involvement directly, and correct any scope mismatch explicitly rather than restating other merits of the opportunity.

## Key Takeaways

Objections in this role family often trace back to a labeling or scope mismatch rather than a general trust concern, are best resolved by naming and correcting that mismatch directly, and should always acknowledge a candidate's own independent involvement within their professional community.
