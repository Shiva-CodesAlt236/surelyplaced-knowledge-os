# Buying Patterns

## Purpose

This article carries the service-outcome orientation mindset from [Candidate Behaviour](/docs/candidate-intelligence/servicenow/candidate-behaviour) into concrete buying signals, hesitation patterns, and decision style for this role family, using the [Buyer Persona Schema](/docs/candidate-intelligence/BUYER_PERSONA_SCHEMA) taxonomy.

## Scope

These buying signals apply broadly at the role-family level. A working-pattern article flags any meaningful departure from this baseline in its own text.

## Core Content

This population's buying pattern sits closest to `PERSONA-VALUE-UNCERTAIN`, though the uncertainty this population resolves for is specifically about outcome, not price — they engage most readily once they can see a concrete answer to what the opportunity actually changes for them, rather than a list of features or a generic role description. An advisor who can name that outcome directly tends to move the conversation forward quickly.

Hesitation most often traces back to a role being described in narrow technical terms without a clear sense of the outcome it produces, or in language that reduces the candidate's own experience to a single tool rather than the broader service impact they've delivered. Reframing the opportunity around outcome, and correcting any narrow labeling directly, tends to resolve that hesitation.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article maps [Buyer Persona Schema](/docs/candidate-intelligence/BUYER_PERSONA_SCHEMA)'s general taxonomy onto this role family's service-outcome mindset, following the same extension pattern used by [Buying Patterns](/docs/candidate-intelligence/sap/buying-patterns) in the SAP collection.

## Advisor Guidance

Name the concrete outcome an opportunity produces early rather than leading with features or a generic role description, and if hesitation appears, check whether it traces back to narrow or reductive labeling of the candidate's actual scope of work.

## Key Takeaways

ServiceNow candidates engage most readily when the concrete outcome of an opportunity is named directly, hesitate most when a role is described in narrow technical terms without that outcome, and that hesitation typically resolves once the framing is corrected to reflect their actual service impact.
