# Full-Stack Specializations

> This article discusses full-stack engineering only at the ecosystem level. No technology combination named or implied below should be treated as a fixed or exhaustive pattern — actual scope and depth vary considerably by employer and individual candidate.

## Purpose

Full-stack candidates combine elements of the backend and frontend specializations already covered in this collection. This article describes what generally distinguishes that combination, and why it requires a slightly different evaluation lens than either specialization alone.

## Scope

This article covers full-stack engineering as a category of specialization within `ROLE-SWE`, building on [Backend Specializations](/docs/candidate-intelligence/software-engineering/backend-specializations) and [Frontend Specializations](/docs/candidate-intelligence/software-engineering/frontend-specializations) rather than repeating their content. It makes no technical claim about depth or competence in either area.

## Core Content

Full-stack engineering generally describes a candidate who works across both the server-side systems covered in [Backend Specializations](/docs/candidate-intelligence/software-engineering/backend-specializations) and the interface-facing work covered in [Frontend Specializations](/docs/candidate-intelligence/software-engineering/frontend-specializations), rather than specializing exclusively in one.

The breadth this implies varies enormously by individual — some full-stack candidates have deep experience in both areas, while others have broader but shallower exposure across both, and this repository has no basis for assuming which applies to a given candidate. This is precisely the kind of technical depth question that should be routed to internal technical SMEs rather than inferred from the "full-stack" label, consistent with [Technology Mapping](/docs/candidate-intelligence/TECHNOLOGY_MAPPING).

Full-stack candidates sometimes describe their career interest in terms of flexibility and end-to-end ownership of a project, which can be a useful discovery thread distinct from the narrower technical-specialization framing that applies to backend- or frontend-only candidates.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article extends [Backend Specializations](/docs/candidate-intelligence/software-engineering/backend-specializations) and [Frontend Specializations](/docs/candidate-intelligence/software-engineering/frontend-specializations) rather than duplicating their ecosystem-level content, and remains consistent with the non-invention standard in [Technology Mapping](/docs/candidate-intelligence/TECHNOLOGY_MAPPING).

## Advisor Guidance

Avoid assuming equal depth across both backend and frontend work simply because a candidate identifies as full-stack — ask directly, consistent with [Understanding Candidate Goals](/docs/discovery/understanding-candidate-goals), rather than inferring breadth or depth from the label. Consider surfacing the candidate's interest in flexibility or end-to-end ownership as a discovery thread distinct from a narrower technical specialization.

## Key Takeaways

Full-stack candidates combine backend and frontend work in a proportion that varies considerably by individual — never assumed from the label alone — and may respond well to a discovery conversation framed around flexibility and end-to-end ownership rather than a single narrow technical specialization.
