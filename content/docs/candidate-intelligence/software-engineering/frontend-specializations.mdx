# Frontend Specializations

> This article discusses frontend engineering only at the ecosystem level. Any technology named below is an illustrative example of a category, not an endorsement, ranking, or exhaustive list — actual technologies and interview expectations differ from employer to employer.

## Purpose

This article describes what generally distinguishes a frontend specialization at the ecosystem level, giving an advisor enough pattern recognition to have an informed conversation without requiring technical fluency.

## Scope

This article covers frontend engineering as a category of specialization within `ROLE-SWE`. It does not evaluate any specific framework or library technically, and it makes no claim about which frontend technology is more in-demand, more valuable, or better suited to any particular employer.

## Core Content

Frontend engineering generally centers on what a user directly sees and interacts with — interface behavior, visual layout, and the experience of using an application, as distinct from the backend systems powering it behind the scenes. Candidates in this specialization often describe their work in terms of user interfaces, interactivity, and design collaboration.

At the ecosystem level, frontend work commonly involves a client-side scripting language and often a framework or library built on top of it (illustrative examples include ecosystems such as React, Angular, or Vue, among many others) — but which specific tools a given candidate has used, and how deep their experience with each one runs, varies by employer and project and should never be assumed.

Frontend-focused candidates often bring a design-adjacent, user-experience-aware perspective to how they evaluate a service, sometimes paying close attention to how clearly and cleanly information is presented during a call itself, consistent with the general evaluation patterns described in [Software Engineering Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/software-engineering-playbook).

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article extends [Software Engineering Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/software-engineering-playbook) and [Technology Mapping](/docs/candidate-intelligence/TECHNOLOGY_MAPPING) specifically to the frontend specialization, introducing no new technical claim beyond what those articles already establish generally.

## Advisor Guidance

Record a candidate's stated frontend focus in `technology_summary` using their own words, consistent with [Technology Mapping](/docs/candidate-intelligence/TECHNOLOGY_MAPPING), rather than assuming a specific framework from the "frontend" label. Route genuine technical evaluation questions to internal SMEs, and consider that this candidate may be evaluating the clarity and presentation quality of the conversation itself as part of their overall impression.

Frontend engineering candidates generally focus on interface and user-experience work, and may pay particular attention to presentation clarity during the sales conversation itself — but the specific frameworks and tools involved vary by employer and should never be assumed from the specialization label alone.
