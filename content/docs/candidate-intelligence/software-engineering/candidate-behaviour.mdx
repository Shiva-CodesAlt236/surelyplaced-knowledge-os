# Candidate Behaviour

> This article describes general behavioral patterns across a broad role family, not a fixed personality profile. Any individual candidate may differ from these patterns, and this article should never substitute for genuine discovery conversation.

## Purpose

[Candidate Mindset](/docs/candidate-intelligence/reference-profile/candidate-mindset) describes one specific archetype's mindset in depth. This article generalizes that pattern across the full `ROLE-SWE` role family — across specializations, visa categories, and career stages — so an advisor can recognize common behavioral threads without assuming every software engineering candidate matches the Sprint 12 reference case exactly.

## Scope

This article covers general behavioral tendencies across software engineering candidates as a role family. It does not describe any real or specific candidate, and it does not claim these patterns apply universally — they are tendencies worth watching for, not fixed rules.

## Core Content

Across specializations, software engineering candidates as a group tend to bring a comparison-driven, evidence-seeking evaluation style to decisions generally, consistent with the `PERSONA-SKEPTIC` pattern in [Buyer Persona Schema](/docs/candidate-intelligence/BUYER_PERSONA_SCHEMA) — favoring specific, verifiable answers over general reassurance. This holds across backend, frontend, full-stack, and mobile specializations, though it may show up differently depending on the individual.

Career stage meaningfully shapes how this pattern presents. An early-career candidate, consistent with the Sprint 12 reference profile, often combines this skepticism with less experience navigating a major independent decision — sometimes producing more visible hesitation even when the underlying evaluation style is the same. A more experienced candidate may apply the same evidence-seeking approach with more confidence and less visible hesitation, having navigated significant career decisions before.

Visa category introduces its own layer of urgency that operates somewhat independently of specialization or career stage — an H-1B candidate's considerations, for example, differ meaningfully from an F-1 OPT candidate's, even if both are backend engineers at similar career stages. See the relevant Visa Playbook for the specific visa category a candidate holds rather than assuming urgency patterns from role alone.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article generalizes [Software Engineering Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/software-engineering-playbook) and the Sprint 12 [Candidate Mindset](/docs/candidate-intelligence/reference-profile/candidate-mindset) article across the broader role family, and defers to the relevant Visa Playbook for visa-specific urgency rather than restating it.

## Advisor Guidance

Expect the evidence-seeking evaluation style broadly across this role family, but calibrate expected confidence and visible hesitation to the candidate's actual career stage rather than assuming early-career patterns apply universally. Always confirm the candidate's specific visa category and consult the matching Visa Playbook rather than inferring urgency from role classification alone.
