# Buying Patterns

> This article describes general financial decision-making tendencies across a broad role family, not a fixed prediction for any individual. No specific price, discount, or return figure appears anywhere in this article.

## Purpose

This article generalizes the financial decision-making patterns established in Pricing and demonstrated for one archetype in [Reference Profile: Candidate Mindset](/docs/candidate-intelligence/reference-profile/candidate-mindset) across the full software engineering role family.

## Scope

This article covers general buying-pattern tendencies across software engineering candidates. It does not state or imply any actual pricing, discount, or return figure, and it does not claim these patterns apply to every individual candidate.

## Core Content

Across the role family, the comparison-objection and value-uncertainty patterns described in [Handling Price Objections](/docs/pricing/handling-price-objections) are common, consistent with this role family's generally evidence-seeking evaluation style described in [Candidate Behaviour](/docs/candidate-intelligence/software-engineering/candidate-behaviour). A direct request for a specific ROI figure is a frequent pattern across specializations and career stages, requiring the discipline in [ROI Conversations](/docs/pricing/roi-conversations) regardless of whether the candidate is early-career or experienced.

Career stage and financial independence interact meaningfully here. An early-career candidate, as in the Sprint 12 reference profile, may be navigating a major independent financial decision for the first time, which can heighten the guilt/fear/hope dynamic described in [Investment Psychology](/docs/pricing/investment-psychology). A more experienced candidate with established financial footing may approach the same conversation with less of that heightened emotional weight, though this varies by individual circumstance and should never be assumed.

A financial co-decision-maker, as covered in [Financial Decision-Makers](/docs/pricing/financial-decision-makers), is statistically more likely to be relevant for earlier-career candidates than for established professionals, though this too should be confirmed directly with each candidate rather than assumed from career stage alone.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article generalizes [Handling Price Objections](/docs/pricing/handling-price-objections), [ROI Conversations](/docs/pricing/roi-conversations), [Investment Psychology](/docs/pricing/investment-psychology), and [Financial Decision-Makers](/docs/pricing/financial-decision-makers) across the software engineering role family, introducing no new pricing concept beyond what those articles already establish.

## Advisor Guidance

Expect comparison and value-uncertainty objections broadly across this role family, and hold the ROI Conversations discipline regardless of a candidate's apparent seniority or confidence. Calibrate sensitivity to first-time major financial decisions and potential co-decision-makers based on the candidate's actual stated career stage and circumstances, not an assumption from their specialization or role classification.

## Key Takeaways

Software engineering candidates broadly tend toward comparison and value-uncertainty objections and frequently request a specific ROI figure, but the emotional weight of the financial decision and the likelihood of a co-decision-maker vary meaningfully with career stage and individual circumstance — never assumed from role classification alone.
