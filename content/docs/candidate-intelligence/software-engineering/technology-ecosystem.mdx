# Technology Ecosystem

> This article names technology categories, not specific products, as illustrative examples only. It does not evaluate, rank, or recommend any specific tool, and it does not state what any employer specifically requires — those expectations vary by employer and role.

## Purpose

[Backend](/docs/candidate-intelligence/software-engineering/backend-specializations), [Frontend](/docs/candidate-intelligence/software-engineering/frontend-specializations), [Full-Stack](/docs/candidate-intelligence/software-engineering/fullstack-specializations), and [Mobile](/docs/candidate-intelligence/software-engineering/mobile-specializations) each touch on specialization-specific technology categories. This article covers the categories that cut across all four, so they aren't repeated separately in each specialization article.

## Scope

This article names general technology categories relevant to software engineering broadly — version control, cloud infrastructure, testing, and collaborative development practices — without naming or evaluating specific products within each category as anything more than an illustrative example.

## Core Content

**Version control** is a near-universal practice across all four specializations — candidates across backend, frontend, full-stack, and mobile work are generally expected to have some familiarity with tracking and collaborating on code changes, regardless of specialization.

**Cloud infrastructure** exposure is increasingly common, particularly for backend and full-stack candidates, though the specific platform and depth of experience varies enormously and should never be assumed.

**Testing practices** — writing and running automated checks on code — appear across specializations in varying forms and depth, and are often a genuine point of interview evaluation that sits outside what an advisor should assess directly.

**Collaborative development practices** — code review, working within a team's established workflow — matter across every specialization and are sometimes more relevant to a candidate's actual job readiness than any specific technology choice.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article is the cross-cutting companion to the four specialization articles in this collection, consolidating the categories that would otherwise be repeated in each. It stays consistent with [Technology Mapping](/docs/candidate-intelligence/TECHNOLOGY_MAPPING)'s non-evaluative standard throughout.

## Advisor Guidance

Use this article as general orientation, not as an interview-readiness checklist — genuine assessment of a candidate's standing in any of these categories belongs to internal technical SMEs. When a candidate raises a specific tool or platform, record it factually in `technology_summary` rather than evaluating it, consistent with [Technology Mapping](/docs/candidate-intelligence/TECHNOLOGY_MAPPING).
