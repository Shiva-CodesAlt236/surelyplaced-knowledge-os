# SOC Analyst

> Platform categories such as SIEM tooling are named below purely as illustrative examples. No specific product is evaluated, compared, or recommended — actual platforms and shift structures vary by employer.

## Purpose

Security Operations Center analyst work is a distinct, more structured pattern within `ROLE-SEC`. This article describes what generally sets it apart from the broader [Security Analyst](/docs/candidate-intelligence/cybersecurity/security-analyst) pattern.

## Scope

This article covers the SOC analyst pattern specifically. It does not evaluate any specific SIEM or monitoring platform, and takes no position on which is better suited to any given environment.

## Core Content

SOC analyst work generally centers on real-time monitoring, triage, and initial response to security alerts, often organized around tiered escalation levels and shift-based coverage — a more structured, continuous-operations pattern than the broader security analyst role.

At the ecosystem level, this pattern commonly involves a security information and event management category of platform for aggregating and reviewing alerts, and some familiarity with incident-response procedures — but the specific platform, tier structure, and shift arrangement all vary considerably by employer and should never be assumed.

SOC analysts, given the often high-volume, procedural nature of the work, sometimes evaluate a career change through the lens of sustainability and growth path — whether a new role offers a way to move beyond entry-tier monitoring work over time — which can be a valuable discovery thread distinct from the broader analyst pattern's risk-framing focus.

SOC analyst work is a structured, shift-based, tiered-response pattern within cybersecurity, and candidates in this pattern often care specifically about growth beyond entry-tier work — while the specific monitoring platform and shift structure vary by employer and should never be assumed from the title.
