# Objection Guide

> Nothing in this guide describes a real conversation, and no price, discount, or guarantee is stated anywhere.

## Purpose

This guide routes the objection patterns most likely across `ROLE-SEC` to existing Objections and Pricing guidance.

## Scope

This guide covers likely objection patterns for cybersecurity candidates broadly, without introducing new objection-handling technique.

## Core Content

**Comparison objections**, often backed by more independent research than average given this role family's professional habits per [Buying Patterns](/docs/candidate-intelligence/cybersecurity/buying-patterns), are common — apply [Handling Price Objections](/docs/pricing/handling-price-objections), engaging the candidate's research directly rather than being caught off guard by its depth.

**Trust or credibility objections** may surface more explicitly here than in other role families, given this group's professional training in spotting inconsistency — apply [Trust and Credibility](/docs/objections/trust-and-credibility) with particular care, since a single inconsistency noticed by this role family can be more damaging than elsewhere.

**Value-uncertainty objections** framed as a request for a specific outcome figure are common — apply [ROI Conversations](/docs/pricing/roi-conversations) precisely.

**Visa-timeline urgency** varies by specific visa category rather than by cybersecurity pattern — route to the matching Visa Playbook.

## Cross-Module Alignment

Every pattern named here routes to existing Objections, Pricing, or Visa Playbook guidance — this guide introduces no technique unique to cybersecurity.

## Advisor Guidance

Take trust and credibility objections from this role family especially seriously, engage independent research directly rather than dismissively, and hold the ROI Conversations discipline precisely.
