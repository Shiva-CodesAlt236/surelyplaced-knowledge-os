# Cloud Architect

## Purpose

This article describes the Cloud Architect working pattern within `ROLE-CLOUD` — candidates whose focus has shifted from hands-on implementation toward cross-team design and strategy for cloud infrastructure.

## Scope

This article covers what typically distinguishes an architecture-focused candidate from the other three working patterns in this collection, for sales-conversation purposes only.

## Core Content

Candidates in this pattern typically have moved further into design and decision-making than the other three patterns in this collection — they describe their work in terms of how systems should be structured, how teams should work within a given cloud environment, and how tradeoffs between cost, scalability, and reliability get decided at a broader level. This pattern most often represents a later career stage, frequently reached by candidates who started in one of the other three patterns and grew into a more strategic role over time.

Because this pattern sits closer to organizational decision-making, candidates here sometimes evaluate a career move partly through a lens of scope and influence, not just technical fit — questions about the breadth of a role's decision-making authority can matter as much as questions about the specific technology involved.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This pattern represents the most senior end of the spectrum the [Cloud & DevOps Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/cloud-devops-playbook) describes in general terms, and often traces a career path upward from [Infrastructure Engineer](/docs/candidate-intelligence/cloud-devops/infrastructure-engineer) or [DevOps / Platform Engineer](/docs/candidate-intelligence/cloud-devops/devops-platform-engineer).

## Advisor Guidance

Ask about scope and decision-making authority, not just technology, when discovery touches this pattern. Recognize that a candidate in this pattern may be weighing a career move as much on influence and breadth as on the specific technical environment.

Cloud Architects operate at a design-and-strategy level above the other three working patterns in this collection, often represent a later career stage reached from within this same role family, and may weigh a career move partly on scope and decision-making authority rather than technology alone.
