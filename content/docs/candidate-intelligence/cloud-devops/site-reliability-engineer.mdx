# Site Reliability Engineer

## Purpose

This article describes the Site Reliability Engineer (SRE) working pattern within `ROLE-CLOUD` — candidates centered on keeping systems running reliably and measuring how well they do it.

## Scope

This article covers what typically distinguishes an SRE-focused candidate from the other three working patterns in this collection, for sales-conversation purposes only.

## Core Content

Candidates in this pattern describe their work through the lens of reliability: uptime, incident response, on-call rotations, and the practice of turning "is it working" into something measurable. Many will speak naturally in terms of targets and thresholds for how reliable a system needs to be, and a good number will describe a specific incident or outage as a formative professional experience — not as a failure story, but as evidence of how they think under pressure.

This pattern tends to carry a distinct emotional register compared to the other three: candidates who've spent time on-call often talk about sustainability and pace of work as a real career consideration, not just compensation or title. An advisor who picks up on this and treats it as a legitimate priority — rather than steering the conversation back toward advancement alone — tends to build faster trust with this pattern specifically.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This pattern maps to the reliability and operational-ownership themes named generally in the [Cloud & DevOps Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/cloud-devops-playbook) and gives more specific shape to the "keeping something running" identity that playbook describes only briefly.

## Advisor Guidance

Take a mention of career sustainability or on-call burnout seriously as a real discovery signal, not as a tangent from the main conversation. Ask what "reliable" means in the candidate's own context rather than assuming a specific uptime target or measurement approach.

## Key Takeaways

Site Reliability Engineers center their work on uptime, incident response, and measurable reliability, often carry a genuine career-sustainability priority worth taking seriously in discovery, and are distinguished from Infrastructure Engineers by their emphasis on operating and measuring systems rather than building them.
