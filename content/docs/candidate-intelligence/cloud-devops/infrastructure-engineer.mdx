# Infrastructure Engineer

## Purpose

This article describes the Infrastructure Engineer working pattern within `ROLE-CLOUD` — candidates whose focus sits at the systems and networking layer that everything else runs on.

## Scope

This article covers what typically distinguishes an infrastructure-focused candidate from the other three working patterns in this collection, for sales-conversation purposes only.

## Core Content

Candidates in this pattern tend to describe their work in terms of servers, networking, storage, and the foundational layer that platform and application teams build on top of. Many have a background that predates widespread cloud adoption and have carried that systems-level thinking forward into cloud environments, while others came up entirely cloud-native. What distinguishes this pattern from [Site Reliability Engineer](/docs/candidate-intelligence/cloud-devops/site-reliability-engineer) is emphasis: infrastructure-focused candidates tend to talk about what they built and provisioned, while SRE-focused candidates tend to talk about what they keep running and how they measure it.

This pattern often overlaps with hybrid or on-premises-to-cloud migration work, which can give these candidates a distinctive vantage point — they've frequently seen both older and newer approaches to the same problem and can speak to the tradeoffs in practical terms.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This pattern maps to the general infrastructure emphasis described in the [Cloud & DevOps Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/cloud-devops-playbook) and shares its foundational-layer framing with [Backend Specializations](/docs/candidate-intelligence/software-engineering/backend-specializations) in the Software Engineering collection, though the infrastructure pattern here sits one layer further down, closer to the systems the backend runs on.

## Advisor Guidance

Let the candidate describe their systems in their own terms rather than assuming a specific cloud platform or on-premises setup. Ask what layer they're actually responsible for — provisioning, networking, storage — rather than assuming "infrastructure" means one specific thing.

## Key Takeaways

Infrastructure Engineers focus on the systems and networking layer beneath applications and platforms, often bring a hybrid or migration-era vantage point, and are best understood through what they've provisioned and built rather than assumed to fit one specific platform or setup.
