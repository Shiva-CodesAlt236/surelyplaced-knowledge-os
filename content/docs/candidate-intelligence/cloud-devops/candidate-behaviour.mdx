# Candidate Behaviour

## Purpose

This article describes the mindset and communication style that most consistently shows up across the four Cloud & DevOps working patterns, giving this collection's sales intelligence a distinct anchor separate from the Software Engineering, Data & AI, and Cybersecurity collections.

## Scope

This article covers general behavioural tendencies at the role-family level. Individual working-pattern articles note where a specific pattern departs from this baseline.

## Core Content

Where Software Engineering candidates tend toward general evidence-seeking skepticism and Cybersecurity candidates carry a professional habit of spotting inconsistency, Cloud & DevOps candidates are most consistently defined by an identity built around operational ownership — a habit of describing their work through what they're responsible for keeping running, building, or automating, rather than through job titles alone. That ownership identity tends to travel with a strong preference for directness: this population evaluates conversations, including sales conversations, on efficiency, and tends to disengage from anything that reads as a long, generic pitch.

A second consistent trait is sensitivity to imprecise technical language. Candidates in this field have often been miscategorized by recruiters who use infrastructure and platform terminology loosely, and that history tends to make them alert — sometimes visibly so — when a conversation uses a term incorrectly or interchangeably with something else. This isn't hostility; it's closer to the same precision instinct that makes them effective at their actual work, applied reflexively to how they're being spoken to.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article extends the general mindset and buying-behaviour observations already made at the industry level in the [Cloud & DevOps Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/cloud-devops-playbook), giving them a more specific behavioural frame consistent with how the Software Engineering, Data & AI, and Cybersecurity collections each anchor their own sales intelligence in a distinct theme.

## Advisor Guidance

Use precise, correct technical vocabulary or ask rather than guess, keep the conversation efficient and specific rather than general, and let the candidate define their own ownership and responsibilities rather than assuming a title tells you everything.
