# Payment & Agreement Process

> **Honesty note:** This repository does not yet contain SurelyPlaced's specific pricing, payment terms, contract or agreement language, refund policy, or enrollment paperwork process. This lesson does not invent any of that. It teaches the part that doesn't depend on it: how to communicate honestly during this stage, and where to direct a candidate's specific questions instead of guessing.

Once a candidate says yes, the conversation shifts from selling to operations. Trust is reinforced or quietly damaged right here, depending on how honestly this stage is handled.

**Why this matters:** the moment right after a yes is the easiest moment to fill a knowledge gap with a guess that sounds reasonable — and the easiest moment for that guess to quietly damage trust.
**When to use this:** immediately after a candidate says yes, before any payment or agreement detail gets discussed.
**What skill this builds:** stating clearly what you know for certain and routing anything you don't to the accurate source, without ever approximating.
**What happens next:** once next steps are honestly confirmed, the closing call moves to its final checklist to make sure nothing was left vague.

## Lesson Objective

- Confirm the decision plainly before moving into logistics
- State clearly what you know and what you don't — never approximate
- Direct pricing, refund, and contract questions to the correct current resource, never memory

## Coaching Notes

<CoachingNotes title="Coaching Notes">
Keep this stage calm and unhurried. Neha just made a real decision — she deserves a steady, clear handoff, not a rushed pivot into paperwork.
</CoachingNotes>

<CoachingNotes title="Why This Worked">
Being explicit about the boundary — "here's what I know, here's what needs to come from elsewhere" — is more trustworthy than a confident-sounding guess. Candidates remember being told the truth far longer than they remember a smooth answer.
</CoachingNotes>
