# Decision to Enrollment

A closing call isn't a separate persuasion event bolted onto the end. It's the natural conclusion of discovery and discussion — and it should feel that way to the candidate.

## Lesson Objective

- Confirm readiness honestly before asking for anything
- Sequence the call: readiness, then the ask, then respond to the real answer
- Treat both "yes" and "not now" as legitimate, complete outcomes

<ManagerTip from="Aayush" priority="important">
When a candidate hands you a clean "I'm ready, no open questions," don't respond with more selling. That's the moment to ask, plainly, and nothing else. Adding explanation here reads as if you didn't believe him.
</ManagerTip>

## Coaching Notes

<CoachingNotes title="Coaching Notes">
"Where are you at with this?" is open-ended on purpose. A closed question like "are you ready to sign up today?" skips past real information — it invites a yes/no before you know what's actually true.
</CoachingNotes>

<CoachingNotes title="Why This Worked">
Naming something specific Rahul actually did — checking his STEM extension — proves the readiness check wasn't a formality. It's the same principle as the Discussion recap: precision proves you were paying attention.
</CoachingNotes>
