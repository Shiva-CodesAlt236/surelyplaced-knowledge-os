# Closing Call Checklist

## Purpose

This article closes out the Closing Calls module the same way the Discovery and Discussion checklists close out their own modules — a practical check run near the end of a call to confirm it was handled well, regardless of whether the outcome was a yes, a no, or a "not yet."

The checklist itself:

1. Did I confirm readiness honestly before asking for a decision, rather than assuming it?
2. Was the ask itself clear and direct — not buried in a longer sentence?
3. Did I hold the silence after the ask, rather than filling it?
4. If hesitation came up, did I find out what was actually behind it before responding?
5. If the answer was "not now," did I set a specific follow-up rather than an open-ended one?
6. If the answer was "yes," did I communicate the agreement and payment stage honestly, without guessing at details I don't actually know?
7. Did I avoid stating any pricing, guarantee, statistic, or policy I wasn't certain was accurate?
8. Does the candidate have a clear, accurate sense of what happens next, whatever the outcome was?

If any answer isn't a clear yes, that's useful information about what to address before considering the call complete — not a sign the call failed.

## Learning Objectives

- Use this checklist as a genuine check at the end of a closing call, not a formality
- Confirm that the call's outcome — yes, no, or not yet — was handled with the same care regardless of which one it was
- Identify when a closing call needs a specific, scheduled follow-up rather than an open-ended one
- Catch any moment where an advisor may have stated something as fact without being sure it was accurate
- End every closing call with the candidate having a clear, correct understanding of what happens next

## When To Use

- In the final minutes of every closing call, regardless of outcome
- When reviewing a closing call afterward, either on your own or with a manager, to check it was handled well
- When training a newer advisor on what a complete, honest closing call actually looks like

## Conversation Framework

Run through the checklist mentally as the call nears its end, and confirm the outcome-specific items directly with the candidate — what happens next should never be left as something the advisor assumes the candidate understood.

If the call ended in hesitation or "not now," use the final minutes to set a specific, real follow-up rather than letting the call end on an open-ended note.

If the call ended in a yes, use the final minutes to confirm the candidate has an honest, accurate picture of what comes next in the agreement and payment stage — including anything you were honest about not being able to confirm yourself.

## Best Practices

- Treat every outcome — yes, no, not yet — as equally deserving of a clear, complete close to the call.
- Confirm next steps out loud rather than assuming a good overall feeling about the call means the candidate is clear on what happens next.
- If you're unsure whether something you said during the call was fully accurate, flag it to yourself and follow up rather than letting it go unchecked.
- Don't let a call that's run long skip the final confirmation — it matters more, not less, when time is short.
- Use this checklist as a running background check throughout the call, not only a review at the very end.

## Common Mistakes

- Ending a call on a "not now" without setting a specific follow-up date
- Assuming a "yes" means the call is essentially over, and rushing past a clear explanation of next steps
- Failing to notice, in the moment, that something stated during the call wasn't actually confirmed as accurate
- Treating this checklist as something to run through silently without ever confirming the key items with the candidate directly
- Letting a difficult or emotional call end abruptly instead of closing it with the same care as any other

## Examples

**Confirming next steps after a "yes":**

> **Advisor:** "Before we finish up, I want to make sure you're clear on what happens next."
> **Candidate:** "I think so — you're going to get me the exact agreement and payment details rather than tell me from memory, right?"
> **Advisor:** "Exactly right. That's coming directly and accurately, not guessed at on this call."

**Confirming next steps after a "not now":**

> **Advisor:** "So just to close the loop — we said a week, is that still right?"
> **Candidate:** "Yeah, a week sounds good."
> **Advisor:** "Good, I'll reach out then. And if anything comes up before that, you don't need to wait for me."

## Key Takeaways

A closing call is complete when its outcome — whatever it was — was handled with clarity and honesty, and when the candidate leaves the call knowing exactly what happens next. Confirm this directly rather than assuming it from a good overall feeling, and never let uncertainty about a specific detail go unflagged. A well-closed "not now" is just as much a successful call as a well-closed "yes."
