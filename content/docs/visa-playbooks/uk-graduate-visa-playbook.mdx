# UK Graduate Visa Playbook

> This is a sales playbook, not immigration guidance, and it follows the shared standard in [Visa Discovery Framework](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-discovery-framework). Neither the length of this permission nor its specific eligibility rules appear anywhere in this article; those live in the candidate's own paperwork and in the guidance of a qualified immigration adviser.

## Candidate Profile

Candidates on the UK Graduate route are typically recent graduates of a UK institution using a period of permission to work that isn't tied to a specific employer. This is often, though not always, their first work authorization in the UK, and it's generally understood to be a transitional stage — most candidates using it will eventually need employer sponsorship to continue working in the UK long-term.

## Typical Mindset

Because this permission isn't employer-tied, candidates on this route often feel more immediate freedom than candidates managing sponsor-tied status elsewhere — but many are also aware, at some level, that this stage doesn't last indefinitely and that securing a sponsoring employer is the real goal underneath the current freedom. That awareness can create a specific kind of background pressure that doesn't always show up as urgency early on but tends to sharpen as time passes.

## Buying Motivation

The core motivation is usually finding a role with an employer who both wants to hire the candidate and is positioned to support sponsorship when the time comes. Candidates are often motivated to move efficiently precisely because they know this window of employer-independent flexibility is temporary, even without stating an exact deadline.

## Common Pain Points

- Difficulty identifying which employers are realistically able to support sponsorship, versus those who simply aren't set up to do so
- Uncertainty about how much time they genuinely have to find the right role before needing to make a less ideal decision
- A sense of competing against other graduates for a limited pool of sponsorship-capable roles
- Sometimes, underestimating how early they need to start engaging with the sponsorship question, since the current permission doesn't require it immediately

## Discovery Questions

1. "What's your current visa status, and do you know roughly how your timeline is structured?"
2. "Have you started looking specifically at employers who are able to sponsor going forward, or has your search been broader so far?"
3. "What's your understanding of what needs to happen before your current permission ends?"
4. "Have you spoken with an immigration adviser about your options?"
5. "Is there anything about your timeline or next steps that feels unclear to you?"

## Qualification Signals

- The candidate is aware that sponsorship is the eventual goal, even if they haven't fully mapped out the timeline
- The candidate can describe their field and target roles with real specificity
- The candidate has documentation or a clear reference point for their current status, even if they defer exact details to it rather than guessing

## Risk Signals

- The candidate is unsure whether their current permission is still valid or how much time may remain
- The candidate hasn't considered the sponsorship question at all and seems to be treating current flexibility as open-ended
- The candidate describes a plan that assumes a specific employer will sponsor them without having confirmed that employer's ability or willingness to do so

If any of these come up, pause and see [Visa Red Flags](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-red-flags) before continuing the sales conversation.

## Presentation Strategy

Help the candidate see the sponsorship question clearly and early, without creating false urgency — the goal is genuine strategic clarity, not panic. Be specific about how the program approaches identifying employers relevant to sponsorship goals, without promising or implying that any specific employer's sponsorship decision can be predicted or guaranteed.

## Objection Patterns

Candidates who haven't yet engaged with the sponsorship question may initially respond with a version of [Already Applying Myself](/docs/objections/already-applying-myself), not yet realizing how the sponsorship dimension changes the search. As the sponsorship question becomes clearer, questions about certainty of outcome are also common — see [No Guarantee Concern](/docs/objections/no-guarantee-concern).

## Closing Strategy

Confirm the candidate has a clear, personally-verified understanding of their own timeline before closing — from their documentation or an immigration adviser, not from the advisor's assumption. Make sure the candidate understands the sponsorship dimension of their search clearly enough that the close reflects genuine strategic readiness, not just general enthusiasm.

## Advisor Mistakes

- Treating this category the same as an employer-tied status, when the current flexibility is real and shouldn't be minimized
- Failing to help the candidate see the sponsorship question early enough, if it hasn't yet occurred to them
- Promising or implying that a specific employer will sponsor a candidate
- Creating urgency that isn't grounded in the candidate's actual, confirmed timeline

## Key Takeaways

Candidates on the UK Graduate route often have more immediate flexibility than employer-tied categories, but most are working toward an eventual need for employer sponsorship — help them see that clearly and early, without manufacturing urgency that isn't grounded in their actual timeline. Never state this permission's length or eligibility criteria; confirm those against the candidate's own documentation and qualified immigration counsel.
