# Visa Playbook Checklist

> This checklist confirms a conversation stayed within the sales-playbook standard this module is built on — it does not confirm anything about a candidate's actual immigration eligibility, which is never an advisor's determination to make.

## Candidate Profile

This article applies to any candidate whose category was discussed using this module, across any of the seven playbooks it contains.

## Typical Mindset

By the end of a conversation that's touched on work authorization, most candidates are gauging whether the advisor handled the topic with genuine care and accuracy, or with vague reassurance. This checklist exists to make sure the former actually happened.

## Buying Motivation

Not applicable in the usual sense — this article is a quality check, not a candidate-facing conversation. It exists to protect the buying motivation established elsewhere in the funnel by making sure nothing said during a visa-related discussion undermined it.

## Common Pain Points

- Advisors under time pressure may be tempted to smooth over a status question rather than confirm it properly
- It can be hard, in the moment, to remember every relevant category-specific consideration from the playbook that applied
- Reviewing a call after the fact for accuracy takes discipline that's easy to skip when a call otherwise went well

## Discovery Questions

The checklist itself, run as self-directed questions after any call that touched work authorization:

1. Did I confirm the candidate's category directly, rather than assuming it from context?
2. Did I avoid stating any specific duration, eligibility rule, or process detail as fact?
3. If a genuine red flag came up, did I pause the sales conversation and redirect to qualified counsel?
4. Did I use the candidate's own documentation and understanding as the source of truth, rather than my own assumption?
5. Did I avoid promising or implying any outcome tied to a specific employer's sponsorship decision?
6. Is there anything I said today that I'm not fully certain was accurate?

## Qualification Signals

A conversation passes this checklist when every question above can be answered with a clear yes, and any red flag that surfaced was handled by pausing and redirecting rather than continuing.

## Risk Signals

- Any "no" or uncertain answer to the checklist above
- A call where a category was assumed rather than confirmed
- A call where a red flag was noticed but not acted on, because the conversation was going well otherwise

## Presentation Strategy

Run this checklist as a habit immediately after any call that touched work authorization, while the details are still fresh, rather than relying on memory later. If something needs correcting with the candidate, do it proactively rather than waiting for them to notice.

## Objection Patterns

Not applicable — this is an internal quality check, not a candidate-facing conversation.

## Closing Strategy

If this checklist surfaces a gap — an unconfirmed category, an unresolved red flag, an uncertain claim — treat it as something to correct before any closing conversation proceeds, not as an administrative afterthought.

## Advisor Mistakes

- Skipping this checklist because a call felt like it went smoothly
- Treating a "yes, that sounds right" from a candidate as full confirmation without having actually asked the specific questions this module recommends
- Noticing a red flag in hindsight and not following up to correct course
- Using this checklist to judge whether a candidate is a good fit, rather than to judge whether the advisor's own conduct met the standard

## Key Takeaways

This checklist isn't about judging a candidate's eligibility — it's about confirming an advisor stayed within the sales-playbook standard: confirm rather than assume, defer specifics to the candidate's own documentation, and pause and redirect the moment a genuine red flag appears. Run it as a habit after every call that touches work authorization, not just when something felt off.
