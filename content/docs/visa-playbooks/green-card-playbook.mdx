# Green Card Playbook

> This is a sales playbook, not immigration guidance. The [Visa Discovery Framework](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-discovery-framework) sets the standard this article follows. "Green card" spans everything from lawful permanent residents to candidates at an early stage of an application, and this article does not assume or state which one applies to any individual candidate — that determination rests with the candidate's own paperwork and their own immigration counsel.

## Candidate Profile

This category spans a genuinely wide range: candidates who are already lawful permanent residents with status independent of any employer, and candidates who are at some earlier stage of an employment-based or other green card process, which may still involve some degree of employer dependency. These are meaningfully different situations, and the label "green card" alone doesn't tell you which one applies.

## Typical Mindset

Candidates who already hold permanent residency often approach a job search with more freedom and less status-driven anxiety than candidates earlier in the process — the urgency, if present, is usually about career goals rather than status risk. Candidates still in process may carry some of the same employer-related caution seen in earlier categories, depending on exactly where they are.

## Buying Motivation

For candidates with settled status, motivation tends to center on genuine career advancement — better title, better compensation, better fit — without the complicating factor of status risk shaping every option. For candidates still in process, motivation may be more mixed, sometimes including a desire to make a move before or after a particular stage of their process, depending on their specific situation.

## Common Pain Points

- For candidates in process, uncertainty about how a job change might interact with their pending case, without a clear answer they trust
- For candidates with settled status, sometimes an assumption from employers or advisors that they still carry status-related constraints they no longer have
- Difficulty finding a program or advisor who takes the time to understand exactly where they are in this spectrum, rather than treating "green card" as one single situation

## Discovery Questions

1. "Where are you in the green card process — do you already have your green card, or are you at an earlier stage?"
2. "If you're still in process, is your case tied to your current employer in any way?"
3. "Has anyone advised you on how a job change might interact with your current situation?"
4. "What's actually driving this search — is it more about opportunity, or is there something status-related shaping the timing?"
5. "Is there anything about your situation that still feels unclear to you?"

## Qualification Signals

- The candidate can clearly describe which stage of the spectrum they're in — settled status or in-process, and if in-process, roughly how
- The candidate's stated motivation is coherent with their described stage (career-driven for settled status, more nuanced for in-process candidates)
- The candidate has documentation or a clear reference point for their situation, even if they defer some specifics to their own paperwork

## Risk Signals

- The candidate is unsure whether they currently hold permanent residency or are still in an earlier stage
- The candidate describes a job change plan that could interact with a pending process, without having confirmed how
- The candidate is relying on informal advice about how their process works rather than their own documentation or counsel

If any of these come up, pause and see [Visa Red Flags](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-red-flags) before continuing the sales conversation.

## Presentation Strategy

Tailor the conversation entirely to where the candidate actually is in the spectrum — a candidate with settled status should be treated like any other candidate with independent status, while a candidate still in process may need the same care around employer-tied risk seen in earlier categories. Never assume the more favorable end of the spectrum without confirming it.

## Objection Patterns

Candidates with settled status sometimes raise a version of [Already Applying Myself](/docs/objections/already-applying-myself), since they may have more freedom and confidence to search independently. Candidates still in process may raise concerns closer to those seen in [H-1B Playbook](/docs/visa-playbooks/h1b-playbook), depending on their specific situation.

## Closing Strategy

If a candidate is still in process, confirm they've verified how any job change might interact with their case — from their own documentation or counsel, not from the advisor — before closing on anything that involves a change of employer. For candidates with settled status, this concern typically doesn't apply, but confirm status directly rather than assume it.

## Advisor Mistakes

- Treating every "green card" candidate identically, without asking where they actually are in the process
- Assuming settled status when a candidate is still in an earlier stage, or the reverse
- Speculating about how a job change might affect a pending case instead of routing that question to counsel
- Missing that a candidate with settled status may not need the same caution applied to earlier, employer-tied categories

## Key Takeaways

"Green card" describes a spectrum, not a single situation — always ask where a candidate actually is, since a lawful permanent resident and a candidate early in an employer-tied process need very different conversations. Never speculate about how a job change might affect a pending case; that question belongs to the candidate's own documentation and qualified immigration counsel.
