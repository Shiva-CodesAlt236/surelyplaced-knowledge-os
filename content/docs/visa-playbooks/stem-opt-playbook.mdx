# STEM OPT Playbook

> This is a sales playbook, not immigration guidance. The full non-legal-advice standard for this module is set out in [Visa Discovery Framework](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-discovery-framework); nothing in this article should be read as confirming eligibility or interpreting rules on a candidate's behalf.

## Candidate Profile

Candidates in this category hold a STEM-eligible degree and are using or planning to use an extension of their post-completion work authorization beyond the standard window. They've often already had some initial work experience in the country and are now further along in figuring out what a longer-term path might look like.

## Typical Mindset

Compared to a candidate earlier in their post-completion authorization, candidates using a STEM extension are frequently thinking in more concrete terms about what happens after this window closes — whether that means employer sponsorship, a different path, or something else. There's often a sharper awareness that this stage feels like a more consequential one than the first, even when a candidate can't articulate exactly why.

## Buying Motivation

Motivation here tends to center on using the remaining time well — building experience and relationships with an employer who might plausibly support a longer-term path, rather than just landing any role. Candidates are frequently more selective and more strategic at this stage than earlier in their post-completion window, having already learned something about the market from initial experience.

## Common Pain Points

- Awareness that this extension represents meaningful, but not unlimited, additional time, without always having a clear picture of exactly how much or what happens afterward
- Pressure to find not just any employer, but one who has a track record of supporting employees through a longer-term work authorization path
- Some candidates carry frustration from an initial job search that didn't fully deliver on what they'd hoped, and this stage now feels higher-stakes as a result
- Difficulty finding clear, trustworthy information about which employers are realistic targets for longer-term sponsorship

## Discovery Questions

1. "Where are you at with your STEM extension — has it already been filed, or are you in the process of that?"
2. "What's your understanding of what needs to happen before this window closes?"
3. "How did your initial work authorization period go — did you find a role, and how did that experience shape what you're looking for now?"
4. "Have you had any conversations with an employer about supporting a longer-term path, even informally?"
5. "Is there anything about your timeline that still feels unclear to you?"

## Qualification Signals

- The candidate can speak to their initial work experience and describe what they learned from it
- The candidate is asking specific, strategic questions about employer fit rather than general "what should I do" questions
- The candidate has a general, documented sense of their extension status, even if they defer specifics to their own paperwork rather than guessing

## Risk Signals

- The candidate is unsure whether their STEM extension has actually been filed or approved, and is proceeding as though it has without confirmation
- The candidate describes a timeline that seems inconsistent with a typical extension scenario, without a clear explanation
- The candidate mentions a change in employer or role that might affect their extension status, without having confirmed how

If any of these come up, pause and see [Visa Red Flags](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-red-flags) before continuing the sales conversation.

## Presentation Strategy

Lead with strategy, not urgency. Candidates at this stage often respond better to a conversation about how to use remaining time well than to language that amplifies pressure they're likely already carrying. Be specific about how the program's approach connects to finding employers with a track record relevant to their longer-term goals, without overstating what can be promised about any individual employer's decisions.

## Objection Patterns

Candidates in this category sometimes push back with confidence from their earlier job search experience — see [Already Applying Myself](/docs/objections/already-applying-myself). Given the higher stakes many candidates feel at this stage, questions about guaranteed outcomes are also common — see [No Guarantee Concern](/docs/objections/no-guarantee-concern).

## Closing Strategy

Before closing, confirm the candidate has a clear, current, and personally-verified understanding of their extension status and timeline — sourced from their own documentation or immigration counsel, never asserted by the advisor. Given the elevated stakes many candidates feel at this stage, take extra care that the close isn't happening under pressure the advisor introduced rather than pressure the candidate's actual situation creates.

## Advisor Mistakes

- Amplifying urgency instead of channeling it into a specific, strategic conversation
- Assuming a candidate's STEM extension has been filed or approved without confirming it directly
- Treating this category the same as earlier-stage post-completion authorization, when the candidate's experience level and stakes are often meaningfully different
- Speculating about which employers will or won't support a longer-term path instead of being honest about what can and can't be known in advance

## Key Takeaways

Candidates on a STEM extension are often more strategic and more aware of the stakes than earlier in their post-completion authorization — meet that with specificity, not amplified urgency. Never assert the status of a candidate's extension; confirm it against their own documentation, and route genuine uncertainty to qualified immigration counsel.
