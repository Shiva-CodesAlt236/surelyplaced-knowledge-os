# Visa Red Flags

> This is a compliance-critical article, not immigration guidance. It teaches advisors when to stop a sales conversation and redirect a candidate to qualified immigration counsel — it does not teach advisors how to resolve, interpret, or work around any of the signals below. See [Visa Discovery Framework](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-discovery-framework) for the full standard this article operates under.

## Candidate Profile

This article applies to any candidate, in any category covered by this module, whose discovery conversation surfaces one or more of the signals below. It cuts across every category-specific playbook rather than describing a single type of candidate.

## Typical Mindset

A candidate whose situation includes a genuine red flag is often carrying real anxiety about it, whether or not they say so directly. Some candidates raise the concern openly; others minimize it, hoping it isn't as significant as they fear, or hoping the advisor won't notice. Either way, the underlying uncertainty is real and deserves to be treated seriously rather than smoothed over to keep a sales conversation moving.

## Buying Motivation

Buying motivation is not the relevant frame once a red flag has surfaced. The advisor's job at that point shifts entirely from advancing a sales conversation to making sure the candidate gets accurate information from the right source before anything else continues.

## Common Pain Points

- Not knowing whether a status concern is minor or serious, and not having anyone qualified to ask
- Fear that raising the concern will make an advisor lose interest in helping them
- Having received conflicting or informal advice from friends, forums, or previous recruiters
- Anxiety that goes beyond the practical question and touches on their ability to stay in the country at all

## Discovery Questions

1. "Can you walk me through exactly what you know about your current status, in as much detail as you're comfortable with?"
2. "Has anything changed recently — a job, a filing, a notice — that might affect your status?"
3. "Have you spoken with an immigration attorney or accredited adviser about this specific concern?"
4. "Is there anything about what you've just described that you're not fully sure about?"

## Qualification Signals

Signals that a status question is minor and doesn't require pausing the conversation:

- The candidate has clear, consistent documentation and can describe their situation accurately
- Any uncertainty is about a detail the candidate can look up or confirm quickly, not about their fundamental eligibility to work
- The candidate has already engaged appropriate counsel and is simply relaying what they were told

## Risk Signals

The following signals mean the sales conversation should pause immediately, and the candidate should be directed to qualified immigration counsel before continuing:

- The candidate is unsure whether they are currently authorized to work at all
- The candidate describes a status that may have lapsed, been denied, or is otherwise actively in question
- The candidate mentions a pending legal or immigration case relevant to their status
- The candidate's description of their situation is inconsistent or contradicts itself as the conversation continues
- The candidate is relying on informal, secondhand, or outdated advice for a specific, high-stakes question
- The candidate asks the advisor directly to confirm eligibility, interpret a rule, or predict a government decision

## Presentation Strategy

When a red flag surfaces, stop advancing the sales conversation. Acknowledge the concern directly and without alarm, and tell the candidate plainly that this specific question needs a qualified immigration professional, not an advisor's best guess. Offer to continue the broader conversation once that's resolved, rather than ending the relationship abruptly.

## Objection Patterns

Some candidates will push back on being asked to pause, especially if they're anxious to keep moving forward — this isn't a sales objection to be handled with a technique, it's a moment to hold the line calmly and explain why the pause protects them, not the advisor. See [Trust and Credibility](/docs/objections/trust-and-credibility) for the broader pattern of handling skepticism with calm specificity rather than reassurance.

## Closing Strategy

Never close, recommend a track, or discuss investment while a red flag from this list is unresolved. If a candidate wants to move forward despite an open red flag, be direct that Surely Placed can't responsibly continue that specific part of the conversation until it's resolved.

## Advisor Mistakes

- Continuing a program-focused conversation after a genuine red flag has surfaced, because pausing feels disruptive to the call
- Offering reassurance about a status question the advisor isn't qualified to answer
- Treating a candidate's request for confirmation as a normal discovery question rather than a signal to redirect
- Softening the redirect so much that the candidate doesn't realize how seriously it should be taken

## Key Takeaways

When any of the signals in this article surface, the advisor's job changes immediately from advancing a sales conversation to making sure the candidate gets accurate information from qualified immigration counsel. Never guess, reassure, or improvise an answer to a genuine status concern — pause the conversation, redirect clearly and calmly, and resume the broader relationship once it's resolved.
