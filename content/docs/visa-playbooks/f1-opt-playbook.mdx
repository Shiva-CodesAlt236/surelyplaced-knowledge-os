# F-1 OPT Playbook

> This is a sales playbook, not immigration guidance. See [Visa Discovery Framework](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-discovery-framework) for the full standard this article operates under — advisors never confirm eligibility or interpret rules, and any specific question about dates or requirements goes to the candidate's own documentation and qualified immigration counsel.

## Candidate Profile

Candidates using F-1 post-completion Optional Practical Training are typically recent graduates authorized to work in their field of study for a defined window after finishing school. This is frequently their first experience with employer-relevant work authorization in the US, and often their first professional job search of any kind.

## Typical Mindset

Many candidates in this category are navigating two unfamiliar things at once: a first professional job search, and a first experience with time-limited work authorization. That combination can produce urgency that's driven as much by unfamiliarity with the process as by an actual imminent deadline — a candidate who doesn't yet know how to read their own documentation may feel more pressure than their actual timeline warrants, or less, and won't necessarily know which.

## Buying Motivation

The dominant motivation is usually establishing a first real foothold — a role that uses their degree, builds a track record, and ideally sets up a path toward longer-term authorization down the line. Financial independence and a sense of having "made it" after the cost and effort of studying abroad are often close underneath the surface as well.

## Common Pain Points

- Limited or no professional experience in the country yet, which can make some employers hesitant regardless of authorization status
- Uncertainty about how their specific authorization window and category actually work, sometimes compounded by advice from friends or forums that may not reflect their individual situation
- Pressure to secure a role that can plausibly lead to longer-term sponsorship, without always knowing which employers are realistic targets for that
- A sense of being new to both the country's job market and its systems more broadly

## Discovery Questions

1. "When did your post-completion work authorization start, and do you know roughly how it's structured?"
2. "Do you have your documentation in front of you, or a clear sense of what it says?"
3. "Is this your first time job searching in this country, or have you done this before?"
4. "Have you talked to your school's international student office or an immigration attorney about your options?"
5. "What's your understanding of what would need to happen for you to keep working here longer-term?"

## Qualification Signals

- The candidate can describe their field of study and how it connects to the roles they're targeting
- The candidate has some reference point for their authorization documentation, even if they haven't memorized every detail
- The candidate is asking genuine, specific questions about next steps rather than expressing general uncertainty about their whole situation

## Risk Signals

- The candidate seems unsure whether they're currently authorized to work at all
- The candidate describes a timeline that sounds inconsistent with what a first-time post-completion authorization would typically allow, without being able to explain why
- The candidate mentions having already had an authorization period lapse or a related issue

If any of these come up, pause and see [Visa Red Flags](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-red-flags) before continuing the sales conversation.

## Presentation Strategy

Because this is often a candidate's first professional job search in the country, lead with orientation as much as recommendation — helping them understand the shape of the market and the process itself is often as valuable to them as any specific program detail. Avoid assuming familiarity with US hiring norms that a candidate raised and educated elsewhere may not have.

## Objection Patterns

Candidates in this category sometimes hesitate because they're unsure whether investing in a program makes sense before they've tried applying more broadly on their own — see [Already Applying Myself](/docs/objections/already-applying-myself). Cost concerns are also common here, since many candidates in this category have just finished paying for their education (see [Price Objection](/docs/objections/price-objection)).

## Closing Strategy

Confirm the candidate has a clear, accurate understanding of their own authorization timeline — from their own documentation, not from the advisor — before closing on any specific track or timeline commitment. If that understanding is shaky, route them to confirm it before finalizing anything time-sensitive.

## Advisor Mistakes

- Assuming a specific authorization length or set of rules instead of asking the candidate what their documentation shows
- Treating every candidate in this category as equally urgent, when unfamiliarity with the process and genuine timeline pressure are two different things
- Overlooking that this may be a candidate's first professional job search in the country at all, and pitching as though they already understand the market
- Failing to route a candidate with real status uncertainty to immigration counsel before continuing

## Key Takeaways

Candidates on F-1 post-completion work authorization are often managing their first professional job search and their first experience with time-limited status at the same time — treat both as real, and don't assume which one is driving their urgency without asking. Never state specific authorization dates or rules; confirm against the candidate's own documentation and route genuine uncertainty to qualified immigration counsel.
