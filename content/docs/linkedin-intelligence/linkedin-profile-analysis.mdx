# LinkedIn Profile Analysis

## Purpose

Every other article in this module assumes an advisor already knows how to take in a profile as a whole before zooming into any single section. This article sets that baseline approach so the section-specific articles that follow can build on it.

## Scope

This article covers how to review a profile holistically before a coaching conversation — not how to write or edit any section on the candidate's behalf, and not how any particular algorithm decides what to surface to a recruiter, which this repository doesn't claim to know.

## Core Content

**Coherence first.** Look at the profile the way a recruiter skimming quickly would: does the headline, the current role, and the summary of experience tell a consistent story within a few seconds, or does something feel disconnected? A profile that reads as scattered on first pass is worth addressing before anything more granular.

**Completeness second.** Note which sections are filled in with real substance and which are thin or missing entirely — an empty About section or a list of roles with no description each leave a recruiter with less to go on than a fuller profile would, regardless of how strong the candidate's actual background is.

**Consistency third.** Compare the profile against what the advisor already knows about the candidate from other conversations, and against the candidate's resume where one exists. A material mismatch in dates, titles, or responsibilities between the two is worth raising directly rather than treating as a minor detail.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This three-part approach parallels the structure-substance-signal sequence [Resume Reading Framework](/docs/resume-intelligence/resume-reading-framework) already establishes for resumes, applied here to a profile that's public, ongoing, and viewed very differently — quickly, and often by someone who has never spoken with the candidate.

## Advisor Guidance

Work through all three passes before commenting on any single section — a reaction to one weak section in isolation often misses a stronger issue with the profile's overall coherence. Keep observations constructive; a LinkedIn profile is diagnostic material for a coaching conversation, not a scorecard.

## Key Takeaways

Take in a LinkedIn profile in three passes — coherence, completeness, then consistency against other known information — before discussing any individual section, and treat a material mismatch against a resume or prior conversation as worth a direct question rather than a minor detail to overlook.
