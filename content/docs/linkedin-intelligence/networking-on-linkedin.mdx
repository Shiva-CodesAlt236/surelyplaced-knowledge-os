# Networking on LinkedIn

## Purpose

A candidate's network on LinkedIn is often built during exactly the period they're working with an advisor, and how they approach it says something about how they'll come across in every other professional interaction too. This article covers how to coach genuine, respectful outreach rather than a volume-based or impersonal approach.

## Scope

This article covers the candidate's own outreach behavior — connection requests, informational conversations, and ongoing relationship maintenance. It does not cover the advisor's own use of LinkedIn on the candidate's behalf, and it does not promise that any specific outreach approach will produce a specific response rate or outcome.

## Core Content

A connection request that includes a short, specific reason for reaching out — a shared interest, a specific piece of the other person's work, a genuine question — tends to land better than a generic invitation with no context. This isn't a guarantee of a response; it's a matter of basic courtesy that also happens to make a request easier to say yes to.

Encourage candidates to treat an informational conversation as genuinely informational — asking real questions and listening — rather than as a disguised pitch for a job. A conversation that feels transactional from the first message is usually recognized as such, and that recognition tends to work against the candidate rather than for them.

Volume is not a substitute for genuine interest. Sending many identical, generic requests is different from building a smaller number of real connections, and this article doesn't treat the two as equivalent strategies — the honest, relationship-first approach is the one this repository encourages throughout.

## Advisor Guidance

Coach candidates toward specific, genuine, low-volume outreach rather than generic, high-volume tactics. Never suggest a message designed to mislead the recipient about the candidate's actual intent, and don't promise a specific response rate for any approach.
