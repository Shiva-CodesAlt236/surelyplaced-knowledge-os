# Advisor Coaching Guide

> Four modules now use this exact title, and each one points at a different conversation. In [Sales Coaching](/docs/sales-coaching/coach-review-framework), it means reviewing an advisor's own call performance. In [Interview Intelligence](/docs/interview-intelligence/advisor-coaching-guide), it's the pre- and post-interview conversation. In [Resume Intelligence](/docs/resume-intelligence/advisor-coaching-guide), it's the resume-review conversation. Here, it's specifically about coaching a candidate through how they present themselves on LinkedIn.

## Purpose

The rest of this module gives an advisor things to notice about a candidate's LinkedIn presence. This article covers how to actually raise those observations in conversation, constructively and without overwhelming the candidate with every possible improvement at once.

## Scope

This article covers the advisor's own conversational approach to LinkedIn coaching. It does not cover writing or editing the profile on the candidate's behalf, and it does not describe a specific LinkedIn-optimization service or session structure this repository doesn't define.

## Core Content

Start by asking the candidate how they currently think about their own LinkedIn presence, before offering any observation — some candidates have already thought carefully about it and just need targeted refinement, while others have never revisited it since it was first created, and the right starting point differs for each.

Prioritize the highest-impact items first rather than listing every possible improvement in one sitting — a coherent headline and a complete About section generally matter more than a smaller formatting detail, and a candidate asked to fix everything at once is less likely to act on any of it.

When something from [LinkedIn Red Flags](/docs/linkedin-intelligence/linkedin-red-flags) needs to be raised, do it directly and without alarm, the same way a resume-related concern would be raised — most have an ordinary explanation, and a calm, direct question preserves trust better than an anxious one would.

Close by checking whether the candidate feels genuinely comfortable with what their profile now says about them, not just whether specific edits were made — a candidate who understands and agrees with their own framing will maintain it going forward; one who was simply handed changes to make often won't.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This conversation draws on the same review material [Advisor Coaching Guide](/docs/resume-intelligence/advisor-coaching-guide) in Resume Intelligence works from, and follows the same prioritize-and-check-in structure established there, adapted to an ongoing profile rather than a single static document.

## Advisor Guidance

Ask before telling, prioritize the highest-impact changes over an exhaustive list, raise any red flag calmly, and confirm genuine candidate buy-in at the end rather than treating a list of edits as the goal in itself.
