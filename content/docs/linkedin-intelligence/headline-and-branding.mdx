# Headline and Branding

## Purpose

The headline is often the only line of a profile a recruiter reads before deciding whether to look further. This article covers how to help a candidate get that line right, and how to think about personal branding more broadly without turning it into something performative.

## Scope

This article covers framing and clarity in a candidate's headline and overall self-presentation. It does not prescribe a single required headline format, does not claim a specific phrasing outperforms another in any measurable way, and does not treat personal branding as something separate from the candidate's actual, honest background.

## Core Content

A headline that states role and focus area plainly — what the candidate does and what kind of work they're pursuing — tends to communicate more in a short scan than a headline built entirely from adjectives with nothing concrete underneath. Specificity generally reads as more credible than enthusiasm alone.

Personal branding, in this context, means presenting a consistent version of the candidate's actual strengths and direction across the headline, the About section, and the experience entries — not inventing a persona distinct from who the candidate actually is. A candidate whose branding overstates their experience risks a credibility problem the moment a real conversation goes beyond the profile.

There is no single correct headline template this repository endorses, and a candidate shouldn't be coached toward one specific formula as though it were required. Different roles and seniority levels reasonably call for different emphasis, and an advisor's job is to help the candidate find language that's authentically theirs and reasonably clear — not to standardize every profile the same way.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article extends the coherence pass from [LinkedIn Profile Analysis](/docs/linkedin-intelligence/linkedin-profile-analysis) into the specific line most likely to shape a recruiter's first impression, and stays consistent with the honesty standard already set in [Ethical Selling](/docs/sales-constitution/ethical-selling) — branding never means overstating what's true.

## Advisor Guidance

Help a candidate find clear, specific, honest language rather than a generic or performative one. Avoid presenting any single headline format as required or proven, and never suggest wording that implies more seniority, scope, or achievement than the candidate's actual background supports.
