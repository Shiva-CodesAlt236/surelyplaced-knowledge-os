# About Section Strategy

## Purpose

The About section is one of the few places on LinkedIn where a candidate gets to write in their own voice at length. This article covers how to help a candidate use that space well, rather than leaving it blank or filling it with a restatement of the experience section below it.

## Scope

This article covers narrative approach and tone for the About section specifically. It does not provide a fill-in-the-blank template this repository treats as official, and it does not claim a specific structure or length performs measurably better than another.

## Core Content

An About section that opens with what the candidate is actually working toward, rather than a chronological recap of past titles, tends to give a reader more reason to keep reading — the experience section already covers the chronology, so the About section is better used to explain the throughline connecting it.

First person tends to read more naturally here than the third-person voice sometimes used in a resume, since the About section is understood as the candidate speaking directly. This is a stylistic observation, not a rule this repository requires a candidate to follow.

Keep specific claims about outcomes or scale grounded in what the candidate can actually stand behind — the same honesty standard that applies everywhere else in this repository applies here too. An About section is a place for genuine narrative, not a place to introduce a claim the rest of the profile or the candidate's actual history doesn't support.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article is the LinkedIn-specific counterpart to [Resume Storytelling](/docs/resume-intelligence/resume-storytelling), applying the same throughline-finding approach to a space that allows more voice and length than a resume typically does.

## Advisor Guidance

Help the candidate find their own throughline and write it in their own words, rather than supplying stock phrasing. Keep every claim grounded in the candidate's actual, verifiable background, and don't imply that any one structure is the only effective one.
