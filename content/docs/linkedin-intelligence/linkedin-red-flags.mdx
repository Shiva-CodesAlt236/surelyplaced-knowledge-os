# LinkedIn Red Flags

## Purpose

Most things that look unusual on a LinkedIn profile have an ordinary explanation. This article separates what's genuinely worth a careful conversation or escalation from what an advisor can simply coach past.

## Scope

This article covers what to notice in a candidate's LinkedIn presence and how to respond. It does not make a legal or work-authorization determination on the advisor's own authority, and it does not treat an inactive or minimal profile as inherently suspicious.

## Core Content

**Worth a direct, careful conversation:**
- A material mismatch between the dates, titles, or responsibilities shown on LinkedIn and what's on the candidate's resume or what they've said in conversation.
- Endorsements or recommendations that describe experience the candidate hasn't otherwise mentioned, worth a clarifying question about accuracy.

**Worth routing rather than resolving directly:**
- Anything touching work authorization status or eligibility claims stated on the profile — this routes to [Visa Red Flags](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-red-flags) and qualified immigration counsel, the same as it would from a resume.
- A candidate asking whether it's acceptable to overstate a title, scope, or achievement to look stronger to recruiters — this is a conversation about honesty grounded in [Ethical Selling](/docs/sales-constitution/ethical-selling), not a LinkedIn tactics question.
- Any indication the profile itself may not genuinely belong to the candidate, or that someone else is managing it in a way the candidate hasn't approved.

**Not actually a red flag:**
- A minimal profile with few connections, no posts, or limited endorsements. Many strong candidates simply haven't invested time in the platform, and that alone says nothing about their credibility.
- A career change or non-linear path reflected on the profile — the same as with a resume, variation in background isn't itself a concern.

## Cross-Module Alignment

This article follows the same escalate-rather-than-resolve pattern already established in [Resume Red Flags](/docs/resume-intelligence/resume-red-flags) and [Interview Red Flags](/docs/interview-intelligence/interview-red-flags), applied here to what surfaces specifically on a LinkedIn profile.

## Advisor Guidance

Raise a mismatch or an unusual claim directly and calmly — most have a simple explanation the candidate can supply immediately. Route anything touching authorization or authenticity rather than guessing at an answer, and never treat a quiet, minimal profile as a mark against the candidate.

Most unusual LinkedIn signals deserve a calm, direct conversation rather than concern, anything touching authorization or profile authenticity must be routed rather than resolved by the advisor alone, and a quiet or minimal profile is never itself a red flag.
