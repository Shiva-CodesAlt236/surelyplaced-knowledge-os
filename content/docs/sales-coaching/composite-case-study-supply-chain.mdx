# Composite Case Study: Supply Chain Candidate on a Canada Work Permit

> **Composite Case Study.** The candidate described here, referred to as "Meera," is entirely fictional — a composite built by combining patterns from the Supply Chain Playbook and Canada Work Permit Playbook, not a real person or real conversation. Nowhere in this article does a price, discount, guarantee, or placement result appear.

## Case Study Type

Composite Case Study

## Purpose

This case study combines two existing modules: a candidate with a supply chain background (see [Supply Chain Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/supply-chain-playbook)) evaluating a Canada work permit path (see [Canada Work Permit Playbook](/docs/visa-playbooks/canada-work-permit-playbook)). It shows how an operationally-minded, process-oriented candidate evaluates both her career fit and her immigration path with the same practical, risk-averse lens.

## Learning Objectives

- Recognize how a supply chain candidate's process-oriented mindset shows up in both career and immigration questions
- Apply honest, non-legal-advice framing consistently across a conversation that touches immigration repeatedly
- Diagnose a "need time to think" moment correctly rather than treating it as a rejection
- See how a genuinely risk-averse candidate evaluates trustworthiness across an entire conversation, not just at one moment

## Scenario Summary

Meera has a background in logistics and supply chain operations and is exploring a Canada work permit path. Consistent with the Supply Chain Playbook's description of candidates who value process, risk management, and clear sequencing, she approaches this decision the same way she'd evaluate a vendor or a process change at work: methodically, with a strong preference for understanding the full sequence before committing to any step.

## Candidate Background

Meera is a composite figure, not a real candidate. She represents a combination described separately in the Supply Chain Playbook (process orientation, risk-aversion, preference for clear sequencing) and the Canada Work Permit Playbook (immigration-specific caution and the standing instruction that immigration specifics are never advised on directly by an advisor).

## Advisor Objective

Present the process clearly and sequentially, consistent with Meera's own professional style, while being scrupulously honest about the boundary between what an advisor can speak to and what requires immigration counsel — see [Visa Discovery Framework](/docs/visa-playbooks/visa-discovery-framework).

## Conversation Walkthrough

Meera asks early and directly for a full sequence of steps — not just the next one, but the whole process end to end, consistent with the Supply Chain Playbook's description of sequencing-oriented candidates. The advisor lays out what can be honestly described about the overall process, consistent with [Explaining the Process](/docs/discussion/explaining-the-process), while being direct that any immigration-specific step requires confirmation from qualified immigration counsel rather than the advisor's own explanation.

Later, Meera asks a direct question about a specific work permit condition — the kind of question that sits outside advisor authority. The advisor doesn't guess or approximate; they acknowledge the question is a fair one and redirect it to the appropriate resource, consistent with the non-legal-advice discipline established throughout the Visa Playbooks module.

Near the end of the discussion call, Meera says she needs time to review everything methodically before deciding — a request that, read through her process-oriented lens, is not hesitation about the value of the program but a genuine reflection of how she makes any decision. The advisor recognizes this and responds using the framework in [Need Time to Think](/docs/objections/need-time-to-think), rather than treating it as a stall to be overcome.

## Decision Analysis

The advisor's central decision was resisting the temptation to answer Meera's immigration-specific question directly, even though doing so might have felt like it would keep the conversation moving more smoothly. Redirecting honestly to immigration counsel, even at the cost of some momentum, was the correct call — and it reinforced rather than undermined Meera's trust, consistent with her own risk-averse, process-first evaluation style.

## What Went Well

- The advisor presented the process sequentially, matching Meera's own professional style rather than a generic pitch structure
- The advisor redirected the immigration-specific question honestly rather than approximating an answer
- The advisor recognized Meera's "need time to think" as genuine process-orientation rather than treating it as a rejection to overcome

## What Could Improve

- The advisor could have proactively named, earlier in the call, that some questions would need to go to immigration counsel — setting that expectation before Meera asked, rather than only in response
- The advisor could have offered a clearer written summary of the sequence discussed, which would likely appeal to Meera's process-oriented style even more than a verbal summary alone

## Coaching Notes

Meera's case is a reminder that a candidate's professional background often predicts how they'll evaluate this decision, not just what career questions they'll ask. Matching her actual evaluation style — sequential, methodical, risk-aware — mattered as much as the content of any individual answer.

## Key Takeaways

A process-oriented candidate evaluates both career fit and immigration path the same methodical way, and a request for time to think from a candidate like this often reflects genuine decision-making style rather than hesitation. Honest redirection on immigration-specific questions, even at some cost to momentum, builds rather than undermines trust with a risk-averse evaluator.
