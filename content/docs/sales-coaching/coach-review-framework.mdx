# Mirroring, Pausing & Handling Silence

Some of the most effective moves in a call involve saying less, not more. This lesson is about the quiet techniques.

**Why this matters:** rushing to fill a pause or substituting your own words for a candidate's often does more harm than the silence or repetition it was trying to avoid.
**When to use this:** any time a candidate pauses, discloses something worth reflecting back, or goes quiet after a real question.
**What skill this builds:** mirroring a candidate's own language, pausing deliberately, and letting silence do its work instead of treating it as dead air.
**What happens next:** these quiet techniques are what call ownership, covered next, is actually built from — not talking more, but guiding well.

## Lesson Objective

- Mirror a candidate's own language back to them instead of substituting your own words
- Use a deliberate pause instead of rushing to fill space
- Let silence do its work instead of treating it as dead air

**Excellent — mirrored and followed by a genuine pause:**

<DecisionPoint

<ManagerTip from="Aayush" priority="important">
What should have happened, and how to improve: when a candidate uses an emotionally loaded word, mirror it exactly and then pause. Don't rush past your own mirroring.
</ManagerTip>

## Coaching Notes

<CoachingNotes title="Why This Worked">
Mirroring "stressful" and then genuinely pausing gave Akash room to keep going if he wanted to, without being pushed. The single word carried more weight than a longer, more reassuring sentence would have.
</CoachingNotes>
