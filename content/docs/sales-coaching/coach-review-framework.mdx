# Coach Review Framework

> This is a sales coaching module, not an HR or performance-management document. This article covers how to review a call constructively, not how to evaluate or discipline an advisor formally — any formal performance process is Operational Policy Pending and outside the scope of this repository.

## Case Study Type

Not applicable — this is a coaching framework article, not a case study.

## Purpose

A manager or peer reviewing an advisor's call has an opportunity to genuinely improve how that advisor works, or a missed opportunity that produces only vague feedback. This article applies the four-layer reading framework from [Reading a Sales Conversation](/docs/sales-coaching/reading-a-sales-conversation) specifically to the act of reviewing someone else's call constructively.

## Learning Objectives

- Review a call using the same four-layer framework applied throughout this module, rather than reacting only to the outcome
- Give feedback that's specific and actionable rather than general praise or criticism
- Separate genuine skill gaps from situational factors outside the advisor's control
- Frame a review as coaching toward improvement, not evaluation for its own sake

## Conversation Walkthrough

Approach a call review the same way the case studies in this module are read: identify the moments where the conversation's direction shifted, and for each one, ask what signal was present, how the advisor responded, and whether that response matched the signal. This produces specific, moment-based observations rather than a single overall impression of "good call" or "rough call."

Separate what the advisor controlled from what they didn't. A candidate who was never going to be ready that day isn't evidence of a poor call if the advisor read the situation correctly and responded honestly — see [No Guarantee Concern](/docs/objections/no-guarantee-concern) for why outcome alone isn't the right measure.

## Decision Analysis

The most useful reviews focus on two or three specific moments rather than attempting to comment on everything. A review that tries to cover every second of a call produces feedback too diffuse to act on; a review anchored to specific, well-chosen moments gives the advisor something concrete to practice.

## What Went Well

Not applicable in the case-study sense — the "well" for this article is a review that names specific moments, connects them to a clear signal-and-response analysis, and leaves the advisor with something concrete to work on.

## What Could Improve

Not applicable in the case-study sense — the most common failure mode in call reviews generally is vague feedback ("good energy," "just needs more confidence") that doesn't point to a specific, repeatable behavior to change.

## Coaching Notes

A good review should feel, to the advisor receiving it, like a genuine second pair of eyes on a specific moment — not a judgment on their overall competence. Anchor feedback to what was actually said and done, and frame it toward the next call rather than only toward what already happened.

## Key Takeaways

Reviewing a call well means anchoring feedback to specific moments — signal, response, and shift — rather than an overall impression, and separating what the advisor controlled from what they didn't. Two or three specific, well-chosen observations produce more useful coaching than an attempt to comment on everything.
