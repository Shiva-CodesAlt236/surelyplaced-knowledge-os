# Reading a Sales Conversation

> This is a sales coaching module, not marketing or testimonial content. This article is a diagnostic framework, not a case study — its purpose is to teach advisors how to analyze any conversation, real or composite, using the same lens applied throughout this module.

## Case Study Type

Not applicable — this is a coaching framework article, not a case study.

## Purpose

Every case study in this module, and every real conversation an advisor has, can be read the same way: as a sequence of moves by the candidate and responses by the advisor, each revealing something about mindset, readiness, and risk. This article establishes that reading framework once, so the case studies that follow can apply it directly rather than re-explaining it each time.

## Learning Objectives

- Break a conversation down into candidate signals and advisor responses rather than judging it as a single undifferentiated whole
- Distinguish a candidate's words from the underlying mindset those words often reveal, consistent with Discovery, Objections, and Pricing
- Identify the specific moment in a conversation where its direction changed, for better or worse
- Apply this same reading framework consistently across every case study in this module

## Conversation Walkthrough

Read any conversation in four layers. First, the literal content — what was actually said, by whom, in what order. Second, the underlying signal — what that content suggests about the candidate's mindset, using the frameworks already established in Discovery (qualification), Objections (the real concern behind an objection), and Pricing (investment psychology). Third, the advisor's response — not just what was said back, but whether it addressed the surface content, the underlying signal, or neither. Fourth, the resulting shift — did the candidate's engagement, openness, or trust move forward, stay flat, or move backward as a result.

This four-layer read is what every case study walkthrough in this module applies, moment by moment, rather than only judging the conversation's overall outcome.

## Decision Analysis

A conversation should be evaluated by whether the advisor's responses matched the actual underlying signal, not by whether the candidate said yes. An advisor who reads the signal correctly and responds well, but the candidate still isn't ready, has done the job correctly — see [No Guarantee Concern](/docs/objections/no-guarantee-concern) for why outcome alone isn't the right measure of a good conversation.

## What Went Well

Not applicable in the case-study sense — this article's "well" is a well-applied framework: reading past the literal words to the underlying signal, at each of the four layers, consistently.

## What Could Improve

Not applicable in the case-study sense — the most common way this framework is misapplied is responding only to the literal content of what a candidate says, while missing the underlying signal underneath it. Every case study in this module includes at least one moment where that distinction matters.

## Coaching Notes

When reviewing any conversation — a real one or one of this module's composite case studies — resist jumping straight to the outcome. Walk through the four layers first: what was said, what it likely meant, how the advisor responded, and what shifted as a result. The outcome makes far more sense once that walkthrough is done than it does on its own.
