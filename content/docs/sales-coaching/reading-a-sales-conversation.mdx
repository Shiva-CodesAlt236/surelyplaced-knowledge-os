# Reading Buying Signals & Hesitation

A candidate's words are the surface. This lesson is about reading what's underneath them, in real time, while you're still on the call.

## Start Here

**When do I use this?** Any time you're on a call — this module isn't tied to Discovery, Discussion, Closing, Objection Handling, or Pricing specifically. It's the set of skills running underneath all of them.

**What will this module help me achieve?** Sharper listening, steadier tone, better timing, and the habit of reviewing your own calls honestly — the difference between knowing the framework from Objection Handling or Pricing and actually applying it well, live.

**When should I move to the next module?** Once these individual skills start feeling less like separate techniques and more like one instinct — Sales Constitution is where you step back from technique altogether and look at who you're actually being on a call.

## Lesson Objective

- Tell a buying signal apart from politeness, and hesitation apart from a soft no
- Notice the exact moment a conversation's direction shifts
- Respond to the real signal, not just the literal sentence

<CoachingNotes title="What happened, and why">
The rep heard urgency and treated it as readiness. What happened: a close attempt on borrowed pressure. Why: the literal words ("move soon") got answered instead of the signal underneath them (someone else's patience, not his own readiness).
</CoachingNotes>

**Better — the signal gets partially read:**

    { sender: "rep", text: "Okay — no rush on my end though. Take the time you need.", annotation: "Better: didn't push, but didn't name the actual signal either" },
  ]}
/>

**Excellent — the real signal gets named directly:**

<ConversationViewer
  repName="Aayush"
  candidateName="Akash"
  appearance="phone"
  candidateMood="hesitant"
  messages={[
    { sender: "candidate", text: "I want to move on this soon, before my brother's patience runs out with covering things." },
    { sender: "rep", text: "I want to slow down for a second — this sounds like it's coming from his timeline more than yours. Is this actually right for you, independent of that?", annotation: "Excellent: names the real signal underneath the words and checks it directly" },
  ]}
/>

<DecisionPoint
  context="Three responses to the exact same sentence. Only one actually addresses what's really happening underneath the words."
  prompt="What made the excellent response different from the better one?"
  bestAnswer="It named the specific signal out loud — borrowed urgency, not personal readiness — instead of just avoiding pressure without addressing what was actually driving it."
/>

<ManagerTip from="Aayush" priority="important">
What should have happened, and how to improve: read past the literal sentence to what it's actually signaling, then say what you noticed out loud. Silence about the signal is safer than pushing, but naming it is what actually helps the candidate.
</ManagerTip>

## Coaching Notes

<CoachingNotes title="Why This Worked">
Naming the signal directly — "this sounds like his timeline, not yours" — gave Akash room to admit the truth. A conversation can only be read correctly if the read gets said out loud at some point, not just held internally.
</CoachingNotes>
