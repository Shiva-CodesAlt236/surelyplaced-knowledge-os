# Curiosity & Avoiding Overexplaining

Curiosity before pitching isn't just a principle — it's a practical skill. This lesson is about staying curious instead of reaching for more words.

**Why this matters:** an unprompted pitch, even an accurate one, can undo the trust genuine curiosity would have built instead.
**When to use this:** any time a candidate mentions a competing option or directly invites a comparative pitch.
**What skill this builds:** asking a genuine question before explaining anything, and actually stopping to let the answer land.
**What happens next:** this curiosity-first instinct is what the module's closing checklist confirms held up across an entire call.

## Lesson Objective

- Ask before explaining, even when you already know the answer you'd give
- Notice the moment you start explaining more than was actually asked for
- Let a short, curious question replace a longer justification

<ManagerTip from="Aayush" priority="important">
What should have happened, and how to improve: after you ask a genuinely curious question, stop talking completely. If you're already planning your next sentence while they answer, you weren't actually curious.
</ManagerTip>

## Coaching Notes

<CoachingNotes title="Why This Worked">
Asking and then genuinely stopping gave Rahul room to say something real, which is worth more than any pitch Aayush could have given instead. Overexplaining fills silence that would have been more useful left open.
</CoachingNotes>
