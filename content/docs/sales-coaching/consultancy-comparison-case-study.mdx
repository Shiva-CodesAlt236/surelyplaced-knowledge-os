# Consultancy Comparison Case Study

> **Composite Case Study.** The candidate described here, referred to as "Oscar," is entirely fictional — a composite built to illustrate [Already Working With a Consultancy](/docs/objections/already-working-with-a-consultancy) under real conditions, not a real person or real conversation. No specific price, guarantee, or comparative claim about another company is stated anywhere in this article.

## Case Study Type

Composite Case Study

## Purpose

[Already Working With a Consultancy](/docs/objections/already-working-with-a-consultancy) covers the principle of honest, non-disparaging comparison. This case study shows that principle applied to a candidate who is not just aware of another consultancy but actively engaged with one, making the comparison more concrete and higher-stakes than a hypothetical mention.

## Learning Objectives

- Apply honest comparison principles when a candidate is actively engaged elsewhere, not just aware of an alternative
- Avoid disparaging a competitor even when a candidate invites criticism of them
- Ask specific, useful discovery questions about what the other engagement actually includes
- Recognize when a comparison conversation is really a proxy for a different underlying concern

## Scenario Summary

Oscar mentions partway through discovery that he's already paying for services with another consultancy and is unsatisfied with the experience so far, but is still deciding whether to switch, add a second option, or stay where he is.

## Candidate Background

Oscar is a composite figure, not a real candidate. He represents a pattern described generally in [Already Working With a Consultancy](/docs/objections/already-working-with-a-consultancy): a candidate with direct, ongoing experience to compare against, rather than an abstract sense that other options exist.

## Advisor Objective

Understand specifically what Oscar's current engagement includes and where his dissatisfaction is coming from, and respond honestly about what Surely Placed's approach is, without disparaging the other consultancy or inventing a comparison that can't be substantiated.

## Conversation Walkthrough

When Oscar mentions his dissatisfaction, he invites the advisor to weigh in critically — "have you heard of them, are they usually like this?" The advisor doesn't take the invitation to criticize a company they have no direct, verifiable knowledge of; instead, they ask Oscar specifically what's been unsatisfying, rather than speculating about the other consultancy's general reputation.

Oscar describes a specific frustration: infrequent communication and unclear next steps. The advisor doesn't respond by claiming Surely Placed is definitively better at this — a comparative claim that can't be honestly substantiated in the abstract — but instead describes, specifically and honestly, what advisors are expected to provide in terms of communication and process clarity (see [Communication Standards](/docs/sales-operations/communication-standards)), letting Oscar draw his own comparison from concrete description rather than a competitive claim.

Later, Oscar asks directly whether he should drop the other consultancy entirely. The advisor is honest that this is Oscar's decision to make, not something to be pushed toward, and reflects that the choice should be based on what actually serves his goals rather than any pressure to consolidate with one provider.

## Decision Analysis

The advisor's key decision point was declining to criticize a competitor even when directly invited to, and instead redirecting toward specific, honest description of Surely Placed's own approach. This kept the conversation on solid, verifiable ground rather than drifting into unsubstantiated claims about a company the advisor has no direct knowledge of.

## What Went Well

- The advisor declined to speculate about or criticize the other consultancy despite being invited to
- The advisor asked a specific discovery question rather than responding to a vague dissatisfaction with a vague reassurance
- The advisor left the decision about whether to leave the other consultancy explicitly with Oscar, rather than pushing toward it

## What Could Improve

- The advisor could have asked earlier in the conversation whether Oscar was working with anyone else, rather than waiting for him to volunteer it
- When describing Surely Placed's own communication approach, the advisor could have been even more concrete about what that looks like in practice, making the implicit comparison even clearer without stating it directly

## Coaching Notes

Oscar's case is a reminder that being invited to criticize a competitor is not the same as it being appropriate to do so. The stronger, more credible response is almost always specific and honest description of one's own approach, left to speak for itself.

## Key Takeaways

When a candidate is actively engaged with another consultancy and invites criticism of it, the right response is specific, honest description of one's own approach — never speculation or disparagement about a company the advisor has no direct, verifiable knowledge of. Let concrete description do the comparative work rather than making the comparison explicitly.
