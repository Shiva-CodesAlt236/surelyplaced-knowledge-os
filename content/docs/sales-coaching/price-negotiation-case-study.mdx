# Price Negotiation Case Study

> **Composite Case Study.** The candidate described here, referred to as "Farah," is entirely fictional — a composite built to illustrate the Pricing module's guidance under sustained pressure, not a real person or real conversation. No specific price, discount, financing term, or guarantee is stated anywhere in this article — this case study is precisely about what happens when a candidate asks for one.

## Case Study Type

Composite Case Study

## Purpose

[Discount Guidelines](/docs/pricing/discount-guidelines) covers the principle: never invent, imply, or estimate a discount. This case study shows that principle tested by a candidate who pushes harder than a single polite request — repeating the ask, framing it several different ways, and applying real conversational pressure — to show what holding that line actually looks like in practice.

## Learning Objectives

- Apply the discount-guidelines discipline under repeated, escalating pressure rather than a single easy request
- Distinguish between acknowledging a candidate's request warmly and implying that an exception is likely
- Recognize when repeated negotiation attempts might indicate a deeper affordability concern worth addressing directly
- See the difference between holding a boundary rigidly and holding it warmly

## Scenario Summary

Farah has completed the discussion call and is seriously considering enrolling, but asks for a discount — first casually, then more directly when the advisor doesn't immediately offer one, and finally by referencing a discount she believes a friend received through a different advisor.

## Candidate Background

Farah is a composite figure, not a real candidate. She represents a common pattern described generally in [Discount Guidelines](/docs/pricing/discount-guidelines) and [Handling Price Objections](/docs/pricing/handling-price-objections): a candidate testing whether the stated terms are actually fixed, sometimes as ordinary negotiating behavior rather than genuine financial strain.

## Advisor Objective

Hold the line established in [Discount Guidelines](/docs/pricing/discount-guidelines) — never invent, imply, or estimate a discount — while keeping the conversation warm and while checking honestly whether something more serious than ordinary negotiation is actually driving the request.

## Conversation Walkthrough

Farah's first mention of a discount is casual, almost offhand — "is there any flexibility on the price?" The advisor responds warmly, without guessing, saying plainly that this needs to be confirmed through the correct channel rather than answered on the spot, consistent with the Discount Guidelines framework.

When Farah asks again, more directly, the advisor notices the temptation to soften the previous answer just to keep the conversation moving — and doesn't. They repeat the same honest position, without becoming defensive or repetitive in a way that would feel cold, and ask Farah directly what's behind the request: whether it's the number itself or something about her financial situation more broadly.

Farah then mentions a friend who she believes received a discount through a different advisor. The advisor doesn't speculate about whether that's accurate or push back on it defensively; they acknowledge they can't speak to another advisor's conversation, restate that they want to give Farah an accurate answer rather than a guess, and commit to following up with the correct answer directly.

## Decision Analysis

The advisor's central decision, repeated three times across the conversation, was the same: acknowledge the request warmly, decline to guess, and offer to confirm the real answer — rather than letting escalating pressure erode that position bit by bit. The consistency across all three moments, not any single response, is what made this handled well.

## What Went Well

- The advisor held the same honest position consistently across three escalating requests, rather than softening under repeated pressure
- The advisor asked directly what was behind the repeated request, opening space for Farah to reveal a genuine affordability concern if one existed
- The advisor didn't speculate about or dispute the claim regarding another advisor's conversation

## What Could Improve

- The advisor could have followed up on the discount question faster once escalated to the correct channel, rather than leaving Farah to wonder how long it would take
- Earlier in the conversation, before the first discount request came up, the advisor could have set expectations proactively about how pricing questions are handled

## Coaching Notes

This case study exists because holding a boundary once, in an easy moment, is a different skill than holding it consistently across escalating pressure. Farah's third attempt — referencing a friend's supposed discount — is the moment most likely to tempt an advisor into guessing or improvising just to resolve the tension. That's exactly the moment [Discount Guidelines](/docs/pricing/discount-guidelines) is written for.

## Key Takeaways

Holding the discount-guidelines discipline under a single, easy request is straightforward — the real test is holding it consistently across repeated, escalating pressure without softening or improvising. Stay warm, stay honest, and use repeated requests as an opportunity to check for a deeper concern rather than as pressure to invent an answer.
