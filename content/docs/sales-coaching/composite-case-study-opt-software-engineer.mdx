# Active Listening & Asking Better Questions

Good questions come from good listening, not from a list you memorized. This lesson is about hearing what's actually said before deciding what to ask next.

**Why this matters:** five generic follow-up questions waste a call in a way one precise, listening-driven question never does.
**When to use this:** throughout every call, but especially any time a candidate's answer contains a specific detail worth following, not just a topic.
**What skill this builds:** listening for the specific detail in what's actually said, and letting the candidate's own words shape your next question.
**What happens next:** this listening habit, applied consistently, is what the rest of this module's case studies build on next.

## Lesson Objective

- Listen for the specific detail in what a candidate says, not just the general topic
- Ask one precise follow-up instead of several generic ones
- Let the candidate's own words shape your next question

  ]}
/>

**Excellent — the exact detail gets used to ask a precise question:**

<ConversationViewer
  repName="Aayush"
  candidateName="Rahul"
  appearance="phone"
  candidateMood="neutral"
  messages={[
    { sender: "candidate", text: "I'm doing OPT right now, but honestly the role doesn't really use my actual skills." },
    { sender: "rep", text: "What are your actual skills, and what specifically is the current role having you do instead?", annotation: "Excellent: uses his exact word — 'actual skills' — to ask something precise and specific to him" },
  ]}
/>

<DecisionPoint
  context="Rahul gave one specific, usable detail. Three different responses show three different levels of listening to it."
  prompt="What made the excellent question better than the better one?"
  bestAnswer="It used Rahul's own words back to him and asked for something concrete — the actual skills and the actual mismatch — instead of a general question he could answer any number of vague ways."
/>

<ManagerTip from="Aayush" priority="important">
What should have happened, and how to improve: when a candidate uses a specific word or phrase, reuse it in your next question. It shows you were actually listening, not running through a mental list.
</ManagerTip>

## Coaching Notes

<CoachingNotes title="Why This Worked">
Asking about "actual skills" using Rahul's exact phrase, rather than a generic "what are you looking for," got a much more specific and useful answer — and it signaled to Rahul that he was actually being heard, not processed.
</CoachingNotes>
