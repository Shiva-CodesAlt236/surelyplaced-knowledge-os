# Composite Case Study: OPT Software Engineer

> **Composite Case Study.** The candidate described here, referred to as "Arjun," is entirely fictional — a composite built by combining patterns from the F1/OPT Playbook and Software Engineering Playbook, not a real person or real conversation. This article never cites a price, a discount, a guarantee, or a placement result.

## Case Study Type

Composite Case Study

## Purpose

This case study shows the reading framework from [Reading a Sales Conversation](/docs/sales-coaching/reading-a-sales-conversation) applied to a realistic combination of two existing modules: a candidate on F-1 OPT status (see [F1/OPT Playbook](/docs/visa-playbooks/f1-opt-playbook)) working in software engineering (see [Software Engineering Playbook](/docs/industry-playbooks/software-engineering-playbook)). It's designed to show how those two playbooks' guidance combine in a single conversation, not to teach either one from scratch.

## Learning Objectives

- Recognize how visa-timeline urgency and industry-specific evaluation criteria show up together in a single candidate
- Apply the discovery, objection, and closing frameworks to a candidate juggling two distinct sources of pressure
- Practice diagnosing which concern — timeline or career fit — is actually driving a given moment in the conversation
- See the four-layer reading framework applied concretely, moment by moment

## Scenario Summary

Arjun is on F-1 OPT status, currently in a role he describes as not using his actual skills, and aware that his OPT window is finite. He's also a software engineer evaluating this decision the way he'd evaluate a technical option: comparing alternatives, wanting specifics, and skeptical of anything that sounds like a sales pitch rather than a substantive comparison.

## Candidate Background

Arjun is a composite figure, not a real candidate. He represents a common combination described separately in the F1/OPT Playbook (timeline urgency, STEM OPT extension awareness) and the Software Engineering Playbook (technical skepticism, comparison-driven evaluation, distrust of unsubstantiated claims).

## Advisor Objective

Qualify Arjun honestly against both dimensions of his situation — his visa timeline and his technical career goals — without letting the urgency of one overshadow honest evaluation of the other, consistent with [Candidate Qualification](/docs/discovery/candidate-qualification).

## Conversation Walkthrough

Arjun opens the discovery call by stating his OPT timeline directly and asking how quickly the process typically moves — a literal question with an underlying signal of real anxiety about time running out, consistent with the timeline urgency described in the F1/OPT Playbook. The advisor acknowledges the timeline honestly, without stating a specific number of days or a guaranteed pace (a specific processing timeline is not something this repository is positioned to state), and redirects to understanding Arjun's actual situation first.

Midway through discovery, Arjun starts asking pointed, technical-sounding questions about how candidates are actually evaluated and matched — consistent with the Software Engineering Playbook's description of a comparison-driven, skepticism-first evaluation style. The advisor resists the urge to answer with a generic reassurance and instead asks Arjun directly what specifically he's trying to verify, surfacing a more specific concern: whether this process would genuinely reflect his actual technical background or treat him as interchangeable with any other candidate.

At the discussion call, cost comes up. The advisor presents it at a steady pace, consistent with [Presenting Investment Confidently](/docs/pricing/presenting-investment-confidently), and Arjun raises what reads at first like a straightforward affordability objection but, on closer questioning, turns out to be a comparison objection — he's weighing this against a cheaper option he found and wants to understand the actual difference, consistent with the comparison-objection variant in [Handling Price Objections](/docs/pricing/handling-price-objections).

## Decision Analysis

The advisor's key decision point is recognizing that Arjun's two pressures — timeline and technical fit — sometimes point toward the same response and sometimes require different ones. Rushing the technical-fit conversation because of timeline urgency would have addressed the wrong concern at the wrong moment; slowing down the timeline conversation to fully resolve every technical question would have ignored real time pressure. The advisor's job was holding both honestly rather than collapsing one into the other.

## What Went Well

- The advisor acknowledged timeline urgency honestly without inventing a specific processing guarantee
- The advisor asked a clarifying question rather than answering Arjun's technical skepticism with generic reassurance
- The advisor correctly diagnosed the price objection as a comparison variant rather than treating it as a flat affordability objection

## What Could Improve

- Earlier in discovery, the advisor could have asked directly whether Arjun was also considering other options, surfacing the comparison dynamic sooner rather than only at the pricing conversation
- The advisor could have connected the visa-timeline urgency and the technical evaluation concern explicitly for Arjun, showing him the advisor understood both dimensions of his situation at once

## Coaching Notes

This case study is a reminder that real candidates rarely fit neatly into one playbook. When two sources of pressure are present, the skill isn't picking which one matters more — it's tracking both honestly and recognizing, moment to moment, which one is actually driving what's being said.
