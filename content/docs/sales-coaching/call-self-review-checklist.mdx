# Call Self-Review Checklist

> This is a sales coaching module. This checklist is for an advisor's own private reflection immediately after a call, distinct from the operational post-call habits covered in [Sales Operations Checklist](/docs/sales-operations/sales-operations-checklist) and the pricing-specific review in [Pricing Playbook Checklist](/docs/pricing/pricing-playbook-checklist).

## Case Study Type

Not applicable — this is a self-review checklist, not a case study.

## Purpose

The Sales Operations Checklist covers whether the operational habits around a call were followed — records, stage, follow-up. This checklist covers something different: whether the conversation itself was read and responded to well, using the four-layer framework from [Reading a Sales Conversation](/docs/sales-coaching/reading-a-sales-conversation). It's meant to be run immediately after a call, while the details are still fresh.

## Learning Objectives

- Apply the four-layer reading framework to one's own call, not just to case studies or someone else's call
- Identify the specific moment where the conversation's direction shifted, for better or worse
- Distinguish honest self-assessment from either excessive self-criticism or reflexive self-approval
- Build the habit of brief, consistent self-review after every call, not just difficult ones

## Conversation Walkthrough

Run through this immediately after any call:

- [ ] What was the candidate's underlying signal at the moment the conversation shifted, and did I respond to that signal or just to their literal words?
- [ ] Was there a moment I filled a gap with a guess — a price, a policy, a processing detail — instead of saying honestly that I'd confirm it?
- [ ] Did I diagnose which specific version of an objection or hesitation was actually present, or did I respond with a generic answer?
- [ ] Did urgency — mine or the candidate's — cause me to move faster than the candidate's actual readiness supported?
- [ ] Is there anything from this call that needs to go in the candidate's record now, before I move to the next thing?

## Decision Analysis

The value of this checklist comes from running it consistently, immediately after ordinary calls, not just after ones that felt notably good or bad. A pattern only becomes visible across several honest self-reviews, not from a single one.

## What Could Improve

Not applicable in the case-study sense — the most common way this checklist is misapplied is skipping it after calls that felt routine, which are often exactly the calls where a recurring pattern would otherwise go unnoticed.

## Coaching Notes

Be honest rather than either harshly self-critical or reflexively self-approving when running this checklist — both extremes make the pattern harder to see clearly. If the same answer keeps showing up across multiple calls, that's the signal worth bringing to a coach or manager directly.

## Key Takeaways

Running a brief, honest self-review immediately after every call — not just the difficult ones — surfaces patterns that a single review can't. Focus on the signal-versus-response question at the heart of the four-layer framework, and bring any recurring pattern to a coach directly rather than working around it alone.
